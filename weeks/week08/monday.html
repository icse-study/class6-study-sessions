➗ Maths — Simple Equations

What an equation is

An equation is a statement that two expressions are equal — it always has an = sign. Solving it means finding the value of the variable that makes both sides equal.

2x + 3 = 7 is an equation. 2x + 3 on its own is only an expression — there is nothing to solve.

The golden rule — keep the balance

An equation is a weighing balance. Whatever you do to one side you must do to the other:

Add or subtract the same number on both sides · multiply or divide both sides by the same non-zero number.

Transposition — the shortcut

Moving a term across the = sign changes its sign: + becomes −, × becomes ÷.

x + 5 = 12 → x = 12 − 5 = 7
3y = 15 → y = 15 ÷ 3 = 5
2a + 3 = 11 → 2a = 11 − 3 = 8 → a = 4
💡 Two-step rule: undo the + or − first, then undo the × or ÷.

Always check your answer

Put your answer back into the original equation. If the two sides are not equal, the answer is wrong — and you have found it out before the examiner did.

a = 4 in 2a + 3: 2(4) + 3 = 8 + 3 = 11 ✓

Turning words into equations

“The sum of a number and 12 is 25” → x + 12 = 25. “Twice a number is 18” → 2x = 18. “Five less than a number is 12” → x − 5 = 12.

💡 “Five less than a number” is x − 5, never 5 − x. Read the order carefully.
🗣️ ಕನ್ನಡ — ಕಿತ್ತೂರು ರಾಣಿ ಚೆನ್ನಮ್ಮ

ಪಾಠದ ಸಾರಾಂಶ · Summary

ಕಿತ್ತೂರಿನ ರಾಣಿ ಚೆನ್ನಮ್ಮ was the brave queen of Kittur who fought the British East India Company in 1824. She was the wife of Raja Mallasarja. After her husband and son died, the British refused to accept her adopted son as heir and tried to annex Kittur under the Doctrine of Lapse. She refused, and led a rebellion. She won the first battle, but was defeated in the second, taken prisoner and died in captivity at Bailhongal.

She is remembered as one of the earliest Indian rulers to fight British rule — more than thirty years before 1857.

📖 English — The Lost Child (Mulk Raj Anand)

The story in short

A little boy goes to a village fair with his parents. Everything tempts him — toys, sweets, a garland, a balloon-seller, a snake-charmer, the roundabout. Each time he asks, his parents move on without buying. At the roundabout he turns to ask again — and finds his parents gone. He is lost in the throng. A kind man picks him up and offers him every single thing he had wanted. The child refuses them all, sobbing only, “I want my mother, I want my father.”

The story ends there, deliberately. We never learn whether he found them — the point is the change inside the child.

Themes worth quoting in an answer

Childhood innocence — his wants are small and immediate.
Love over material things — the very toys he begged for become worthless the moment he loses his parents.
Emotional security — being lost is frightening not because of danger but because of separation.

💡 The turning point: everything he wanted is now offered free, and he says no. That refusal is the moral of the story.

Vocabulary

Fascinated — strongly attracted, unable to look away.
Tempted — drawn towards wanting something.
Throng — a large, packed crowd.
Console — to comfort someone in distress.
Reckon — to think or consider.

✍️ Why does the child refuse the sweets, the balloon and the ride at the end? (3–4 sentences)
Model answer →
At the fair the child wanted the sweets, the garland and the roundabout ride very badly, but his parents kept refusing him. The moment he was lost, all those wishes disappeared. When the kind man offered him each of those very things, he refused every one of them and cried only for his mother and father. This shows that the love and security of his parents mattered far more to him than any material possession.
