📁 Case file 1 — The wilting pot

Story

Asha's potted plant wilts every sunny afternoon but recovers by evening, though the soil is moist.

💡 Reasoning: in hot sun transpiration is faster than roots can absorb water. By evening transpiration slows and the leaves recover. Fix: shade at noon.
📁 Case file 2 — The yellow lawn

Story

A plank lay on the lawn for a week. Underneath, the grass turned pale yellow.

💡 Reasoning: without light, no new chlorophyll is made and no food by photosynthesis.
📁 Case file 3 — The tea garden

Story

Tea pickers pick only the top "two leaves and a bud".

💡 Reasoning: removing the apical bud makes the axillary buds grow into more side shoots — more leaves to pick.
📁 Case file 4 — The dusty roadside

Story

Plants beside a busy dusty road grow poorly.

💡 Reasoning: dust coats the leaves and blocks light and stomata, so less photosynthesis happens.
📁 Case file 5 — The window plant

Story

A money plant by a window bends towards the glass.

💡 Reasoning: the shoot grows towards light so its leaves get more sunlight for food-making.
🎲 Fun Corner — quick cases
1. Leaves of a plant are covered in tiny white insects sucking sap. Effect?
👀 Show answer
Less food reaches the plant; leaves yellow and curl.
2. A gardener trims the top of a hedge. What happens?
👀 Show answer
Side shoots grow — the hedge becomes bushier.
3. Why do cut flowers last longer in a cool room?
👀 Show answer
Less transpiration, so they lose water slowly.
🎯 Fun Trivia
• 🍵 The "two leaves and a bud" rule has been used in Assam and Darjeeling tea gardens for over a century.
• 🌻 Young sunflower heads turn to follow the Sun across the sky during the day.
• 🌿 Plants in city parks remove dust and pollutants from the air — one reason cities plant roadside trees.
