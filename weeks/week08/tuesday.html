🌡 1. Measurement of Temperature

What temperature is

Temperature is a measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of an object. Heat always flows from a body at a higher temperature to a body at a lower temperature.

💡 Heat and temperature are not the same. Heat is the energy that flows; temperature tells you which way it will flow.

Units of temperature

S.I. unit: kelvin (K).
Common units: degree Celsius (°C) and degree Fahrenheit (°F).

Thermometers

Laboratory thermometer — used in the lab to measure the temperature of substances. Marked from −10 °C to 110 °C.
Clinical thermometer — used by doctors for body temperature. Range 35 °C to 42 °C (95 °F to 110 °F). It has a constriction (kink) in the tube that stops the mercury from running back on its own, so the reading can be taken after removing it from the mouth.

The kink is the only reason a doctor can read the thermometer after taking it out. A lab thermometer has no kink, so it must be read while still in the liquid.

📐 2. Measurement of Area

What area is

The total surface occupied by an object is called its area.

S.I. unit: square metre (m²).
Other units: square centimetre (cm²), square kilometre (km²), are and hectare.

1 hectare = 100 are = 10 000 m² · 1 m² = 10 000 cm² (because 1 m = 100 cm, and 100 × 100 = 10 000).
ShapeFormulaExample
Squareside × side = l²l = 6 cm → 36 cm²
Rectanglelength × breadth = l × b8 × 5 cm → 40 cm²
Triangle½ × base × height = ½ l h½ × 12 × 7 → 42 cm²
Circleπ × radius² = π r²r = 7 cm → 154 cm²

Irregular shapes — the graph paper method

Place the object (a leaf, your palm) on graph paper, trace its boundary, then count:

1. Every complete square → count as 1.
2. Every square that is half or more than half covered → count as 1.
3. Squares less than half covered → ignore.

💡 The answer is only approximate — smaller squares on the graph paper give a more accurate area.

✍️ Short answer — answer on paper, then check
1. Define temperature in your own words.
▸ Show model answer
Temperature is the measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body. It decides the direction in which heat flows — heat always flows from a body at a higher temperature to one at a lower temperature. Its S.I. unit is the kelvin (K).
2. Why can't a clinical thermometer be used to measure the temperature of boiling water?
▸ Show model answer
A clinical thermometer is marked only from 35 °C to 42 °C, because body temperature stays in that small range. Boiling water is at 100 °C, far above the highest mark. The mercury would expand beyond the scale and the thermometer would burst, so it cannot be used.
3. What do you understand by the term 'area of a surface'?
▸ Show model answer
The area of a surface is the total surface occupied by an object — how much flat space it covers. It is measured in square units, and its S.I. unit is the square metre (m²).
4. Name two units of area larger than a square metre (m²).
▸ Show model answer
The hectare (1 hectare = 10 000 m²) and the square kilometre (1 km² = 106 m²). The are (1 are = 100 m²) is also larger than a square metre.
5. State the freezing point and boiling point of water on the Celsius scale.
▸ Show model answer
Freezing point (ice point) = 0 °C. Boiling point (steam point) = 100 °C. On the Fahrenheit scale these are 32 °F and 212 °F.
🔴 Stretch questions
6. A rectangular field is 250 m long and 200 m wide. Find its area in m², and then in hectares.
▸ Show model answer
Area = l × b = 250 × 200 = 50 000 m². Since 1 hectare = 10 000 m², area = 50 000 ÷ 10 000 = 5 hectares.
7. Explain the use of the constriction (kink) in a clinical thermometer.
▸ Show model answer
The constriction is a narrow bend just above the bulb. When the thermometer is taken out of the patient's mouth the mercury cools and tries to run back into the bulb, but the kink stops it. The mercury thread stays where it is, so the doctor can read the correct body temperature after removing the thermometer. It is shaken down by hand to reset it.
8. A leaf is traced on graph paper with 1 cm squares. It covers 32 complete squares, 11 squares that are half or more than half, and 9 squares that are less than half. Find its approximate area, and explain why the answer is only approximate.
▸ Show model answer
Count complete squares + half-or-more squares, ignore the rest: 32 + 11 = 43 cm². It is approximate because the leaf's curved edge never lines up exactly with the squares — counting a part-square as a whole one (or as nothing) always leaves a small error. Using graph paper with smaller squares makes the answer more accurate.

Algebraic Expressions · Loops
Two short slots either side of Physics. In Maths, only like terms can be combined. In Python, count the iterations carefully — range() stops one short of its second number.

➗ Terms, like terms and coefficients

An algebraic expression combines variables, constants and operations, e.g. 3x + 2y − 5. Its terms are the parts separated by + or − signs: 3x, 2y and −5. In the term 5x, the number 5 is the coefficient.

Like terms have exactly the same variable: 4x and −2x are like; 4x and 3y are not. You can only add or subtract like terms. 3x + 4y cannot be simplified any further.

➗ The minus-sign trap

When you subtract a bracket, the minus sign changes the sign of every term inside it.

(2a + 3b) − (a − 2b) = 2a + 3b − a + 2b = a + 5b. Notice −(−2b) became +2b — this is where most marks are lost.

➗ Distributive property & finding the value

Multiply the outside number by every term inside: 4(2x − 3y) = 8x − 12y. To find the value of an expression, substitute the given numbers and simplify: 3x − 2 when x = 5 gives 15 − 2 = 13.

💻 while and for

A while loop repeats as long as its condition stays True — and it checks the condition before each pass. A for loop steps through a sequence, so it suits a known number of repetitions.

range(5) → 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 (starts at 0, stops before 5). range(3, 6) → 3, 4, 5. range(2, 10, 2) → 2, 4, 6, 8. A negative step counts down: range(10, 0, -2) → 10, 8, 6, 4, 2.

💻 Two things to watch

An infinite loop happens when the condition can never become False — while True: with nothing to stop it. Inside a while loop you must always change the variable being tested, or it will never end.

continue skips the rest of the current pass and jumps to the next one; break leaves the loop altogether.
