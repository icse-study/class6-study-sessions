➗ Maths — Mean, Median & Mode
MeasureHow to find it
Mean (average)Sum of all observations ÷ how many there are
MedianArrange in order, then take the middle value. If there is an even count, average the two middle ones.
ModeThe value that appears most often

Two traps

Always sort the data first before finding the median — forgetting to is the commonest error. And the mean is the measure most distorted by one extreme value, because every number feeds into the total.

Working backwards: if the mean of 6, 8, x, 12, 14 is 10, then the total must be 50. Since 6+8+12+14 = 40, x = 10.

Bar graphs

A bar graph uses bars of equal width to compare quantities across categories. The height of each bar shows its value.

🏛️ History — The Mauryan Empire

The rulers

Chandragupta Maurya founded the empire in the 4th century BCE (around 322 BCE), advised by Chanakya — also known as Kautilya or Vishnugupta. The capital was Pataliputra, modern Patna.

Ashoka and Dhamma

Ashoka (273–232 BCE) was the greatest Mauryan ruler. After the terrible bloodshed of the Kalinga War he gave up warfare, embraced Buddhism, and followed Dhamma — a set of moral principles built on non-violence, truth, respect for elders and tolerance of all faiths.

He spread his message by carving edicts on rocks and pillars across the empire — which is how we know his words directly today.
🏛️ Civics — Urban Local Self-Government

Who runs a city

A large city is run by a Municipal Corporation; a smaller town by a Municipality. The city is divided into wards for elections, and each ward elects a Councillor. The Mayor is the elected head.

Their work: water, sanitation, roads, street lights, schools and hospitals. Their money comes from local taxes AND government grants together.
⚡ Physics — Light

The essentials

Light is a form of energy that lets us see. Luminous objects make their own light (the Sun, a star, a bulb, a firefly); non-luminous objects only reflect it (the Moon, a table, a mirror).

Laws of reflection: (1) the incident ray, the reflected ray and the normal all lie in the same plane; (2) the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection. The normal is the perpendicular line at the point where the ray strikes.

Shadows, cameras and eclipses

A shadow forms when an opaque object blocks light. A pinhole camera forms a real, inverted image, working on the rectilinear propagation of light — the fact that light travels in straight lines.

Solar eclipse: the Moon comes between the Sun and Earth. Lunar eclipse: the Earth's shadow falls on the Moon. Smooth surfaces give regular reflection; rough ones give diffuse reflection.
