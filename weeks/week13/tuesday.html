Circles — Chord, Secant & Tangent
Chapter 17 · Sections 17.4–17.5. Lines that cut a circle, lines that touch it, and the one chord that beats all the others.
➗ The ideas

Chord

A chord is a straight line joining any two points on the circumference.

A chord does not have to pass through the centre — but if it does, it is also a diameter.

1. A chord through the centre is a diameter.
2. The diameter is the longest chord of a circle.
💡 Every diameter is a chord, but most chords are not diameters.

Secant

A straight line that cuts the circle at two distinct points is a secant.

A secant is a full line that keeps going on both sides; the piece of it inside the circle is a chord.

Line AB meets the circle at M and N → AB is a secant, MN is a chord.

Tangent

A straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point is a tangent. That single meeting point is the point of contact.

A line at a distance exactly equal to the radius from the centre must be a tangent.
💡 From the centre: distance < radius → the line cuts (secant) · distance = radius → it touches (tangent) · distance > radius → it misses the circle.
🎉 Fun facts & trivia

The word tangent comes from the Latin tangere, 'to touch' — the same root as 'tangible'. A secant comes from secare, 'to cut', which also gives us 'scissors' and 'section'.

A tyre touching the road is a tangent in real life: at any instant the wheel meets the ground at essentially one point.

Longest chord challenge: slide a chord of a circle towards the centre and it grows; the moment it passes through the centre it is a diameter and can grow no more.

📜 Part 2 of 12 — Drawing Straight Lines With a Circle

Ancient builders had no laser levels. To mark a perfect right angle they used a rope with 12 equally spaced knots, forming a 3–4–5 triangle. But to mark a perfect circle they needed only a peg, a rope and a stick — one fixed point, one fixed distance.

That is why circles appear in the oldest surviving architecture, from stone circles to temple plans. The compass used in your geometry box is the same idea in metal: the sharp point is the centre and the pencil traces the circumference.
