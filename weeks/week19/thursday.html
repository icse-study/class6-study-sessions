⚗️ Chemistry · Week 19 · Ch 4 Elements, Compounds, Symbols and Formulae (Part 1)
Elements — metals, non-metals, metalloids & noble gases
Matter can be sorted by its chemical composition into pure substances and mixtures. This week: what makes a substance pure, the four families of elements and their exceptions, how symbols are written, the first 20 elements and the Periodic Table.

🧭 Classifying matter

Matter → Pure substances (always homogeneous: elements, compounds) and Impure substances = mixtures (homogeneous or heterogeneous).
Pure substance: definite composition and consistent properties; one kind of particle; cannot be broken into simpler substances by physical means; fixed melting and boiling points; characteristic colour, smell and taste.

🧱 Elements

An element is a pure substance made of only one kind of atom; it cannot be broken into anything simpler by any physical or chemical process.
• 118 known: 92 natural, 26 artificial
• Four families: metals, non-metals, metalloids, noble (inert) gases

🔩 Metals (about 93)

Shiny, hard (except sodium — soft) · solid (except mercury — liquid) · high melting and boiling points · ductile (drawn into wires) · malleable (beaten into sheets; zinc is brittle) · good conductors (tungsten is poor) · sonorous · react with water, oxygen and acids

💡 Learn the exceptions — exams love them.

🪨 Non-metals (18), metalloids (7), noble gases (6)

• Non-metals: dull (iodine and carbon as diamond/graphite shine), soft solids (S, P, I) or gases (O, N, H, Cl), bromine the only liquid; low MP/BP except diamond & graphite; brittle; poor conductors (graphite conducts); not sonorous. Diamond is the hardest natural substance.
• Metalloids: some metal + some non-metal properties; all solids — boron, silicon, arsenic, antimony, germanium, tellurium, polonium
• Noble gases: do not react; in air in traces — helium, neon, argon, krypton, xenon, radon

🔤 Symbols

A symbol is a short form of an element's name and also stands for one atom of it. Berzelius first used letters.
• First letter capital, second small: C carbon, Ca calcium, Co cobalt, Cl chlorine
• From Latin: Na (Natrium, sodium), K (Kalium, potassium), Fe (Ferrum, iron), Cu (Cuprum, copper)
• First 20: H He Li Be B C N O F Ne Na Mg Al Si P S Cl Ar K Ca

💡 Co is cobalt; CO is carbon monoxide. Capitals matter!

🗂️ Periodic Table & common elements

Rows = periods; columns = groups (now numbered 1–18 by IUPAC: IA = 1, IIA = 2, IIIA = 13, VIIA = 17, zero = 18).
Most common: universe — H, He · earth's crust — O, then Si, Al, Fe · atmosphere — N · human body — C, H, O

✨ Fun facts
• Hydrogen was the first element formed after the Big Bang, then helium. Heavier elements were cooked inside stars that later exploded — you are made of star dust!
• Gallium, a metal, melts in your hand (about 30 °C).
• Sodium is so reactive it is stored under kerosene to keep air and water away.
• The newest elements (like oganesson, 118) exist only for a fraction of a second in labs.

🎯 Must-remember
• 118 elements: 92 natural, 26 artificial
• Metal exceptions: Na soft, Hg liquid, Zn brittle, W poor conductor
• Non-metal exceptions: iodine & diamond/graphite shine, Br liquid, graphite conducts
• 7 metalloids, 6 noble gases
• Latin symbols: Na, K, Fe, Cu
• Periods = rows, groups = columns
