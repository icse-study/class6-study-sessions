➗ Maths — Integers

What they are

Integers are the whole numbers together with their negatives: … −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3 … Zero is neither positive nor negative. The absolute value |−5| = 5 is simply the distance from zero, so it is never negative.

Adding & subtracting

Same signs → add and keep the sign (−5 + −3 = −8). Different signs → subtract and keep the sign of the bigger absolute value (−5 + 3 = −2).

To subtract, use Keep – Change – Change: 7 − (−3) becomes 7 + 3 = 10. Two minuses next to each other make a plus.

Multiplying & dividing

Same signs → positive. (−6) × (−4) = 24. Different signs → negative. (−4) × 7 = −28.

With three or more numbers, count the minus signs: an even count gives a positive answer, an odd count gives a negative one.

Derived units

A derived unit is built from fundamental ones: area m² · volume m³ · speed m/s · density kg/m³ · force newton (N).

Density = Mass ÷ Volume. A body of mass 50 g and volume 10 cm³ has a density of 5 g/cm³. Speed = Distance ÷ Time.

Measuring instruments

Length: ruler, measuring tape, vernier calliper, screw gauge. Mass: beam balance, electronic balance. Time: clock, stopwatch.

Measurement is comparing an unknown quantity against a known standard unit — not guessing or estimating.

Traps to watch

input() always returns a string, so wrap it in int() or float() before doing arithmetic. And "5" + "3" gives "53", not 8 — that is string joining, not addition.

A valid variable name may use letters, digits and underscores but must not start with a digit and must contain no spaces or hyphens: my_var ✔   2var, my-var, my var ✘. Plain / always gives a float: 5 / 2 = 2.5.
