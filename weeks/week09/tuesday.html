Integers — Addition & Subtraction on the Number Line
Chapter 4 · Section 4.5. Every sum here is a walk along the number line: which way do you step, and how far?
➗ Adding on the number line

Which way do I move?

Start at the first number, then:

Adding a positive number → move right
Adding a negative number → move left

Example: (+3) + (−4) → start at +3, move 4 steps left → −1

The shortcut rules

Same signs: add the numbers and keep the common sign.

(−5) + (−8) = −13  ·  (+5) + (+8) = +13

Different signs: subtract the smaller from the larger and keep the sign of the larger number.

(+7) + (−10) = −3  (10 − 7 = 3, and 10 is negative)
(−4) + (+6) = +2  (6 − 4 = 2, and 6 is positive)
➗ Subtracting on the number line

Add the opposite

Subtracting a positive → move left.
Subtracting a negative → move right, because taking away a negative is the same as adding a positive.

(+8) − (−5) = 8 + 5 = +13
(−8) − (−5) = −8 + 5 = −3
(−7) − (+12) = −7 − 12 = −19
💡 The single rule that covers every subtraction: change the sign of the number being subtracted, then add.

Reading the words correctly

"5 more than −3" → −3 + 5 = 2
"7 less than −2" → −2 − 7 = −9
"Subtract −4 from −9" → −9 − (−4) = −9 + 4 = −5

💡 "Subtract a from b" always means b − a, not a − b. Read the sentence twice before you write the sum.
Before the quiz
Rewrite every subtraction as an addition first — (−8) − (−5) becomes (−8) + 5. Once there are no minus-signs-in-front-of-brackets left, the sign rules do the rest.
