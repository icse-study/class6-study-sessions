Framing Algebraic Expressions from Words
Chapter 13 · Turning English into algebra. Get the order right — "5 less than x" is x − 5, never 5 − x.
➗ The four operations in words

Words that mean add or subtract

Sum, total, increased by, more than → +.   Difference, less than, decreased by, reduced by → −.

x increased by 6 → x + 6  ·  6 less than x → x − 6  ·  the sum of a and 2b → a + 2b
💡 "5 less than x" reverses the order you hear it in: it is x − 5. "5 less x" would be 5 − x. Read subtraction phrases twice.

Words that mean multiply or divide

Product, times, twice, thrice, of → ×.   Quotient, divided by, shared equally, per → ÷.

twice x → 2x  ·  one-third of y → y/3  ·  the product of p and q → pq  ·  z divided by 4 → z/4
➗ Building longer phrases

Two steps need brackets

When a whole phrase is multiplied, wrap it in brackets before multiplying.

3 times the sum of x and 5 → 3(x + 5)  ·  3 times x, plus 5 → 3x + 5
💡 The comma in English does the job brackets do in algebra. 3(x + 5) = 3x + 15, which is not the same as 3x + 5.

Consecutive and related numbers

If a number is x, the next whole number is x + 1, and the one before it is x − 1.

Three consecutive numbers → x, x + 1, x + 2  ·  an even number → 2x  ·  an odd number → 2x + 1
➗ Real-life statements

Age, money and measurement

Name the unknown first ("let Riya's age be x years"), then build every other quantity from it.

Riya is x years old; her father is 5 times as old → 5x. In 4 years Riya will be x + 4.
💡 Every statement must use the same letter for the same unknown. Introducing a second letter when one will do is the usual mistake.
📜 The Story of Algebra · Part 11 of 16 — Algebra as Translation

The hardest step was writing it down

For nearly 4000 years the hard part of algebra was not solving — it was stating. A Babylonian scribe describing a square and its side needed a paragraph. You can now write it in five characters.

“a number, added to its square, gives six”  →  x² + x = 6
💡 Framing an expression from a sentence is exactly the skill that took civilisations millennia to develop. Take it slowly.

Translate word by word

English phrases map onto operations in a fixed way. More than and increased by mean add; less than reverses the order; of usually means multiply; per means divide.

7 less than y → y − 7 (not 7 − y)  ·  5 times the sum of x and 3 → 5(x + 3)

Descartes: an equation is also a picture

Once a sentence becomes an equation, Descartes' insight kicks in: that equation also describes a shape. y = 2x + 3 is a straight line; a quadratic is a curve. Words → symbols → geometry → physics.

That chain is why algebra ended up running engineering, animation and every computer game you have played.
⚗️ Chemistry — Matter: Effect of Heat (Chapter 3, Part 2)

🔥 Heat does exactly three things to matter

Every question in this chapter is really about one of these three effects.

1. Change of state (interconversion)
2. Expansion
3. Chemical change — new substances are formed

🔄 The five changes of state

Learn these with their temperatures for water — they are asked constantly.

Melting (fusion) — solid → liquid on heating · melting point of ice = 0 °C
Freezing (solidification) — liquid → solid on cooling · freezing point of water = 0 °C
Vaporisation (boiling) — liquid → gas on heating · boiling point of water = 100 °C
Condensation (liquefaction) — gas → liquid on cooling · condensation point of steam = 100 °C
Sublimation — solid → gas directly, with no liquid stage
💡 Sublimation examples worth memorising: camphor, iodine, naphthalene, dry ice and ammonium chloride.

🌍 The water cycle is this whole chapter, outdoors

The sun evaporates water from seas and rivers. The vapour rises, cools and condenses into tiny droplets that form clouds, which fall as rain and run back to the sea.

Evaporation → condensation → precipitation → collection, repeating forever.
💡 Note the exam wording: water vapour first condenses into clouds (tiny droplets), and only then falls as rain.

📈 Expansion, and the change that cannot be undone

Solids, liquids and gases all expand on heating and contract on cooling. The ball-and-ring experiment proves it for solids: the ball passes through the ring when cold, but not after heating.

A thermometer works on exactly this principle — the liquid inside expands as the temperature rises.
💡 A chemical change is permanent and irreversible — burning paper gives ash and gases you can never turn back into paper. A burning candle does both at once: the wax melts (physical) and the wick burns (chemical).
🎯 Fun Trivia
Ever wondered why naphthalene balls in a cupboard slowly shrink and vanish, leaving no wet patch behind? They sublime — they turn straight from solid into vapour without ever becoming a liquid. That escaping vapour is exactly what keeps the insects away.

The nationality rule

Add e for the feminine. If it ends in -en, double the n first: -enne. If it already ends in e, no change.

français → française · indien → indienne · belge → belge · russe → russe
💡 A mixed group of boys and girls uses the masculine plural: Anirudh et Neha sont indiens.

en, au, aux — living in a country

en + feminine country: en France, en Inde, en Espagne
au + masculine country: au Canada, au Japon, au Danemark
aux + plural country: aux États-Unis

♀️ Making an adjective feminine

Five rules

1. Usually add e: grand → grande, haut → haute, court → courte
2. Already ends in e → no change: jeune, rouge, facile, difficile
3. Some double the last consonant + e: gros → grosse, bas → basse, bon → bonne, ancien → ancienne, gentil → gentille
4. -eau → -elle: beau → belle, nouveau → nouvelle
5. Irregular — learn by heart (below)

bel, nouvel, vieil

Before a masculine singular noun starting with a vowel or silent h, beau / nouveau / vieux become bel / nouvel / vieil.

un bel étudiant · un nouvel ami · un vieil homme
👥 Making an adjective plural

Four rules

1. Usually add s: grand → grands, joli → jolis, noir → noirs
2. Already ends in s or x → no change: gros, épais, gris, heureux
3. -eau → -eaux: beau → beaux, nouveau → nouveaux
4. Feminine plural = feminine singular + s: grande → grandes, belle → belles

🎯 Fun Trivia
French divides rivers into two kinds. A fleuve flows straight into the sea or ocean — the Seine flows into the English Channel. A rivière flows into another river — the Marne flows into the Seine just outside Paris.
