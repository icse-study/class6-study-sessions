🌍 Geography · Ch 4 Agriculture · Part 4
Crops of the World & Cropping Seasons
Food crops, cash crops, fibre, horticulture and beverage crops — where are they grown? Plus India's kharif, rabi and zaid seasons and traditional vs modern farming.

🍚 Food crops

Grown for human consumption: rice, wheat, millets, oats, barley, corn (maize) and pulses. Cereals are grains (rice, wheat, maize) that form the staple diet. Pulses (moong, masoor, arhar) are legumes — rich in protein and they fix nitrogen in the soil.

💡 Legumes = plants with pods (peas, beans, pulses).

📏 Extensive vs intensive cereals

Wheat on the vast semi-arid grasslands is extensive farming; rice in the small fragmented fields of monsoon Asia is intensive farming.

💰 Cash crops

Grown only to sell. They earn money for farmers and foreign exchange for the country, and supply raw materials to industries. In temperate lands even wheat, corn and oats can be cash crops.
• Fibre: jute, cotton, flax, hemp
• Horticultural: flowers (roses, tulips), fruits, vegetables
• Plantation: tea, coffee, rubber, spices, sugar cane, cocoa

🎋 Purpose decides the class

Sugar cane is a cash crop in Australia but a food crop in India. Classification depends on the purpose of cultivation.

🗺️ Distribution of major crops

Tea: Nilgiri Hills (Tamil Nadu), Darjiling (West Bengal), Kandy Plateau (Sri Lanka).
Coffee: Brazilian, Mexican and Colombian Highlands.
Rice: lowlands of China, India, Bangladesh, Indonesia, Japan.
Wheat: rolling grasslands of USA, Canada, Argentina, Ukraine.

📅 India's cropping seasons

Kharif (summer): sown June–July, harvested Oct–Nov — rice, sugar cane, cotton, jute.
Rabi (winter): sown Oct–Nov, harvested March — wheat, mustard, gram.
Zaid: short summer season between rabi harvest and kharif sowing — seasonal fruits and vegetables for extra income.

⚖️ Traditional vs modern farming

Traditional: more manual labour (more jobs); depends on monsoon so fewer crops a year; natural manure and pest control (less harm); lower yield.
Modern: machines (less labour); irrigation allows dry-season crops; chemical fertilisers and pesticides (pollution); large yield.

🎉 Harvest festivals

Farmers celebrate a good harvest — Lohri in Punjab, Pongal in Tamil Nadu, and (closer to home) Makara Sankranti in Karnataka.

🎯 Must-remember facts
• Food: rice, wheat, millets, oats, barley, maize, pulses · Pulses = legumes = protein + nitrogen
• Fibre: jute, cotton, flax, hemp · Horticulture: flowers, fruit, veg · Plantation: tea, coffee, rubber, spices, sugar cane, cocoa
• Tea: Nilgiris, Darjiling, Kandy · Coffee: Brazil, Mexico, Colombia highlands
• Kharif Jun–Jul → Oct–Nov · Rabi Oct–Nov → Mar · Zaid in between

✍️ English Grammar · Ch 4 Degrees of Comparison
Degrees of Comparison — positive, comparative, superlative
Adjectives can compare. Tall (positive), taller (comparative, 2 things), tallest (superlative, 3 or more). The big exam skill: changing the degree without changing the meaning.

📈 Three ways to form degrees

• Short words add -er / -est: clean, cleaner, cleanest · cheap, cheaper, cheapest · dirty, dirtier, dirtiest (y → i) · big, bigger (double the last letter)
• Long words use more / most: difficult, more difficult, most difficult
• Irregular: good–better–best · bad–worse–worst · little–less–least · many/much–more–most · far–farther/further–farthest/furthest

💡 Never double up: ✗ more better, ✗ most cheapest.

🔁 Interchange 1 — comparative ↔ positive

C: Iron is more useful than copper.
P: Copper is not as useful as iron.

P: He is as wise as Solomon.
C: Solomon is not wiser than he.

💡 Swap the two nouns and add/remove NOT.

🔁 Interchange 2 — the 'only one' pattern

S: Delhi is the largest city in India.
C: Delhi is larger than any other city in India.
P: No other city in India is as large as Delhi.

Shakuntala is the best Sanskrit play → better than any other Sanskrit play → No other Sanskrit play is as good as Shakuntala.

🔁 Interchange 3 — the 'one of' pattern

S: Ashoka was one of the greatest Indian kings.
C: Ashoka was greater than most other Indian kings.
P: Very few Indian kings were as great as Ashoka.

💡 one of the … → most other … → very few …

🧑 Pronoun at the end

After than and as, careful English uses the subject pronoun because a verb is hidden: I am taller than he (is). She is as tall as I (am).

She is more energetic than I (am).

⚠️ Common errors

• ✗ the more popular band in the UK → ✓ the most popular (more than two bands)
• ✗ slower as a turtle → ✓ slower than
• ✗ not as smarter as → ✓ not as smart as
• ✗ the most cheapest → ✓ the cheapest
• ✗ more better → ✓ better
• ✗ more healthy → ✓ healthier
• ✗ a cleverest animal → ✓ the cleverest / a clever animal

✨ Fun facts
• Good, better, best come from two different old words. 'Better' and 'best' are related to an Old English word bōt (remedy) — not to 'good' at all!
• Farther is for physical distance; further also means 'more' (further details).
• Shakuntala (Abhijnanashakuntalam) by Kalidasa is often called the greatest Sanskrit play.
• Some adjectives cannot be compared: perfect, unique, complete, square. Something is either unique or not!

🎯 Must-remember rules
• Two things → comparative + than. Three or more → the + superlative
• S: the largest → C: larger than any other → P: No other … as large as
• S: one of the greatest → C: greater than most other → P: Very few … as great as
• Never 'more better' or 'most cheapest'
• as … as (positive); than (comparative)
