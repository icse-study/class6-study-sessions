🧭 Directions

Cardinal and intermediate

The four cardinal directions are North, South, East and West. Between each pair lies an intermediate direction — NE, NW, SE and SW. North is normally at the top of a map.

Clockwise from the top: N → E → S → W. A compass uses a magnetic needle that always comes to rest pointing north.
🗺️ The three components of a map
ComponentWhat it tells you
DirectionWhich way is north — shown by an arrow or a compass rose
ScaleThe ratio between the distance on the map and the actual distance on the ground
SymbolsAgreed conventional signs for features — so a map can be read in any language

Scale in practice

If the scale is 1 cm = 100 km, then 4 cm measured on the map means 400 km on the ground. Scale is what lets a small drawing represent a huge area accurately.

Contour lines

A contour line joins all the places that are at the same height above sea level. Lines drawn close together mean a steep slope; lines far apart mean a gentle slope. Contours never cross — one point cannot be at two heights.

🌐 The grid — latitudes & longitudes

Parallels and meridians

Latitudes run east–west as complete circles that never meet, so they are called parallels. They get shorter towards the poles. Longitudes (meridians) run north–south from pole to pole and are all of equal length.

Adjective or adverb?

Ask what the word is describing. Describing a noun → adjective. Describing a verb → adverb. Most (not all) adverbs end in -ly.

"She runs quickly" — quickly tells how she runs, a verb → adverb.
"The clever fox" — clever tells what kind of fox, a noun → adjective.
🔗 Subject–verb agreement

The rules that get tested

A singular subject takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb. Two traps:

1. When two singular subjects are joined by and but each is preceded by every / each / no, the verb stays singular.
2. Words between the subject and the verb do not change it.

"Every boy and girl was present today." — every makes it singular, so was, not were.
"The box of chocolates is on the table." — the subject is box, not chocolates.

Finding the subject

Ask "who or what is doing the verb?" The subject includes the words describing it.

"The bright sun shone over the hills." → who shone? The bright sun.

Direct → indirect speech

Remove the quotation marks, add that, shift the tense one step back, and change the pronouns to match the speaker.

He said, "I am happy." → He said that he was happy.
am → was, and I → he.

✉️ Writing formats

The formal letter

A formal letter is written to someone you do not know personally — a principal, an editor, an official — to make a request, a complaint or an application. Its purpose is to communicate officially and politely, so the language stays respectful and no slang or contractions are used.

Order: sender's address → date → receiver's address → subject → salutation (Dear Sir/Madam) → body → complimentary close (Yours faithfully) → signature.
💡 An informal letter, by contrast, goes to family or friends and can be chatty — no subject line needed.
