⚗️ Chemistry · Week 17 · Ch 3 Matter (Part 1)
Matter — what it is made of and why solids, liquids and gases behave differently
Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. This week: atoms and molecules, the three things that decide a state (force of attraction, motion, gaps), the properties of solids, liquids and gases, diffusion and Brownian motion.

📦 Matter, mass & volume

• Matter: has mass and occupies space; we sense it and it resists us (swimming against a current).
• Mass = amount of matter · Volume = space it occupies
• Natural non-living: wood, coal, silk, cotton, stone, water, air, sand · Man-made: plastic, soap, glass, nylon, steel, cement
• The word material comes from matter.

⚛️ Atom vs molecule

• Atom: smallest unit of matter showing its properties; usually cannot exist independently.
• Molecule: smallest unit that shows all properties of that matter and can exist independently. O2 = 2 oxygen atoms; H2O = 2 H + 1 O.
• Ideas of atoms came from Maharishi Kanad (India) and later John Dalton.

💡 Atoms are smaller than molecules — molecules are built from atoms.

🧲 Three keys to every state

1. Intermolecular force of attraction — strongest in solids, weakest in gases
2. Random motion (kinetic energy) — rises with temperature
3. Intermolecular space — tiny in solids, large in gases
Sugar in water: the level doesn't rise because sugar fills the gaps between water molecules.

💡 More force → particles closer → less space → more solid-like.

🧊 Solid · 💧 Liquid · 💨 Gas

• Solid: definite shape & volume, rigid, almost incompressible, any number of free surfaces, high density, does not diffuse
• Liquid: definite volume, no definite shape, flows from higher to lower level, one free surface, diffuses slowly
• Gas: no definite shape or volume, highly compressible, flows in all directions, exerts equal pressure all round, no free surface, very low density, diffuses very fast
Liquids + gases = fluids (they flow).

🌫️ Diffusion & Brownian motion

• Diffusion: intermixing of particles of one kind with another — incense smell spreading, a drop of red ink colouring water. Fast in gases, slower in liquids.
• Miscible liquids mix (water + alcohol); immiscible don't (water + oil).
• Brownian motion: zig-zag movement of particles suspended in a liquid or gas, named after Robert Brown. Increases on heating.
• Cohesive force: attraction between like particles; mercury has a very strong one.

LPG has no smell; a strong-smelling chemical (ethyl mercaptan) is added so leaks can be detected by diffusion.

🥚 Egg-in-a-bottle

A burning matchstick heats air inside a bottle → the air expands and some escapes. As it cools, the air inside contracts → lower pressure → outside air pushes the egg in. Blowing air in raises the pressure and pushes it out.

💡 Gases exert pressure and expand/contract with temperature.
✨ Fun facts
• The air in an empty classroom has a mass of over 200 kg — about three adults!
• Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature; its strong cohesive force makes it form round beads.
• Robert Brown saw pollen grains jiggling in water in 1827. Einstein explained why in 1905 — it proved atoms exist.
• Glass looks solid, but scientists still argue about whether it is a true solid because its particles aren't arranged in a pattern.

🎯 Must-remember
• Matter = mass + occupies space
• Atom: may not exist alone · Molecule: exists independently
• Force: solid > liquid > gas · Gaps: gas > liquid > solid
• Free surfaces: solid many · liquid one · gas none
• Diffusion: gas fastest, liquid slow, solid none
• Liquids + gases = fluids
