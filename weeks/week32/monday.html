📖 English Literature · Week 32
Ch 10 · The Happy Prince — a play
A play adapted from Oscar Wilde’s story. A golden statue who can see his people’s suffering, and a little swallow who becomes his messenger. Themes: compassion, sacrifice and true beauty.
👑 The statue and its legend

The statue of the Happy Prince stands on a tall column, covered in gold, with a gold heart, two blue sapphires for eyes and a ruby in his sword.
Mother tells Daughter the legend: the Prince’s father, the King, was generous and shared his fortune with his people. When the King died suddenly, the sad Prince died soon after. The grateful people collected the gold and jewels the King had given them and made the statue. Daughter: “He looks like an angel.”

🐦 The swallow’s missions

A swallow, on a long trip to Egypt, rests between the statue’s feet. Drops fall from a clear sky — the Prince is crying. When alive he never knew tears; now, high up, he sees his people’s misery.

1. Ruby → a poor woman embroidering a ball dress, whose son lies sick and hungry; she can only give him water. She cries and hugs her son. The swallow feels “so satisfied” — “because you have done a good deed”.
2. First sapphire → a writer, too cold and hungry to finish a play for the Director of the Theatre; he will sell it for food and firewood. The swallow protests — he should be in Egypt by the Nile — but obeys.
3. Second sapphire → a little match-girl with no shoes or coat, whose matches fell in the mud. Now the Prince is blind. “She needs it more than I.”
4. The swallow: “Now that you are blind, I cannot leave you.” The Prince asks him to give away his gold, leaf by leaf — “This is their gold.” The poor rejoice: bread, firewood, medicine — “a miracle!”
5. Exhausted, the swallow dies little by little at the Prince’s feet.

👼 The ending

The Mayor finds the statue bare and calls it “worthless” — it is destroyed and thrown away with the swallow. Only the gold heart and the dead swallow remain together. An Angel, sent by God to find the two most beautiful things on earth, takes them to God.

Themes: compassion · sacrifice · friendship · true beauty is goodness, not gold · the irony of a “Happy” Prince who weeps.

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Oscar Wilde (1854–1900)

Irish playwright and poet, famous for his sharp wit and brilliant conversation.

🌟 Fun facts
• “The Happy Prince” comes from The Happy Prince and Other Tales (1888) — the same collection as “The Selfish Giant” you read in Week 6!
• In Wilde’s original story the Prince has a heart of lead, which snaps in two when the swallow dies; the play changes it to a gold heart.
• In the original, the swallow had stayed behind because he fell in love with a Reed.
• His only novel is The Picture of Dorian Gray; his most famous play is The Importance of Being Earnest.
• Other works: the essay “The Soul of Man under Socialism” and the long poem The Ballad of Reading Gaol.
• He had two sons, Cyril and Vyvyan.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. Why was the Prince known as the Happy Prince, and why does he cry now?
👀 Show model answer
When he was alive and had a human heart, he never worried about anything and did not know what tears were, so people called him the Happy Prince. Now, standing high above the city as a statue, he can see the suffering and misery of his people, and he cries because he cannot move to help them himself.
2. What values does the play teach us?
👀 Show model answer
It teaches compassion — the Prince cannot bear to see others suffer. It teaches sacrifice — he gives away his jewels and gold, and the swallow gives up Egypt and even his life. It teaches friendship and loyalty, and it shows that true beauty lies in goodness, not in gold, which is why the Angel chooses the heart and the swallow.
