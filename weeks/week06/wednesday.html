🗺️ Map Revision Warm-up

The five elements of a map — recite them

Title (what it shows) · Scale (map distance vs real distance) · Direction (the North arrow) · Legend / Key (what the symbols mean) · Grid (latitudes and longitudes for locating places).

🌍 Geography — Maps & Landforms

Working with scale

If a scale reads 1 cm = 10 km, then to find the real distance you multiply the map measurement, and to find the map distance you divide the real one.

5 cm on the map → 5 × 10 = 50 km on the ground. And 250 km on the ground at 1 cm = 50 km → 250 ÷ 50 = 5 cm on the map.

Map or plan?

A plan shows a small area in great detail (a classroom, a house). A map shows a large area with much less detail. Types of map: physical (landforms), political (countries and borders) and thematic (one topic, such as rainfall).

LandformDescriptionExample
MountainHigh and steep, rising sharplyThe Himalayas
PlateauFlat-topped but elevated — a tablelandThe Deccan Plateau
PlainFlat and low-lying, usually very fertileThe Indo-Gangetic plains
ValleyThe low ground between hills or mountainsThe Kashmir Valley

Careful

A river is a water body, not a landform. The four cardinal directions are North, South, East and West; north-east and the rest are the intermediate directions.

✍️ English — The Three Simple Tenses
TenseStructureUsed for
Simple PresentSubject + V1 (+ s/es for he/she/it)Habits & general truths
Simple PastSubject + V2Finished past actions
Simple FutureSubject + will/shall + V1Actions still to come

Let the time markers tell you

every day, always, usually → simple present. yesterday, an hour ago, last week → simple past. tomorrow, next week, soon → simple future.

Irregular verbs to know: go–went · buy–bought · run–ran · write–wrote · eat–ate. After will, always use the plain V1: "She will go", never "will went".
