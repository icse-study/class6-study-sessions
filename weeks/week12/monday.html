Simple Linear Equations — The Balanced Scale
Chapter 14 · An equation is a balance. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other — and the scale stays level.
➗ Expression vs equation

The equals sign is the difference

An expression has no equals sign and cannot be solved (3x + 5). An equation says two things are equal (3x + 5 = 20) and has a solution.

Expression: 2x − 7  ·  Equation: 2x − 7 = 9  ·  Solution: x = 8
💡 You simplify an expression but you solve an equation. If there is no "=", there is nothing to solve.

What "solution" means

The solution (or root) is the value of the letter that makes the left side equal the right side.

Is x = 4 a solution of 3x − 2 = 10? LHS = 3(4) − 2 = 10 = RHS. Yes.
💡 Checking is free marks: substitute your answer back and see whether both sides match.
➗ Solving by balancing

Do the same to both sides

Add, subtract, multiply or divide both sides by the same number, until the letter stands alone.

x + 7 = 12 → subtract 7 from both sides → x = 5
5x = 45 → divide both sides by 5 → x = 9
x/4 = 3 → multiply both sides by 4 → x = 12

Undo in reverse order

Remove the term that is added or subtracted first, then deal with the number multiplying the letter.

3x + 5 = 20 → 3x = 15 (subtract 5) → x = 5 (divide by 3)
💡 Dividing before subtracting is the usual wrong turn: from 3x + 5 = 20 you cannot jump to x + 5 = 20 ÷ 3.
➗ Checking and vocabulary

Every equation deserves a check

Substitute the answer into the original equation, work out both sides separately, and confirm they are equal.

2x − 3 = 11 gives x = 7. Check: LHS = 2(7) − 3 = 11 = RHS. ✔
💡 LHS means Left Hand Side, RHS means Right Hand Side. Use those labels in your answer — ICSE examiners expect them.

📜 The Story of Algebra · Part 13 of 16 — A New Question in the 19th Century

The question changed

For most of history mathematicians asked: how do I solve this equation? By the 19th century they began asking something deeper: what are the underlying rules that make a whole mathematical system work?

That shift created abstract algebra — the study of structure itself, rather than of individual answers.

Groups, rings and fields

Abstract algebra studies sets of things together with the operations allowed on them: groups (the simplest structure), rings, fields, and symmetries — the ways an object can be turned or flipped and still look the same.

Mathematicians such as Évariste Galois, Niels Henrik Abel and Emmy Noether built this new world.
💡 It sounds far from x + 8 = 15 — but it grew directly out of asking why the balancing rules you are using today always work.

The balance that never lies

Solving an equation is keeping a balance level. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other. That single rule is the whole method, and it is the small seed that abstract algebra grew from.

x + 11 = 20 → subtract 11 from both sides → x = 9
