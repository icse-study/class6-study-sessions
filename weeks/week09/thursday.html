Algebra — Introduction
Chapter 12 · Sections 12.1–12.5. The vocabulary of algebra: constants, variables, terms, like and unlike, and the names for expressions.
➗ The building blocks

What algebra is

Algebra is generalised arithmetic — the same rules of number work, but with letters standing in for numbers so one statement can cover every case at once.

Arithmetic: 2 + 3 = 3 + 2  ·  Algebra: a + b = b + a  (true for all numbers)

Constants and variables

Constant: a symbol with a fixed value — 5, −7, ¾.
Variable (literal): a symbol whose value can change — x, y, a, b.

In 3x + 5: 3 is the coefficient, x is the variable, 5 is a constant.

Terms and coefficients

A term is a constant, a variable, or a product of them: 5x, −3, xy, 7x²y.

The coefficient of a part of a term is everything else multiplied with it:

In 7x²y → coefficient of x² is 7y; coefficient of y is 7x²
In −3xy → coefficient of x is −3y; coefficient of xy is −3

Like and unlike terms

Like terms have exactly the same variables raised to exactly the same powers: 3x and −5x; 2xy and 7xy; −2ab and 5ba (order doesn't matter).

Unlike terms differ in variable or in power: 3x and 3y; 2x² and 2x; 4x and 5y².

💡 Only like terms can be added or subtracted into a single term. 2a + 3b has to stay as it is.

Degree, and what counts as a polynomial

The degree of a polynomial in one variable is the highest power of that variable: 5x³ − 2x + 7 has degree 3.

For a single term with several variables, the degree is the sum of the exponents: 3xy²z³ → 1 + 2 + 3 = 6.

💡 An expression is not a polynomial if a variable sits in a denominator or under a root — 5x + 1/x fails the test.
Before the quiz
When a question asks for "the coefficient of x", cover x with your thumb — whatever is left in that term is the answer, sign included.
