🏆 Nobel Prize Week

🧠 Medicine — 5 Oct

Karl Deisseroth (USA) and Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel (Germany) won for optogenetics. They used light-sensitive proteins, first found in tiny green algae, to switch nerve cells on and off with light. It helps scientists understand how the brain works.

💡 Opto = light. Optogenetics = controlling cells with light.

❄️ Physics — 6 Oct

Francis Halzen won for leading the IceCube Neutrino Observatory, buried deep in the ice at the South Pole in Antarctica. IceCube caught high-energy neutrinos — ghost-like particles — coming from far out in space.

💡 IceCube uses about one cubic kilometre of ice as its detector.

🧪 Chemistry — 7 Oct

Henri Kagan (France) and Kenso Soai (Japan) won for discovering how mirror-image molecules — molecules that are like your left and right hands — can end up with one “hand” winning. This matters for making medicines, where one hand can heal and the other may not work.

💡 Molecules that cannot be placed exactly on their mirror image are called chiral, from the Greek word for hand.

📖 Literature — 8 Oct

Anne Carson, a Canadian poet and professor of classics (ancient Greek and Latin), won for her bold, inventive writing.

🕊️ Peace — 9 Oct

Navi Pillay, a judge from South Africa and former UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, won the Nobel Peace Prize.

💡 The prizes come from the will of Alfred Nobel, the inventor of dynamite. They were first given in 1901.
🔬 Science & Nature

🐊 A crocodile from the Sphinx's rock — 6 Oct

Scientists named a new extinct crocodile, Herugavialis cairensis, from fossils found in the 45-million-year-old limestone that the Great Sphinx of Egypt was carved from. The skull was collected in the 1920s, but CT scans showed it was a new species. It is named after the Egyptian god Heru (Horus) — and, like the Sphinx, its skull is missing the tip of its nose!

💡 It was a gharial relative. India's gharial lives mainly in the Chambal river.

🐢 Tortoises outnumber people — 7 Oct

A survey found about 183,000 Aldabra giant tortoises on Aldabra Atoll in the Seychelles — more than the country's 122,000 people. In the 1800s fewer than 1,000 were left. Protection, and removing wild goats that ate their food, brought them back.

🦴 Our ancestors arrived earlier — 7 Oct

A skull piece from Drimolen Cave in South Africa shows that Homo erectus, an early human species, lived in southern Africa about 2 million years ago — around 200,000 years earlier than scientists thought.

💡 Homo erectus means “upright man”; Homo sapiens (us) means “wise man”.

🐧 A shrinking penguin city — 7 Oct

Zavodovski Island, near Antarctica, has lost more than 500,000 chinstrap penguins since 2011 and is no longer the world's largest penguin colony.

🌎 Earthquake in Panama — 9 Oct

A powerful earthquake, above magnitude 7.5, struck Panama in Central America, and a tsunami warning was issued.

💡 The Panama Canal joins the Atlantic and Pacific oceans.

🌀 Hurricane Isaias — 7–9 Oct

Isaias became the first Atlantic hurricane of 2026 — very late, in a quiet season linked to El Niño. It grew to Category 3 and made landfall in Florida, USA, on 9 October.

🦧 Orangutans go home — 11 Oct

Indonesia released eight orangutans, rescued from wildfires, into a protected forest.

💡 Orang utan means “person of the forest” in Malay.
🎨 Arts, Culture & Special Days

💷 Animals on Britain's banknotes — 8 Oct

The Bank of England chose four British animals for its next banknotes: the Atlantic puffin, barn owl, buff-tailed bumblebee and hedgehog. Nearly 500,000 people took part in the public vote. They will replace famous people like Winston Churchill and Jane Austen — the first time animals replace people on English notes.

💡 Puffins are nicknamed “clowns of the sea”, and a hedgehog has about 5,000–7,000 spines.

🪔 Navratri begins — 11 Oct

Sharad Navratri, nine nights honouring Goddess Durga, began on 11 October. It ends with Vijayadashami (Dussehra) on 20 October, celebrating the victory of good over evil.

💡 Karnataka's Mysuru Dasara is the state festival (Nada Habba); its Jamboo Savari procession has an elephant carrying a golden howdah.

✈️ Indian Air Force Day — 8 Oct

The Indian Air Force was founded on 8 October 1932, so 2026 is its 94th anniversary. Its motto, “Nabha Sparsham Deeptam” (Touch the sky with glory), comes from the Bhagavad Gita.

💡 Army Day is 15 January and Navy Day is 4 December.

🛰️ World Space Week ends — 10 Oct

The week ends on 10 October, the day the Outer Space Treaty came into force in 1967. It says space belongs to all humankind and no country can claim the Moon.

🏅 Sports

🏏 India vs West Indies T20 series

In the first of five T20 Internationals, at Lucknow on 6 October, India won by 8 wickets. Captain Shreyas Iyer hit 102 not out — his first (maiden) T20I century — and debutant Naman Dhir took 3 wickets. West Indies won the second match in Ranchi by 6 wickets, so the series is level 1–1.

🏎️ Verstappen wins in Singapore — 11 Oct

Max Verstappen (Red Bull) won the Singapore Grand Prix at Marina Bay from pole position — his first win there. Charles Leclerc was second and Kimi Antonelli, the championship leader, third.

💡 In 2008, Singapore hosted Formula 1's first-ever night race.

🏀 A record fifth MVP — 8 Oct

A'ja Wilson won a record fifth Most Valuable Player award in the WNBA, the top women's basketball league in the USA.

🚴 Youngest-ever winner — 10 Oct

France's Paul Seixas became the youngest winner of Il Lombardia in Italy, one of cycling's five great one-day races, called the “Monuments”.

🏑 Hockey: Bangladesh replaces Pakistan

Pakistan pulled out of the men's Asian Champions Trophy in Punjab, so Bangladesh will play instead. The tournament runs from 27 October to 5 November in Jalandhar and Mohali.

Energy Minerals — Coal, the Black Gold — think and write
Wednesday's topic, one level deeper. Write each answer in your notebook first (4–6 lines), then open the model answer and compare. Then take the 10-question Geo Quiz.
1. How is coal formed?
👀 Show model answer
Plants that died millions of years ago were buried under layers of sediment. Under high heat and pressure beneath beds of sedimentary rocks, over millions of years, their remains changed into carbon-rich coal.
2. What is coal also known as? Why was it so called?
👀 Show model answer
Black gold — black in colour and as valuable as gold, because it powered steam engines, furnaces and electricity and became the backbone of industrial growth.
3. Why were steel industries located near coal mines?
👀 Show model answer
In the early 20th century steel was made in coal-fired furnaces needing huge amounts of coal. Coal is heavy and bulky, so locating plants near mines saved transport cost.
4. Name the four types of coal and explain which is best.
👀 Show model answer
Peat, lignite, bituminous, anthracite. Anthracite is best — over 90% carbon, so it gives the most heat and least smoke. Peat has very little carbon.
5. Mention any two uses of coal.
👀 Show model answer
1. Generating thermal power. 2. Fuel and raw material in cement, chemical and steel industries.
6. Name the leading coal producers of the world and the coal states of India.
👀 Show model answer
World: USA, China, India. India: Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra.
7. Can we live without coal and petroleum? How could industries and cars run in future?
👀 Show model answer
Today it is hard, as they supply most energy. In future we can shift to renewable energy — solar, wind, hydel, tidal and nuclear power; electric vehicles charged by solar/wind; and use energy efficiently.
8. Why is coal mined by shaft mining? Describe a shaft mine.
👀 Show model answer
Coal lies at great depths. A long vertical shaft is dug; miners go down in lift cages on cables and pulleys to coal seams at different levels; coal is brought up and dumped on the surface.
9. How did the Industrial Revolution change the use of coal?
👀 Show model answer
Before, coal mainly heated homes and ran steam engines. From the 18th-century Industrial Revolution, it was also used to generate electricity and fuel factories and furnaces, so demand rose enormously.
10. Explain two environmental problems caused by coal.
👀 Show model answer
1. Air pollution — smoke and ash from burning, and mine dust causing respiratory diseases. 2. Global warming — carbon dioxide traps heat, melting ice caps and glaciers.
