🔬 Science & Space

⭐ A star racing around a black hole — 19 Aug

Astronomers found S301, the closest and fastest star ever seen orbiting Sagittarius A* — the supermassive black hole at the centre of our Milky Way. It goes round once every 8.7 years at about 25,000 km per second.

💡 Light travels about 3,00,000 km/s — so S301 moves at roughly one-twelfth the speed of light!

🧪 Animals that can eat bioplastic — 17 Aug

Scientists at the Max Planck Institute for Marine Microbiology (Germany) found that several animal species have enzymes that break down PHA bioplastics. Until now, only microorganisms were thought to do this.

💡 Enzymes are proteins that speed up chemical reactions in living things.

🚀 India's space plans — 18 & 21 Aug

News reports said ISRO would end a seven-month launch gap with the GISAT-1A satellite in September. On 21 Aug the head of IN-SPACe (Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre) said ISRO will stop making launch vehicles and the private sector will build them.

🎨 Arts & Culture

📷 World Photography Day — 19 Aug

Celebrated every year on 19 August. It remembers 1839, when France announced the daguerreotype — one of the first practical ways of taking photographs, invented by Louis Daguerre.

🏙️ Madras Day — 22 Aug

Chennai (earlier Madras) celebrates its founding day on 22 August, remembering 1639, when the English East India Company got the land where Fort St. George was built.

📚 Booker Prize 2026 — the race is on

The Booker Prize longlist has 13 novels chosen from 163. The shortlist is due on 22 September and the winner on 9 November. The winner gets £50,000.

🏅 Sports

🏸 BWF World Championships in New Delhi — 17–23 Aug

India hosted badminton's World Championships at the Indira Gandhi Arena, New Delhi. Winners: Alex Lanier (France) — men's singles, his first world title; An Se-young (South Korea) — women's singles, her second. India won one bronze: Treesa Jolly & Gayatri Gopichand in women's doubles — only the second Indian women's doubles pair ever to win a World Championships medal. Satwik–Chirag lost in the quarter-finals.

💡 The first Indian women's doubles medal came in 2011 from Jwala Gutta & Ashwini Ponnappa.

🏏 India win at Galle — 15–19 Aug

India beat Sri Lanka by 165 runs in the first Test. Devdutt Padikkal scored 167 — his maiden (first) Test century — and was Player of the Match. Left-arm spinner Manav Suthar took 10 wickets in the match (4/76 and 6/55).

🇫🇷 Sunday · Week 10 · Révision
Pluriels et verbes en -ir — révision
Workbook pages 16–18 and textbook pages 30–33. The -ail / -aux pair, the chassez l'intrus traps, and -ir verbs with tricky subjects.
⚠️ The -ail words

Most -ail words add s — five change to -aux

+ s: le détail → les détails · le portail → les portails · le chandail → les chandails
→ -aux: le bail → les baux · l'émail → les émaux · le travail → les travaux · le vitrail → les vitraux · le corail → les coraux

💡 Remember travaux from the poem: « le travail devient les travaux durs ».
🔍 Chassez l'intrus — why each word is the odd one

Workbook p.17

cheval · chacal · signal · journal → chacal is odd: it takes s (chacals). The others go to -aux.
croix · nez · fils · fille → fille is odd: it changes (filles). The others don't.
parler · réussir · obéir · accomplir → parler is odd: it is an -er verb.
bijou · pou · chou · cou → cou is odd: it takes s (cous). The others take x.

🔧 -ir verbs — find the real subject first

Two subjects joined together

« Ton père et toi » = you (plural) → use the vous form.
« Manuel et moi » = we → use the nous form.

Ton père et toi réunissez… · Manuel et moi choisissons…

Mettez au pluriel — change every word

L'homme porte une croix. → Les hommes portent des croix.
L'enfant joue avec un joujou. → Les enfants jouent avec des joujoux.
Je choisis le journal français. → Nous choisissons les journaux français.
Le professeur donne un signal. → Les professeurs donnent des signaux.

💡 Don't forget the verb! Les hommes portent, les enfants jouent.
🎯 Fun Trivia
Des yeux is so different from un œil because both come from an old Latin word, oculus, that changed differently over the centuries. English does the same with mouse → mice and child → children.
