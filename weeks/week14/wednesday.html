🌍 Geography · Ch 4 Agriculture · Revision Week
Agriculture — Full Chapter Revision
All of Chapter 4 in one go: conditions, types of farming, crops, seasons and the Green Revolution. Use the summary cards, then test yourself with fresh mixed questions.

🗂️ Big picture

Agriculture → depends on climate, soil, topography → two types: subsistence (intensive, shifting) and commercial (plantation, extensive, mixed) → crops: food, cash (fibre, horticulture, plantation) → India's seasons: kharif, rabi, zaid → Green Revolution: benefits vs drawbacks.

🧮 Farming types table

Intensive — monsoon Asia; tiny plots; food crops; manual.
Shifting — forests; slash-and-burn; rice, millets, cassava, yam.
Plantation — colonies; single crop on estates; grown + processed on site.
Extensive — temperate grasslands; vast mechanised farms; wheat, oats, barley.
Mixed — crops + animals; Europe & Asia.

🟫 Soils & crops

Alluvial — all crops · Chernozem — humus-rich (Russia) · Black — cotton (Deccan) · Terra roxa — coffee (Brazil) · Laterite — tea.

🌍 Places to remember

Prairies (Canada, USA) · Steppes (Russia) · Veld (South Africa) · Pampas (Argentina) · Downs (Australia) · Tea: Nilgiris, Darjiling, Kandy · Coffee: Brazil, Mexico, Colombia highlands · Plantations: Assam tea, Ghana cocoa, Brazil coffee, Malaysia rubber.

📝 Exam traps

• Plantation farming sells ALL produce — it is commercial.
• Animals are central to mixed, not extensive, farming.
• Wheat and corn are NOT plantation crops.
• Rice CAN be grown on hill slopes — by terracing.
• Drip irrigation was NOT a Green Revolution input.

🎯 Must-remember facts
Revise the five farming types, the five temperate grasslands, the five Green Revolution inputs and the three cropping seasons — these give most exam marks.

✍️ English Grammar · Ch 6 Kinds of Sentences · Ch 7 Framing Questions
Kinds of Sentences & Framing Questions
Every sentence has a job: to tell, ask, order or exclaim. This week you learn to spot each kind, punctuate it correctly, and build perfect Wh- questions — including the tricky helping verb that most people forget.

🗣️ Four kinds of sentences

• Assertive / declarative — states a fact; ends with a full stop: It's very warm today.
• Interrogative — asks; ends with ?: Shall I make some lemonade?
• Imperative — order, request, advice; subject you is hidden: Don't go outdoors. Switch on the AC. Let's go to the pool.
• Exclamatory — strong feeling; ends with !: What a fool I've been! How hot it is!

💡 Let's + verb is imperative, not a question.

✏️ Punctuating a paragraph

"what a fool I've been why didn't I think of it before"
→ What a fool I've been! Why didn't I think of it before?
Each new sentence starts with a capital; I is always capital; names (Shirley) take capitals.

Good heavens! Look at the time! I've forgotten I have a cake in the oven.

❓ The Wh- words

What — things/information · Why — reason · Where — place · When — time · Who — person (subject) · Whom — person (object) · Whose — owner · Which — choice from a set

🔧 The magic of HOW

• in what way: How did you make this sauce?
• + adjective: How tall are you? How old is your house?
• + much/many: How much are these tomatoes? How many people are coming?
• + adverb: How quickly can you finish? How often do you go to London?
• condition: How are you?

🏗️ Question word order

Wh- word + helping verb + subject + main verb
✗ When you returned home? → ✓ When did you return home?
✗ Which drawer you found the letter? → ✓ In which drawer did you find the letter?
✗ How long you owned this bicycle? → ✓ How long have you owned it?
✗ Where they have hide the treasure? → ✓ Where have they hidden it?
✗ How many sons has your aunt? → ✓ How many sons does your aunt have?

💡 did + base verb: did you return (not returned).

🎯 Questions from answers

Look at the italic part and ask for it:
• India became independent on 15 August 1947 → When did India become independent?
• The car cost me ninety thousand rupees → How much did the car cost you?
• That sports car belongs to my uncle → Whom does that car belong to? / Whose sports car is that?
• My uncle is a mechanical engineer → What is your uncle?
• She is staying with an old friend → With whom is she staying?

✨ Fun facts
• A popular story says the question mark comes from the Latin word quaestio (question): scribes wrote 'qo' at the end of a question, and over time it shrank into '?'.
• In Spanish, questions start with an upside-down mark too: ¿Cómo estás?
• The exclamation mark was once called the 'note of admiration'.
• Who asks about the subject (Who called you?) — and needs no 'did'! Compare: Whom did you call?

🎯 Must-remember rules
• Assertive . · Interrogative ? · Imperative (order/request) . or ! · Exclamatory !
• Wh- + helping verb + subject + main verb
• did / does / do + base form: When did you return?
• Who = subject, Whom = object, Whose = owner
• How + adjective / much / many / often / long
