⚗️ Chemistry · Week 29 · Noble Gases · Day 3 of 5
Superpowers — Properties and Uses of the Noble Gases
Every noble gas has a “superpower” — a property — that gives it a real job. Read the cards, make the 3-column table, then take the 25-question quiz.

🎈 Helium — lighter than air

Property: much less dense than air. Uses: party balloons, weather balloons and airships (blimps). Helium is used instead of hydrogen, which is lighter but catches fire — the airship Hindenburg burned in 1937.
Liquid helium boils at about −269 °C, the lowest of any substance, so it is used to cool the magnets in MRI scanners. Deep-sea divers breathe a helium–oxygen mix.

💡 Helium makes your voice squeaky because sound travels faster through it. Never breathe it in from a balloon — it pushes oxygen out of your lungs and can make you faint.

💡 Neon — glows when electrified

Passing electricity through neon in a sealed glass tube makes it glow a bright reddish-orange. The first neon signs were shown by Georges Claude in Paris in 1910. Other noble gases glow other colours: argon — violet-blue, krypton — whitish, xenon — bluish-white. Many “neon” signs of other colours actually use other gases or coloured coatings (phosphors).

🔥 Argon — the lazy bodyguard

Argon makes up about 0.93% of air — the third most common gas after nitrogen and oxygen. Because it is unreactive, welders blow it around hot metal to keep oxygen away (shielding). It also fills filament light bulbs so the hot filament does not burn up, and the gap in double-glazed windows, where it slows heat loss.

🔦 Krypton and xenon — bright ideas

Krypton is used in some energy-saving and high-performance lamps and camera flashes. Xenon gives a very bright, white light in car headlamps, lighthouse lamps and cinema projectors.

☢️ Radon — the dangerous relative

Radon is radioactive. It forms when uranium and radium in rocks and soil break down. It is very dense, so it can collect in basements and low rooms, where breathing it over many years is harmful. It is the only noble gas found in nature that is radioactive.

💡 Density rises down the group: helium is the lightest noble gas and radon the heaviest.

✨ Fun facts
• The US Declaration of Independence is kept in a case filled with argon to protect it.
• Earth’s helium comes from natural gas wells; it is not renewable.
• Air is about 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen and 0.93% argon.
