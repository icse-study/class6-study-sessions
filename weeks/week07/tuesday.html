⚡ Physics · Day 1 · Measurement
Measurement: Length, Mass & Time
Physical quantities, the systems of units, and how length, mass and time are actually measured. This is the groundwork for Day 2 (temperature & area) — read the cards, try the ✍️ Practice, then take the quiz.
📏 1. Physical Quantities & Units

Magnitude = number + unit

A quantity that can be measured is a physical quantity. Its magnitude is always written as a number followed by a unit — "5" means nothing on its own, but "5 metres" is a measurement.

💡 Change the unit and the number must change with it: 2 m = 200 cm. It is the same length written two ways.

Fundamental vs derived quantities

Fundamental (basic) quantities do not depend on any other quantity — length, mass and time (also temperature, electric current, luminous intensity, amount of substance).
Derived quantities are obtained by combining fundamental ones — area (length × length), volume, speed (length ÷ time), density (mass ÷ volume).

Their units follow the same rule: m is a fundamental unit, while m² and m/s are derived units.

The S.I. system

The C.G.S. system is the one called the metric system. The M.K.S. system was later extended into the S.I. system (International System of Units), which is what science uses worldwide today.

S.I. units to remember: length → metre (m) · mass → kilogram (kg) · time → second (s) · temperature → kelvin (K) · area → square metre (m²).

Conventions for writing S.I. units

1. A unit symbol is never made plural — write 10 kg, not 10 kgs.
2. A symbol takes no full stop after it, unless it ends a sentence — 5 m, not 5 m.
3. A unit named after a scientist is spelt with a small letter but its symbol is a capital — newton → N, kelvin → K.
4. Leave a space between the number and the symbol — 25 cm, not 25cm.

📐 3. Measurement of Length

Units and instruments

S.I. unit: the metre (m). Smaller units: 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm. Larger unit: 1 km = 1000 m.
A metre ruler reads to the nearest millimetre; a measuring tape is used for longer lengths.

1 km = 1000 m · 1 m = 100 cm · 1 cm = 10 mm

Error of parallax

An error of parallax happens when the eye is not kept vertically above the mark being read — seen from a slant, the mark appears shifted and the reading is wrong. Keep the line of sight straight down onto the mark, and start from the zero mark (or subtract the starting reading if the ruler's end is worn).

Measuring something thinner than 1 mm

A ruler cannot read a single coin or a single sheet of paper. Stack many identical items, measure the whole stack, then divide by the number of items.

💡 20 coins stack to 3.0 cm → one coin = 3.0 ÷ 20 = 0.15 cm = 1.5 mm. The same trick gives the thickness of one page of a book.
⚖️ 4. Measurement of Mass

Units and instruments

Mass is the quantity of matter contained in a body. S.I. unit: the kilogram (kg), measured with a beam balance (or a physical / electronic balance).

1 kg = 1000 g · 1 g = 1000 mg · 1 quintal = 100 kg · 1 metric tonne = 1000 kg
⏱ 5. Measurement of Time

The second

S.I. unit: the second (s). One second was defined as 1/86 400 of a mean solar day, because a day contains 24 × 60 × 60 = 86 400 seconds.

1 minute = 60 s · 1 hour = 3600 s · 1 day = 86 400 s

Time-measuring devices

A pendulum clock uses the regular swing of a pendulum; a quartz wall clock uses a vibrating crystal. For short intervals — an athletic race, a science experiment — a stopwatch is used, because it starts and stops on command and reads to a tenth or hundredth of a second.

💡 Every time-keeping device is built on something that repeats itself at a perfectly regular rate.
🔁 6. Looking ahead to Day 2

Area — a derived quantity

Area is length × length, so its S.I. unit is the square metre (m²). Rectangle = l × b · Square = l² · Triangle = ½ × base × height · Circle = π r².

Larger units: 1 are = 100 m² · 1 hectare = 10 000 m² · 1 km² = 106 m². And on the Fahrenheit scale, water freezes at 32 °F (0 °C).

✍️ Short answer — answer on paper, then check
1. State any two proper conventions followed while writing S.I. units.
▸ Show model answer
(i) The symbol of a unit is never written in the plural — 10 kg, not 10 kgs.
(ii) No full stop is put after a unit symbol unless it ends the sentence — 5 m, not 5 m.
Also acceptable: a unit named after a scientist is spelt with a small letter but its symbol is a capital (newton → N); and a space is left between the number and the symbol.
2. How can a metre ruler be used to measure the thickness of a single coin when its individual thickness is less than 1 mm?
▸ Show model answer
Stack a large number of identical coins one above the other, keeping the pile straight. Measure the total height of the stack with the ruler and divide by the number of coins. For example, 20 coins measuring 3.0 cm give 3.0 ÷ 20 = 0.15 cm = 1.5 mm per coin. The method is needed because the ruler's smallest division (1 mm) is larger than one coin.
3. Distinguish between fundamental quantities and derived quantities with one example each.
▸ Show model answer
Fundamental quantities are independent — they are not obtained from any other quantity. Example: length (S.I. unit: metre). Derived quantities are obtained by combining two or more fundamental quantities. Example: area = length × length (S.I. unit: m²); speed = length ÷ time (m/s) is another.
4. Calculate the area of a rectangular park that is 50 m long and 30 m wide.
▸ Show model answer
Area = length × breadth = 50 m × 30 m = 1500 m².
5. Convert 2.5 kilometres into metres.
▸ Show model answer
1 km = 1000 m, so 2.5 km = 2.5 × 1000 = 2500 m.
🔴 Stretch questions
6. What is an error of parallax, and how is it avoided?
▸ Show model answer
An error of parallax is the wrong reading obtained when the eye is not placed vertically above the mark being read on the scale — seen from a slant, the mark appears to shift against the scale. It is avoided by keeping the line of sight perpendicular to the scale, directly above the reading mark.
7. Show, with working, that one mean solar day contains 86 400 seconds.
▸ Show model answer
1 day = 24 hours, 1 hour = 60 minutes, 1 minute = 60 seconds.
So 1 day = 24 × 60 × 60 = 86 400 s. That is exactly why one second was defined as 1/86 400 of a mean solar day.
8. A book of 400 pages is 2 cm thick, ignoring the covers. Find the thickness of one sheet of paper in millimetres. (Careful — one sheet carries two pages.)
▸ Show model answer
400 pages = 200 sheets. Thickness of one sheet = 2 cm ÷ 200 = 0.01 cm = 0.1 mm. Dividing by 400 instead gives 0.05 mm, which is the thickness of a page, not of a sheet.
