Revision 2 — Integers, Rationals & Properties
Weeks 2–9 revision. Signs on the number line, the four operations, rational numbers and the laws of arithmetic.
➗ The ideas

Integers and the number line

Integers are the positive numbers, the negative numbers and zero. On the number line, values grow to the right.

−12 < −8 < 0 < 5
Adding moves right · Subtracting moves left
💡 With negatives the bigger-looking number is the smaller one: −12 is less than −8.

Sign rules

(+) + (+) → add · (−) + (−) → add, keep the minus
Different signs → subtract, keep the sign of the bigger value
(+) × (+) = + · (−) × (−) = + · (+) × (−) = −
Same rules for division
💡 Two minuses that touch turn into a plus: 8 − (−6) = 8 + 6 = 14.

Rational numbers

A rational number can be written as p/q, where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0. Every integer, fraction and terminating decimal is rational.

−3 = −3/1 · 0.25 = 1/4 · 2/7 is already in that form
💡 The denominator can never be zero — dividing by zero has no meaning.

Properties of operations

Commutative — order does not matter: 17 × 8 = 8 × 17.
Associative — grouping does not matter: (2 × 3) × 4 = 2 × (3 × 4).
Distributive — 25 × 198 = 25 × (200 − 2) = 5,000 − 50 = 4,950.
Identity — a + 0 = a, a × 1 = a.

Subtraction and division are NOT commutative: 8 − 5 ≠ 5 − 8.
🎉 Fun facts & trivia

Negative numbers were used in China by about 200 BCE with red and black counting rods, and in India by Brahmagupta, who explained them as debts. European mathematicians still called them 'absurd numbers' as late as the 1600s.

The word rational has nothing to do with being sensible — it comes from ratio, because such a number is a ratio of two integers.

BODMAS has a twin in other countries: BIDMAS in Britain and PEMDAS in America. The rule is identical; only the nicknames differ.

📜 The Year in Numbers · Part 2 of 4 — The Numbers Nobody Wanted

When merchants first wrote down debts, they had no symbol for 'less than nothing'. Brahmagupta, writing in India in 628 CE, gave the rules we still use: a debt minus a debt is a debt, a debt subtracted from nothing is a fortune, and the product of two debts is a fortune.

That last rule — two negatives make a positive — bothered people for a thousand years. Even in the 17th century, respected European mathematicians refused to accept negative answers and called them false roots.

What settled the argument was the number line: once negatives had a home to the left of zero, they stopped looking like a trick and started looking obvious.

5️⃣ Motion

Four kinds

Rectilinear (straight line) · Circular (along a circle) · Rotational (spinning on its axis) · Periodic (repeats in equal intervals)

6️⃣ Force

Remember

Push or pull · six effects · never changes mass · needs magnitude and direction · contact: muscular, friction, tension, normal reaction · non-contact: gravitational, electrostatic, magnetic · double distance → ¼ force

7️⃣ Friction

Remember

Opposes motion · caused by interlocking bumps · makes heat and wear · depends on roughness, weight, medium (not area) · static > sliding > rolling · reduce: polish, lubricate, ball bearings, streamline · increase: roughen, dry, add weight
