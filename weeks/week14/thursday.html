➗ The ideas

Ten words, ten meanings

Centre — the fixed point.
Radius — centre to circumference.
Diameter — through the centre, 2 × radius, the longest chord.
Circumference — the boundary.
Chord — joins two points on the circle.
Secant — cuts the circle twice.
Tangent — touches once, at the point of contact.
Arc — part of the circumference (minor / major).
Sector — two radii + an arc (quadrant = 90° sector).
Segment — chord + arc (major segment holds the centre).

💡 If you can sort a figure into these ten names, the chapter is done.

Numbers to keep ready

Diameter = 2 × Radius · Radius = Diameter ÷ 2
Quadrant = 90° · Semi-circle = 180° · Whole circle = 360°
Chord = radius → angle at centre 60°
Angle in a semi-circle = 90°

🎉 Fun facts & trivia

π is irrational: its decimals never end and never repeat. Computers have now calculated over 100 trillion digits, but 39 digits are enough to measure the whole observable universe to the width of a hydrogen atom.

Pi Day is 14 March, written 3/14 in the American style. Some Indian students prefer 22 July — 22/7 — the old fraction approximation for π.

The Indian mathematician Aryabhata gave π as 3.1416 in 499 CE, and hinted that the value could never be written exactly — over a thousand years before that was proved.

📜 Part 7 of 12 — Aryabhata's Approximation

In 499 CE, a 23-year-old astronomer named Aryabhata wrote a verse: add 4 to 100, multiply by 8, add 62,000, and that is roughly the circumference of a circle of diameter 20,000.

Work it out: (104 × 8 + 62,000) ÷ 20,000 = 62,832 ÷ 20,000 = 3.1416. Correct to four decimal places.

The remarkable part is the word he used — āsanna, meaning 'approaching' or 'approximate'. He seems to have understood that π can never be written down exactly, a fact that was only proved in 1761, by Johann Lambert.
