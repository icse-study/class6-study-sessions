➗ The ideas

Ten words, ten meanings

Centre — the fixed point.
Radius — centre to circumference.
Diameter — through the centre, 2 × radius, the longest chord.
Circumference — the boundary.
Chord — joins two points on the circle.
Secant — cuts the circle twice.
Tangent — touches once, at the point of contact.
Arc — part of the circumference (minor / major).
Sector — two radii + an arc (quadrant = 90° sector).
Segment — chord + arc (major segment holds the centre).

💡 If you can sort a figure into these ten names, the chapter is done.

🎉 Fun facts & trivia

π is irrational: its decimals never end and never repeat. Computers have now calculated over 100 trillion digits, but 39 digits are enough to measure the whole observable universe to the width of a hydrogen atom.

Pi Day is 14 March, written 3/14 in the American style. Some Indian students prefer 22 July — 22/7 — the old fraction approximation for π.

The Indian mathematician Aryabhata gave π as 3.1416 in 499 CE, and hinted that the value could never be written exactly — over a thousand years before that was proved.

📜 Part 7 of 12 — Aryabhata's Approximation

In 499 CE, a 23-year-old astronomer named Aryabhata wrote a verse: add 4 to 100, multiply by 8, add 62,000, and that is roughly the circumference of a circle of diameter 20,000.

Work it out: (104 × 8 + 62,000) ÷ 20,000 = 62,832 ÷ 20,000 = 3.1416. Correct to four decimal places.

The remarkable part is the word he used — āsanna, meaning 'approaching' or 'approximate'. He seems to have understood that π can never be written down exactly, a fact that was only proved in 1761, by Johann Lambert.

🚫 La négation

ne before the verb, pas after it

To make a sentence negative, sandwich the verb between ne and pas.

Subject + ne + verb + pas + rest
Je parle anglais. → Je ne parle pas anglais.
💡 Find the verb first. Then put ne just in front of it and pas just after it.

ne → n' before a vowel or silent h

Before a, e, i, o, u, y and a silent h, ne loses its e.

Il est médecin. → Il n'est pas médecin.
Elle aime le chocolat. → Elle n'aime pas le chocolat.
Elle habite à Londres. → Elle n'habite pas à Londres.

Textbook examples (p.53)

Nous ne sommes pas stupides.
Je ne suis pas chinois.
Manuel et Denis ne regardent pas la télé.
M. Dupont ne travaille pas à l'ambassade.
Il n'est pas journaliste.

💡 Good to know: after ne … pas, un / une / des usually become de — J'ai un chien → Je n'ai pas de chien. You'll study this properly later; for now, the definite articles le / la / les stay the same.
💬 Answering yes and no

Change the person, then answer

When someone asks with tu, you answer with je. When they ask with vous, you answer with nous (or je if vous means just you).

Tu parles français ?
• Oui, je parle français.  • Non, je ne parle pas français.
Vous êtes françaises ?
• Oui, nous sommes françaises.  • Non, nous ne sommes pas françaises.

1️⃣ What is chemistry?

The study of matter and its changes

Chemistry studies what matter is made of, its properties, and how it changes.

Cooking, rusting, digestion, soap cleaning, medicines, fireworks — all chemistry.
2️⃣ From alchemy to chemistry

The alchemists

Alchemy comes from the Greek khemeia, 'the art of changing metals'. Alchemists hunted for the Philosopher's Stone to turn cheap metals into gold and give everlasting life. They never found it — but they first isolated zinc and phosphorus and invented useful apparatus.

💡 Alchemy failed as a science because it mixed experiments with magic and secrets.

🇮🇳 Ancient Indian chemistry

The Iron Pillar near the Qutub Minar in Delhi is about 1600 years old and has hardly rusted — proof of India's great skill with metals.

Why glass, why Pyrex?

Glass is transparent, easy to clean and doesn't react. Pyrex / Borosil expands very little, so it doesn't crack when heated.

Safety rules

Lab coat on · never taste or smell chemicals directly · never point a heated test tube at anyone · wash hands after · report every accident · a spill on skin → cold running water, then tell your teacher.

Heat and expansion

Heating makes particles move faster and spread apart, so solids, liquids and gases expand. The ball-and-ring experiment: a heated ball no longer passes through the ring.

💡 A burning candle shows both: wax melts (physical) and burns (chemical).
