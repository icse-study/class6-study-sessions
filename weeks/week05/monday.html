➗ Maths — Fractions & Decimals

The three kinds of fraction

Proper — numerator smaller than denominator (3/5). Improper — numerator bigger (17/4). Mixed — a whole number plus a fraction (4¼).

Mixed → improper: multiply the whole by the denominator, add the numerator. 4⅗ = (4×5+3)/5 = 23/5. Improper → mixed: divide. 17÷4 = 4 remainder 1 → 4¼.

Equivalent fractions & simplest form

Multiply or divide both top and bottom by the same number. 3/7 with denominator 28: 28÷7 = 4, so multiply both by 4 → 12/28.

Simplest form = divide both by their HCF. 24/36: HCF is 12 → 2/3.

The four operations

Add/subtract: like fractions → just the numerators. Unlike → take the LCM of the denominators first. Multiply: top×top, bottom×bottom, then simplify. Divide: flip the second fraction and multiply.

3/4 ÷ 9/16 = 3/4 × 16/9 = 48/36 = 4/3. The flipped fraction is called the reciprocal.

Decimals

Place values after the point are tenths, hundredths, thousandths. To add or subtract, line up the decimal points and fill gaps with zeros.

0.45 = 45/100 = 9/20. And 3/20 = 15/100 = 0.15.

Plurals, opposites and grammar words

Most things add -ಗಳು for the plural: ಮನೆ → ಮನೆಗಳು, ಪುಸ್ತಕ → ಪುಸ್ತಕಗಳು. People usually take -ರು: ಹುಡುಗ → ಹುಡುಗರು.

A verb is ಕ್ರಿಯಾಪದ and a noun is ನಾಮಪದ. The opposite of ದೊಡ್ಡ (big) is ಸಣ್ಣ (small); the feminine of ಅಣ್ಣ is ಅಕ್ಕ.
📖 English — Reading a Story

The parts of a narrative

The introduction sets up the characters and the setting (where and when). The climax is the turning point, and the conclusion resolves the conflict. The main character is the protagonist; whoever opposes them is the antagonist.

Internal conflict happens inside a character's mind — a moral or emotional dilemma. External conflict is against another person or force.

Figures of speech & terms to know

Simile compares using like or as ("brave as a lion"). Metaphor compares directly. Alliteration repeats the opening consonant sound ("silent snakes slither"). Imagery is descriptive writing that makes you see, hear or feel the scene.

R.T.C. = Reference to Context. Blank verse = unrhymed lines. A fable is a short tale, often with animals, that ends in a moral. An abstract noun names something you cannot touch: honesty, bravery, freedom.
