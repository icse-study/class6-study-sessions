📖 English Literature · Week 26
Ch 8 · Paying the Price — Munshi Premchand
Translated from Hindi by M. Asaduddin. Allarakkhi, a street sweeper with a sick baby, calls the inspector names — and he hears. Themes: poverty, fear, kindness and conscience.
🧹 The story, in order

Allarakkhi sweeps the streets. Almost every month her pay is cut as a penalty; sometimes she gets only three or four rupees. The inspector of sanitation, Khairat Ali Khan, is kind to the hundreds of other sweeper women and has a good reputation — but Allarakkhi always gets a reprimand. She is not a shirker: even in bitter cold she sweeps from before daylight till nine. Her husband Husaini helps when he can.

1. Payday makes her weep; she is on tenterhooks, calculating like a student before an exam.
2. She sits to catch her breath — the inspector appears on his ekka and jots down her name. Later she eats a paisa’s worth of candy — her name “went down in red” again.
3. Her baby has had cough and fever for a month; she has come late several times. She expects half pay.
4. The baby howls and clings. Allarakkhi brandishes the broom: “That rude, evil inspector will appear any moment.” He is right there — on a bicycle today.
5. Gruffly, he tells her to take the sick child home and send Husaini to sweep. Then: “Why were you calling me names?” She denies it, in a blue funk, and bursts into tears.
6. Husaini consoles her: if they cut the pay, let them; he’ll complain to the panchayat; “If our people had such unity, would the inspector ever dare fine us so much?”
7. At the municipal office: vendors with paan, Pathans collecting debts. Her name, always called after Champa’s, is passed over. Humiliated, she wishes the earth would swallow her.
8. At last her name is called: the full six rupees — for the first time in three years. She is stupefied. “No, there’s no fine this time.”
9. She moves away, but is not content: she regrets calling him names.

🔑 What examiners look for

Allarakkhi: hard-working, poor, frightened, honest at heart — she feels guilt at the end.
Khairat Ali Khan: gruff manner but kind actions — he sends her home, doesn’t fire her, and gives her full pay.
Husaini: supportive, practical, believes in unity and fairness.
Title: she escapes the fine, but “pays the price” of her harsh words in regret.
Money: an anna = one-sixteenth of a rupee, so eight annas = half a rupee.

✏️ Words & phrases

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Munshi Premchand (1880–1936)

Real name Dhanpat Rai Shrivastava — a giant of Hindi-Urdu literature, called “Upanyas Samrat” (Emperor among Novelists).

🌟 Fun facts
• He wrote over 250 short stories and about a dozen novels.
• He first wrote under the name Nawab Rai; the British banned his early story collection Soz-e-Watan for being patriotic, so he took a new pen name — Premchand.
• In his story “Idgah”, little Hamid spends his fair money on a pair of tongs so his grandmother won’t burn her fingers.
• His story Shatranj Ke Khilari (The Chess Players) was filmed by Satyajit Ray in 1977 — a link to Javed Akhtar’s chess poem!
• Classic novels: Godaan, Gaban. His work speaks for the poor and attacks poverty, corruption and the class system.
• Our story was translated from Hindi by M. Asaduddin.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. Why do you think Khairat Ali Khan did not cut any money from Allarakkhi’s pay in the end?
👀 Show model answer
When he saw Allarakkhi working with a sick, crying baby, he understood how hard her life was. Instead of punishing her for calling him names, he sent her home with the child and asked Husaini to do the sweeping. Giving her the full pay was an act of compassion. It shows he truly deserved his reputation as a kind-hearted man.
2. Why was Allarakkhi not content even after receiving her full pay?
👀 Show model answer
She had called the inspector a “rude, evil inspector” and then lied that she had not. Yet he had treated her with kindness and given her the full six rupees for the first time in three years. She realised she had judged him too harshly, so instead of feeling happy, she felt guilty and regretted her words.
