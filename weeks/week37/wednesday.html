Grand Revision 5 — Farms, Mines & the Two Americas
The last revision week covers four chapters: Agriculture, Minerals, North America and South America. With weeks 33–36 (Ch 1–3), you have now revised the whole book.

🌾 Agriculture recap

Conditions: climate, soil, topography. Types: intensive & shifting subsistence; plantation, extensive, mixed commercial. Crops: food, cash (fibre, horticulture, plantation). Seasons: kharif, rabi, zaid. Green Revolution: HYV seeds, fertilisers, pesticides, irrigation, machines — benefits vs drawbacks.

⛏️ Minerals recap

Rock ← minerals → ores. Ferrous (iron ore, manganese), non-ferrous (copper, bauxite), precious (gold, silver, platinum), non-metallic (mica, limestone), energy (coal, petroleum, natural gas). Mining: opencast, shaft, alluvial. Conserve!

🔗 Farm meets mine

Fertilisers come from minerals (potash, phosphate) and natural gas; tractors are made of steel (iron ore + coal); pumps run on diesel (petroleum) or electricity (coal, hydel). Modern farming depends on mining.

🌎 North America recap

Laurasia → 3rd largest · 7°N–83°N · Bering Strait, Isthmus of Panama · 4 divisions: Western Cordilleras (Denali 6,190 m), Canadian Shield (Great Lakes), Central Plains (Mississippi), Eastern Highlands (Appalachians) · rain-shadow deserts · Canadian lumbering (high riggers, sawyers, skidders, haulers).

🌎 South America recap

Gondwanaland → 4th largest · 13°N–55°S · 12 countries · Andes (>7,000 km, Aconcagua 6,959 m, Ring of Fire) · Guiana & Brazilian Highlands (Angel Falls 979 m) · Amazon, Pampas, Atacama, Patagonia · Galapagos · Amerindians and the shrinking Selvas.

📖 Story time
The man who saved a billion lives
Norman Borlaug grew up on a small Iowa farm. In Mexico he spent years cross-breeding thousands of wheat plants by hand, hunting for short, sturdy, disease-resistant varieties that would not fall over under heavy grain. In the 1960s, when India and Pakistan faced famine, his seeds arrived by ship. Punjab's farmers doubled their harvests within a few years. In 1970 Borlaug won the Nobel Peace Prize — people say his work saved about a billion people from hunger.
📖 Story time
The deep gold of Kolar
The Kolar Gold Fields in Karnataka were once among the deepest mines in the world — shafts went down about 3 km, where rock was so hot that miners needed cooling machines. In 1902 Kolar became one of the first places in Asia to get electricity, from the Shivanasamudra hydroelectric plant on the Kaveri, built specially to power the mines! Kolar closed in 2001 after over a century of mining.
📖 Story time
Pongal, Lohri, Sankranti — one harvest, many names
Every January, as the Sun moves north, farmers across India celebrate the winter harvest: Makara Sankranti in Karnataka with ellu-bella (sesame and jaggery), Pongal in Tamil Nadu with sweet rice boiled till it overflows, Lohri in Punjab with bonfires, and Bihu in Assam. Different names, one thank-you to the land.
📖 Story time
The lost city in the clouds
For centuries, only local farmers knew of the ruins hidden on a ridge above the Urubamba River in Peru. In 1911, a local farmer led American explorer Hiram Bingham up the steep slope, and the world learned of Machu Picchu. The Spanish conquerors had never found it, so it survived almost untouched — its earthquake-proof Inca walls still standing 500 years later.
