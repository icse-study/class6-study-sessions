🔁 Quick recap

Paying the Price (Munshi Premchand) — Allarakkhi, a poor sweeper fined almost every month, calls the inspector “rude, evil” in front of him. Instead of firing her, he sends her home with her sick baby and gives her full pay — and she is left regretting her words.
What Is This Game? (Javed Akhtar) — a chess player, instead of moving, wonders whether the pieces are toys or a real army, whether chess is a game or a war, and why its rules are what they are.

🧩 Linking ideas

Both texts are about rules and power: the inspector’s fines and the rules of chess. Both make us ask whether rules are fair. Both have a twist of perspective: Allarakkhi misjudges the inspector; the chess player sees the game in a new way.
Premchand’s Shatranj Ke Khilari (The Chess Players) links the two authors.

📋 Grammar checklist

FANBOYS and other conjunctions · correlative verb agreement · hyphen (three uses) · -tion / -sion / -cion / -cian · phrasal verbs · repetition · expressing opinions (I strongly feel, as I see it, in my opinion) · notice writing.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. Do you think Allarakkhi should have been more sincere at work? Give reasons.
👀 Show model answer
Allarakkhi was hard-working — she swept from before daylight even in bitter cold. But she sometimes rested, ate candy at work and came late, and the inspector noted these. Her reasons were real: exhaustion and a sick baby kept her up at night. She was sincere at heart, but she needed help, not just fines — which is what the inspector finally gave her.
2. Do you think the title “What Is This Game?” is appropriate?
👀 Show model answer
Yes. The whole poem is a string of questions the speaker asks himself: what the pieces are, whether chess is a game or a war, and why its rules exist. The title is the question repeated in the poem — “Then what is this game?” — and it captures his puzzled, thoughtful mood.
