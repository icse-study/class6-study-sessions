🔬 Science & Space

🛰️ ISRO launches EOS-05 (GISAT-1A) — 4 Sep (IST)

The GSLV-F17 rocket launched EOS-05, also called GISAT-1A, from Satish Dhawan Space Centre, Sriharikota. It is India's first imaging (Earth-watching) satellite built to work from geosynchronous orbit, about 36,000 km up. It ended ISRO's seven-month launch gap.

💡 A satellite in geosynchronous orbit goes round the Earth once a day, so it can keep watching the same region.

🌋 Anak Krakatau erupts — 4–6 Sep

The volcano Anak Krakatau in Indonesia erupted for more than 24 hours, throwing ash up to 50,000 feet. Eight airports shut and nearly 3,000 flights were disrupted; air quality worsened as far as Jakarta.

💡 'Anak Krakatau' means 'child of Krakatoa' — it grew from the remains of Krakatoa, whose 1883 eruption was one of the deadliest ever.

🐖 Pig kidney record — 3 Sep

A genetically modified pig kidney worked inside a living human for 271 days before the patient received a human donor kidney. Using animal organs in humans is called xenotransplantation.

🎨 Arts & Culture

🦁 Venice Film Festival — Golden Lion

At the 83rd Venice Film Festival (Italy), the top prize, the Golden Lion, went to Woman Unknown by Danish-Egyptian director May El-Toukhy. Actor George Clooney received a Golden Lion for Lifetime Achievement.

💡 Venice is the world's oldest film festival (started 1932).

🍎 Teachers' Day — 5 Sep

India celebrates Teachers' Day on the birthday of Dr Sarvepalli Radhakrishnan, a great teacher and philosopher who became India's second President.

🪈 Janmashtami — 4 Sep

The birth of Lord Krishna was celebrated. In Maharashtra, teams form human pyramids to break the dahi handi (pot of curd).

🏅 Sports

🎯 Diamond League Final, Brussels — 4–5 Sep

Sri Lanka's Rumesh Pathirage won the men's javelin at the Diamond League Final with a throw of 89.04 m at the King Baudouin Stadium, Brussels (Belgium). India's Neeraj Chopra also competed.

🎾 US Open begins — 31 Aug

The last tennis Grand Slam of the year began in New York. The four Grand Slams are the Australian Open, French Open, Wimbledon and US Open.

🎯 Fun Trivia
Paris is called la Ville Lumière for two reasons often given: it was a centre of the 18th-century Enlightenment (les Lumières), and it was one of the first cities to light its streets with gas lamps.
