⚗️ Chemistry · Week 16 · Ch 2 Common Laboratory Apparatus and Equipments
The chemistry lab — every tool and why it is made that way
Chemistry is built on experiments — activities done under controlled conditions while we observe what happens. This week you revisit every piece of apparatus in Chapter 2: what it is made of, what it is used for, and the safety rules that keep a lab accident-free.

🔬 Observation & experiment

• Observation: noticing something with our senses (see, hear, touch, smell, taste). Not every observation is scientific — a poet's observation is not.
• Experiment: an activity done under controlled conditions to observe a natural or artificially created event.
• Robert William Boyle was the first to write down the apparatus, procedure and observations of his experiments.

💡 Hot / lukewarm / cold water test: the same lukewarm water feels warm to a cold finger and cold to a hot finger — hotness is relative.

🏫 Lab facilities

• Working table: gas burner, sink with tap, reagent shelf, waste-paper basket
• Reagent shelf: bottles with clear labels
• Exhaust fan near the roof: removes poisonous gases and fumes
• Fume closet: for experiments that give off harmful fumes
• Balance room: dust-free and smoke-free, holds physical, chemical and electronic balances

🧪 Glass apparatus

• Test tube (hard/Pyrex glass): tests and heating small amounts
• Beaker: preparing and keeping solutions
• Flasks: round-bottom (heats evenly), flat-bottom, conical
• Measuring cylinder: measures volume of liquids in mL
• Funnel: filtering and transferring liquids · Thistle funnel: adds liquid in a controlled way
• Gas jar: collecting gases · Delivery tube: carries gases
• Glass rod: stirring · Watch glass: evaporating tiny amounts, covering a beaker or gas jar

🔥 Heating & holding

• Bunsen burner (gas): parts — base, gas pipe, air hole, air regulator, burner tube, flame
• Spirit lamp: glass, brass or steel, burns spirit through a wick
• Tripod stand (iron, 3 legs) + wire gauze (iron mesh, asbestos centre) spreads heat evenly
• Crucible (porcelain): strong direct heating · tongs lift a hot crucible
• Evaporating dish (porcelain) · water bath when a substance cannot be heated directly
• Test-tube holder: metal clamp with a handle for hot tubes

🔩 Other equipment

Mortar & pestle (porcelain): grinding · Test-tube stand (wood/plastic) · Reagent bottle: storing chemicals · Water trough: collecting gas over water · Analytical balance: weighing · spatula, cork borer, file, beehive shelf, sand bath, iron stand with clamp, test-tube brush

💡 A round-bottom flask is NOT for storing chemicals — that is a reagent bottle's job.

🛡️ Why glass? & safety rules

Glass is transparent, withstands high temperature, is easy to clean and does not react with most chemicals. Pyrex/Borosil hardly expands, so it does not crack on heating.

Safety: never work alone · wear a lab coat · use small quantities · never touch, taste or sniff unknown chemicals · point a test tube's mouth away from everyone · don't pour hot concentrated acid into the sink · on a burn or acid splash, wash with cold running water and tell the teacher · broken glass and filter paper go in the dustbin, not the sink · wash hands with soap.

✨ Fun facts
• Pyrex glass was first sold in 1915. It contains boron, which is why it barely expands when hot.
• Robert Bunsen popularised the Bunsen burner in 1855 — and he never patented it.
• Old labs had no exhaust fans; chemists sometimes worked outdoors to escape poisonous fumes.
• Lab balances are kept in a separate, dust-free room because even a speck of dust or a draught can change a reading.

Q. Why is a test tube's mouth pointed away while heating?
👀 Show answerHot liquid can spurt out and hurt someone.

Q. Which piece covers the mouth of a gas jar?
👀 Show answerA watch glass (or a glass plate).
🎯 Must-remember
• Experiment = activity under controlled conditions
• Porcelain: evaporating dish, crucible, mortar & pestle
• Measuring cylinder measures volume; reagent bottle stores chemicals
• Wire gauze spreads heat evenly; water bath for gentle heating
• Glass: transparent, heat-resistant, easy to clean, unreactive
• Burn or splash: cold running water + tell the teacher
