Grand Revision 2 — Latitudes, Longitudes & Earth's Motions
The invisible grid around Earth and the two journeys our planet makes every day and every year — plus a sailor who lost a whole day!

🧵 Latitudes (parallels)

Imaginary lines running east–west, measured in degrees north or south of the Equator (0°). They are parallel and get smaller towards the poles (90°N, 90°S). Key ones: Tropic of Cancer 23½°N, Tropic of Capricorn 23½°S, Arctic Circle 66½°N, Antarctic Circle 66½°S.

📍 Longitudes (meridians)

Imaginary lines from pole to pole, measured east or west of the Prime Meridian (0°, Greenwich) up to 180°. All are equal in length. The 180° line is roughly the International Date Line.

🌍 Heat zones

Torrid (between the Tropics) — hottest; Temperate (Tropics to Arctic/Antarctic Circles) — moderate; Frigid (Circles to poles) — coldest.

🔄 Rotation

Earth spins on its axis from west to east in about 24 hours → day and night. The axis is tilted 23½° from the vertical (66½° with the orbit). The line between day and night is the circle of illumination.

☀️ Revolution

Earth goes round the Sun in about 365¼ days → seasons (because of the tilt). The extra ¼ day adds up to a leap year (366 days, 29 Feb) every 4 years.

🌗 Solstices & equinoxes

21 June — summer solstice (Sun over Tropic of Cancer, longest day in the north). 22 December — winter solstice (Sun over Capricorn). 21 March & 23 September — equinoxes, Sun over the Equator, day = night everywhere.

⏰ Time

Earth turns 360° in 24 h = 15° per hour = 1° every 4 minutes. Places to the east are ahead in time. IST is based on 82½°E (near Mirzapur) — 5 h 30 min ahead of Greenwich.

📖 Story time
The sailors who lost a day
In 1522, the survivors of Magellan's expedition — the first to sail all the way around the world — reached the Cape Verde islands. Their careful logbook said it was Wednesday, but the people on shore insisted it was Thursday! By sailing westward with the Sun around the globe, they had "lost" a whole day. This puzzle is exactly why we have the International Date Line today.
📖 Story time
Harrison's magic clock
Sailors could find latitude from the stars, but longitude needed exact time — and clocks failed on rolling ships. Thousands of sailors died in shipwrecks. A carpenter named John Harrison spent 40 years building a sea-clock (the marine chronometer, 1760s) that kept perfect time at sea. With it, navigators could finally calculate longitude: every 4 minutes of time difference = 1°.
🤔 Did you know? Trivia corner
• Earth spins at about 1,670 km/h at the Equator — faster than a jet plane — yet we feel nothing.
• A person born on 29 February celebrates a real birthday only once every four years.
• Russia stretches across 11 time zones; India uses just one.
• At the North Pole in summer the Sun does not set for about six months.
🎯 Must-remember facts
• Equator 0° · Tropics 23½° · Polar circles 66½° · Poles 90°
• Prime Meridian 0° (Greenwich) · 180° ~ Date Line
• Rotation W→E, 24 h, day & night · Revolution 365¼ days, seasons
• 21 Jun / 22 Dec solstices · 21 Mar / 23 Sep equinoxes
• 1° = 4 min · IST 82½°E = GMT + 5:30
