Hi, I'm Ruby, a red blood cell 🔴
I live only about four months, but I travel round your body hundreds of thousands of times. Here is my life.

🧩 Warm-up: blood cells

Find me — I'm the one without a nucleus!
StageRuby's story
bornin the bone marrow; I lose my nucleus to make room for haemoglobin
shapea biconcave disc — thin in the middle, so gases get in and out fast, and I can bend
jobcarry oxygen on my iron-containing haemoglobin
daily loopright heart → lungs (pick up O₂) → left heart → body (drop O₂) → back again
tight squeezecapillaries are so narrow I pass in single file
dangersPlasmodium (malaria) can burst me; lack of iron means too little haemoglobin (anaemia)
old ageafter about 120 days I'm worn out and broken down; my iron is recycled
replacementover 2 million new RBCs are made every second
🎲 Fun Corner — Ruby's riddles
1. I have no nucleus. Can I divide to make new RBCs?
👀 Show answer
No — new RBCs come from the bone marrow.
2. What makes me red?
👀 Show answer
Haemoglobin (iron + protein).
3. Where do I pick up oxygen?
👀 Show answer
In the capillaries around the alveoli.
🎯 Fun Trivia
• 🔴 You have about 25 trillion red blood cells — roughly two out of every three cells in your body!
• 🧲 The iron in your RBCs is the same element as in an iron nail — about enough for a small nail in all.
• 🏔️ People living high in the mountains have more RBCs to catch enough oxygen.
