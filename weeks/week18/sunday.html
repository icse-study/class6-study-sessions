🌍 World

🏅 Last Nobel of 2026 — 12 Oct

The Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences went to American economists Robert Hall and Michael Woodford, for explaining how governments and central banks can keep a country’s economy steady.

💡 This was the sixth and final Nobel of the year — after Medicine, Physics, Chemistry, Literature and Peace last week.

🚢 The oldest shipwreck — 14 Oct

Archaeologists began excavating the oldest known shipwreck in the world, at Kekova on the coast of Turkey. The ship sank about 4,500 years ago (around 2700–2400 BC), in the Bronze Age.

😷 Haze over Singapore — 14 Oct

Smoke from wildfires in Indonesia gave Singapore its worst air of the year, and Timor-Leste closed its schools for three days.

💡 Haze is made of tiny particles called PM2.5 — smaller than 2.5 thousandths of a millimetre — which can get deep into our lungs.

🌉 A bridge finally opens — 15 Oct

The New Yalu River Bridge between China and North Korea opened after a 12-year delay.

🏜️ Drought in England — 15 Oct

The UK declared a severe drought: more than 80% of England was in drought and big reservoirs were below 20% full.

🔬 Science & Nature

💚 The first green star — 15 Oct

Astronomers announced the first green star ever found, WD J1330+6435. “WD” means it is a white dwarf — the small, hot leftover core of a star.

💡 Normal stars never look green: they give out light of many colours at once, which mixes to look white, yellow, orange or blue.

⏱️ Goodbye, leap seconds — 15 Oct

The General Conference on Weights and Measures voted to stop adding leap seconds to world time (UTC) from 2027. Leap seconds were added because the Earth’s spin is slightly uneven.

💡 Since 1972, 27 leap seconds have been added to clocks.

🌳 Good news for the Amazon — 16 Oct

Brazil said Amazon deforestation fell to 5,796 km² in the year to July 2026 — one of the lowest levels since records began in 1988.

🦕 A 210-million-year-old dinosaur — 13 Oct

Scientists described a newly identified dinosaur from Zimbabwe that lived about 210 million years ago, helping them understand Africa’s ancient ecosystems.

🔭 Webb’s week — 13 and 16 Oct

The James Webb Space Telescope found an ancient galaxy cluster packed with dark matter, and a faraway planet whose atmosphere is surprisingly lopsided.

🧲 A third kind of magnetism — 18 Oct

Physicists reported new evidence for altermagnetism, a “hidden” third type of magnetism besides the two known for over a century.

🏅 Sports

🏎️ Formula 1 is going to Africa — 14 Oct

Formula 1 announced a new Grand Prix in Kigali, Rwanda, from the early 2030s — the first F1 race in Africa since South Africa in 1993.

💡 Rwanda is called the “Land of a Thousand Hills”.

⚾ Brewers reach the World Series — 15 Oct

Baseball’s Milwaukee Brewers beat the Los Angeles Dodgers 4–0 to reach the World Series for the first time since 1982.

🏉 A record run ends — 16 Oct

Canada beat England 24–21 in women’s rugby in Toronto, ending England’s 41-match unbeaten run — the longest in rugby union history.

🏒 Hat-trick history — 17 Oct

Ice hockey’s Florida Panthers became the first NHL team to have a natural hat trick (one player scoring three goals in a row) in three games running.

🥊 Dubois keeps his belt — 17 Oct

British boxer Daniel Dubois retained his WBO heavyweight world title.

🤓 Fun Facts & Trivia

🎩 Where “hat trick” comes from

The term began in cricket: in the 1850s a bowler who took three wickets with three balls was given a hat as a prize.

⏰ What is UTC?

UTC (Coordinated Universal Time) is the world’s standard clock time. Indian Standard Time is UTC + 5 hours 30 minutes.

1. Analyse why natural gas has emerged as a preferred energy alternative compared to petroleum and coal.
👀 Show model answer
1. Cleaner: burns with less smoke and lower emissions — eco-friendly. 2. Cheaper as petroleum prices rose. 3. Petroleum reserves are diminishing. 4. Versatile: used at home (LPG/PNG) and in transport (CNG). Coal is the dirtiest, so natural gas is a better bridge fuel until renewables take over.
2. Explain the meaning of "petroleum". How is it obtained and refined?
👀 Show model answer
petra (rock) + oleum (oil). It is an inflammable oil between sedimentary rock layers, formed from decomposed plants and animals. It is drilled from oil wells, sent by pipelines to refineries, where impurities are removed to give petrol, diesel, kerosene.
3. Give four uses of petroleum.
👀 Show model answer
Transport fuel (cars, trains, aircraft, ships); electric power generation; lubrication of machines; petrochemicals — plastics, polyester, paraffin wax.
4. Explain the greenhouse effect and global warming.
👀 Show model answer
The atmosphere absorbs part of the Sun's heat; Earth radiates heat as infrared rays, and greenhouse gases trap some — the greenhouse effect, keeping Earth warm. Human activities (burning fossil fuels, deforestation) increase these gases, trapping extra heat — global warming, melting ice caps and glaciers.
5. Name the major petroleum and natural gas producers of the world.
👀 Show model answer
Petroleum: Russia, USA, Saudi Arabia, Iran, Iraq, Kuwait, Canada, Mexico, China. Natural gas: USA, Russia, Iran, Saudi Arabia.
6. Discuss the harmful effects of global warming the world faces now.
👀 Show model answer
Melting polar ice and glaciers; rising sea levels flooding coasts; more heatwaves, droughts and extreme rain; damage to crops and water supply; loss of habitats for animals like polar bears and corals.
7. Suggest four ways to reduce our carbon footprint.
👀 Show model answer
Use renewable energy (solar, wind); save electricity and fuel; use public transport, cycle or walk; plant trees (reforestation); make smart choices — reduce, reuse, recycle.
8. Why is Mumbai High important to India?
👀 Show model answer
It is India's largest petroleum source, an offshore oilfield in the Arabian Sea, reducing the need to import oil.
9. Why were the 1950s–70s called the large-dam era? What changed in the 1990s?
👀 Show model answer
After independence, large multi-purpose dams were built for irrigation, industry and electricity — Nehru's "temples of modern India". In the 1990s, concern for the environment and climate shifted planning to single-purpose small hydropower projects in hilly states.
10. Compare petroleum and natural gas (three points).
👀 Show model answer
Form: liquid vs gas. Pollution: petroleum products give more smoke; gas burns cleaner. Cost: petroleum prices rose; gas is cheaper. Both are fossil fuels found together in sedimentary beds.
