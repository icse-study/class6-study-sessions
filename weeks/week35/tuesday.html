Intro to AI — How Computers “Learn”
Artificial Intelligence (AI) means computers doing tasks that normally need human intelligence. This week is discussion only — no coding.
🤖 AI you already use

YouTube and Netflix recommendations · voice assistants (Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant) · camera face filters · spam filters · map apps predicting traffic · chatbots · self-driving car features.

📚 How a computer “learns”

Normal programs follow rules a person wrote. Machine learning finds patterns in data instead. Show it thousands of labelled examples (“this is a cat”, “this is a dog”) — the training data — and it learns to sort new pictures. More varied, fair examples → better results. If the examples are one-sided, the AI becomes biased.

🧠 AI vs humans

AI is good at: huge amounts of data, speed, never getting tired.
Humans are good at: feelings, common sense, creativity, understanding situations, and judging right and wrong. AI can be confidently wrong — chatbots sometimes “hallucinate” false facts — so always check.

💬 Discussion — Should AI do homework?

If AI writes your answers, you don’t learn. A responsible way: use AI to explain an idea, then do the work yourself and check facts. Don’t share private information with AI apps.

🐱 Activity — Train a human classifier

Sort a pile of cat and dog pictures. Then talk about how you knew: ears, nose shape, size? That is exactly what a computer has to learn from examples.

✨ Fun facts
• The term “artificial intelligence” was coined in 1956 by John McCarthy for a workshop at Dartmouth College.
• In 1950 Alan Turing proposed the Turing test: can a machine chat so well that a person cannot tell it is a machine?
• In 1997 IBM’s Deep Blue computer beat world chess champion Garry Kasparov.
