🔁 Quick recap

When Granny Wanted a Lemon (R. K. Narayan) — Swami refuses Granny’s errand, throws down the coins and runs to cricket. Afterwards, “face to face with his soul”, he is crushed by guilt — until Granny says, “You did right in not going. Your mother had kept a dozen in the kitchen.”
The Mountain and the Squirrel (Emerson) — a proud mountain calls a squirrel “Little Prig”; the squirrel calmly answers that talents differ: “If I cannot carry forests on my back, / Neither can you crack a nut.”

🧩 Linking ideas

Both texts are about respect — for the old (Granny) and for the small (the squirrel). Both show that worth is not about power or size. Swami is quick-tempered and rude; the squirrel answers rudeness with calm reason — a lesson Swami could learn!
Compare Swami’s guilt with Ramchand’s respect for his parents (Ch 1) and with Allarakkhi’s regret (Ch 8).

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. How do you think old people feel when they are ignored by their children or grandchildren?
👀 Show model answer
They feel lonely, hurt and unimportant, like Granny calling out to people who “paid no heed to her”. They may also feel helpless when they are unwell. A little attention — sitting with them, listening, running an errand — can make them feel loved and valued.
2. What could Swaminathan learn from the squirrel in Emerson’s poem?
👀 Show model answer
Swaminathan lost his temper and spoke rudely when Granny asked him to sit down. The squirrel, though insulted, replied calmly, admitted the mountain’s size, and explained its point with reason and even a compliment. Swami could learn to stay patient, respect others, and answer with kindness rather than anger.
