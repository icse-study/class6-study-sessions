Compounds — and how to write their formulae
A compound forms when two or more elements combine chemically in a fixed proportion by mass. This week: properties of compounds, atoms vs molecules, atomicity, valency, the criss-cross method for writing formulae, and why elements are chosen for particular uses.

🧬 Compounds

Water, marble, glucose, sugar, salt, alcohol, CO2, CaO, NO2, ZnCl2.
• 2 or more elements in a fixed ratio — water is always H : O = 1 : 8 by mass
• Pure and homogeneous
• Properties are completely different from its elements
• Separated only by chemical methods (e.g. electrolysis), not filtration or boiling
• Energy is absorbed or given out when it forms · has a definite formula

🧂 Two famous examples

• Water: hydrogen burns, oxygen helps burning — yet water puts out fire.
• Common salt: sodium (a metal kept in kerosene because it reacts violently with air and water) + chlorine (a poisonous greenish-yellow gas) → a harmless white solid we eat.
Change the ratio and you get a different compound: H2O is water, H2O2 is hydrogen peroxide; NO2, N2O and NO are all different.

Mass ratios: H2O 1:8 · CO2 3:8 · CaO 5:2 · NaCl 23:35.5

🔢 Atomicity

Atomicity = number of atoms in one molecule of an element.
• Monoatomic: metals, metalloids, noble gases, carbon — Fe, Na, He, C
• Diatomic: H2, N2, O2, F2, Cl2, Br2, I2
• Triatomic: ozone O3
• Polyatomic: P4 (4 atoms), S8 (8 atoms)

💡 2H = two separate atoms · H2 = one molecule · 2O2 = two molecules.

🤝 Valency

Valency = combining capacity: the number of hydrogen atoms (or atoms of another element) one atom combines with.
H 1 · O 2 · N 3 · C 4 · Cl 1 · Na 1 · K 1 · Mg 2 · Ca 2 · Fe 2 · Zn 2 · S 2 · Al 3

✖️ Criss-cross method

1. Write the symbols side by side. 2. Write each valency under its symbol. 3. Swap them as subscripts. 4. Cancel if both are the same; don't write 1.
Na(1) Cl(1) → NaCl · Ca(2) Cl(1) → CaCl2 · Mg(2) O(2) → MgO (not Mg2O2) · Al(3) O(2) → Al2O3 · Na(1) O(2) → Na2O

📖 Reading a formula & uses

A formula tells you which elements are present, how many atoms of each, and their ratio. 2CO2 = two molecules = 2 C + 4 O. H2SO4 = 2 H, 1 S, 4 O.
Uses follow properties: gold/silver/platinum shine and don't tarnish → jewellery · copper/aluminium conduct, ductile → wires, utensils · iron strong → tools, machines · graphite marks paper → pencils, lubricants · argon/neon unreactive → bulbs · frying pan: steel body (conductor), wooden/plastic handle (insulator)

Brass = copper + zinc · Bronze = copper + tin — these are mixtures, not compounds.
✨ Fun facts
• Glucose is C6H12O6 and cane sugar (sucrose) is C12H22O11 — both are just carbon, hydrogen and oxygen!
• Water was thought to be an element for thousands of years, until it was split into hydrogen and oxygen.
• Silver is a better conductor than copper, but copper is used for wires because it is much cheaper.
• Hydrogen peroxide (H2O2), just one extra oxygen atom away from water, is used to clean wounds.

🎯 Must-remember
• Compound: fixed ratio, new properties, chemical separation only
• Water H:O = 1:8 by mass
• Monoatomic: metals, noble gases · Diatomic: H₂ N₂ O₂ F₂ Cl₂ Br₂ I₂ · O₃ · P₄ · S₈
• Valency: H1 O2 N3 C4 Na1 Cl1 Mg2 Ca2 Al3
• Criss-cross, then cancel; don't write 1
• 2CO₂ = 2 molecules = 2 C + 4 O
