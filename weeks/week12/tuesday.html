Transposition — Moving Terms Across the Equals Sign
Chapter 14 · Balancing written short. A term that crosses the equals sign changes its sign — plus becomes minus, and multiply becomes divide.
➗ The rule of transposition

Cross the sign, change the sign

A term moved from one side to the other changes: + ↔ − and × ↔ ÷.

x + 6 = 10 → x = 10 − 6 = 4
x − 6 = 10 → x = 10 + 6 = 16
7x = 21 → x = 21 ÷ 7 = 3
x/7 = 3 → x = 3 × 7 = 21
💡 Transposition is just balancing done in one step. If you ever doubt it, do the long version — add or subtract from both sides.

Letters to the left, numbers to the right

Collect every term containing the letter on one side and every plain number on the other, then simplify each side once.

6x − 5 = 2x + 11 → 6x − 2x = 11 + 5 → 4x = 16 → x = 4
➗ Brackets and fractions

Clear the brackets first

Expand using the distributive property, then transpose.

3(x − 2) = 12 → 3x − 6 = 12 → 3x = 18 → x = 6
💡 Multiply the number outside by every term inside. 3(x − 2) = 3x − 6, not 3x − 2.

Clear the fractions first

Multiply every term of the equation by the LCM of the denominators, then solve as usual.

x/2 + x/3 = 5 → multiply throughout by 6 → 3x + 2x = 30 → 5x = 30 → x = 6
➗ Habits that earn marks

One operation per line

Write each step on a new line, keeping the equals signs vertically aligned, and finish with a check.

4x + 3 = 19
4x = 19 − 3
4x = 16
x = 4   Check: 4(4) + 3 = 19 ✔

📜 The Story of Algebra · Part 14 of 16 — Galois, Abel, and an Equation Nobody Can Solve

The formula that does not exist

You know how to solve equations of degree 1. Degree 2, 3 and 4 all have formulas too. Then the trail stops. The Abel–Ruffini theorem proves that equations of degree 5 have no general formula using ordinary roots — and never will.

This is not a gap in our knowledge. It is a proved impossibility.
💡 Mathematics can prove that something cannot be done — one of its strangest and most powerful abilities.

Évariste Galois — twenty years old

Galois worked out why some equations can be solved by formula and others cannot, inventing what is now called Galois theory. He wrote much of it down in a rush the night before dying in a duel, aged 20.

Niels Henrik Abel reached the degree-5 result independently, and died at 26. Emmy Noether later reshaped the whole subject.
💡 Galois theory now underpins error-correcting codes and cryptography — written by a twenty-year-old in one night.

Transposition, in that light

Every time you move a term across the equals sign and flip its sign, you are using the restoring that al-jabr was named for — and the balance rules Galois eventually explained at the deepest level.

3x + 7 = x + 19 → 3x − x = 19 − 7 → 2x = 12 → x = 6

1️⃣ Why measure — and why standard units?

Measurement

Measurement is comparing an unknown quantity with a known standard quantity (a unit). The result is a number + a unit: '5' means nothing, '5 metres' is a measurement.

💡 Hand-spans and foot-lengths differ from person to person. A standard unit means the same everywhere in the world.

Derived units — built from fundamental ones

area → m² · volume → m³ · speed → m/s · density → kg/m³ · force → newton (N)

💡 The kilogram is the only fundamental S.I. unit whose name already has a prefix (kilo-).

Writing units the S.I. way

✓ 10 kg   ✗ 10 kgs, 10 kg., 10 Kg, 10kg
• Symbols are never made plural and take no full stop.
• Leave a space between number and symbol.
• Unit names named after people are written small (newton, kelvin) but their symbols are capital (N, K).

💡 Big unit → small unit: multiply. Small unit → big unit: divide.
5️⃣ Measuring length correctly

Parallax error

If your eye is not vertically above the mark, the reading looks shifted. Always keep your eye straight above the mark.

Measuring something very thin

Stack many of them and divide. 20 coins measure 3.0 cm → one coin = 3.0 ÷ 20 = 0.15 cm = 1.5 mm.

6️⃣ Time

The second

A mean solar day = 24 × 60 × 60 = 86 400 s. So one second = 1/86 400 of a mean solar day.

1 minute = 60 s · 1 hour = 3600 s · a stopwatch measures short time intervals, like a race.
7️⃣ Rest and motion

Rest or motion — always compared with the surroundings

Rest: position doesn't change with time. Motion: position changes with time.

💡 A passenger sitting in a moving bus is at rest compared with the bus, but in motion compared with the trees outside.
