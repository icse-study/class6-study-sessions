🌍 Geography · Ch 6 North America · Part 2
North America — Location, Boundaries & Countries
The third largest continent: where exactly is it, what surrounds it, how did it get its name, and which countries and capitals does it hold?

🧭 Naming the continent

Christopher Columbus (Italian explorer) reached it in 1492 while looking for a sea route to India. He mistook it for India and called the people Indians and the land Indies — hence the Caribbean islands became the West Indies. The continent was later named after Amerigo Vespucci, who landed a few years after Columbus.

📍 Location

Entirely in the Western and Northern Hemispheres. Latitude 7°N to 83°N; longitude 59°W to 180°W. Area about 9,355,000 sq miles. Lies entirely north of the Equator, mostly between the Arctic Circle and the Tropic of Cancer.

🌊 Boundaries

• North: Arctic Ocean
• South: Gulf of Mexico and Caribbean Sea
• East: Atlantic Ocean (separates it from Europe)
• West: Pacific Ocean (separates it from Asia)
• Bering Strait (north-west) separates it from Asia.
• Isthmus of Panama links it to South America.

📖 Strait vs isthmus

Strait: a narrow stretch of sea linking two large water bodies and separating two land masses (e.g. Bering Strait).
Isthmus: a narrow strip of land with sea on both sides, linking two larger land masses (e.g. Isthmus of Panama).

💡 Strait = water between lands; Isthmus = land between waters.

🏛️ Big countries & capitals

Canada — Ottawa · USA — Washington, D.C. · Mexico — Mexico City.

🌴 Central America

Belize — Belmopan · Guatemala — Guatemala City · Honduras — Tegucigalpa · El Salvador — San Salvador · Nicaragua — Managua · Costa Rica — San José · Panama — Panama City.

🏝️ Caribbean nations

Cuba — Havana · Jamaica — Kingston · Haiti — Port-au-Prince · Dominican Republic — Santo Domingo · Bahamas — Nassau · Barbados — Bridgetown · Grenada — St. George's · Antigua & Barbuda — St. John's · Dominica — Roseau · St Kitts & Nevis — Basseterre · St Lucia — Castries · St Vincent & the Grenadines — Kingstown.

🐻 Natural wonders

North America has Niagara Falls, the Old Faithful Geyser, giant redwood trees, the bison and the grizzly bear. It has more than 20 countries including the Caribbean.

🎯 Must-remember facts
• 3rd largest continent · 7°N–83°N · 59°W–180°W
• Arctic (N), Gulf of Mexico & Caribbean (S), Atlantic (E), Pacific (W)
• Bering Strait ↔ Asia · Isthmus of Panama ↔ South America
• Columbus 1492 → "West Indies" · named after Amerigo Vespucci
• Ottawa · Washington, D.C. · Mexico City · Havana · Kingston

✍️ English Grammar · Ch 7–8 Framing Questions & Question Tags (second pass)
Ask it right — question words, word order and tricky tags
Second pass on Chapters 7 and 8. You will build questions from answers (Exercise B and Revision Time F), learn when a question needs no do/did, and master the special tags: aren't I?, shall we?, will you? and tags after nothing / everyone / hardly.

❓ Pick the right question word

• What — thing / information · Which — choice from a set
• Who — person (subject) · Whom — person (object, formal) · Whose — owner
• Where — place · When — time · Why — reason
• How + much (price/amount), many (number), long (time/length), far (distance), often (frequency), old (age)

🔁 Word order: helper before subject

Statement: They have hidden the treasure.
Question: Where have they hidden the treasure?
No helper? Add do / does / did and use the base verb: Smartphones require a nano-SIM. → What do smartphones require?

💡 After did, the verb goes back to its base form: did you find (not found).

🎯 Subject questions need no 'do'

If who or what is the subject, keep statement order:
• Who broke the vase? (not Who did break)
• What happened at school?
Compare an object question: Whom did you meet? / Who did you meet?

🏷️ Tag basics (recap)

Positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag. Same helper, same tense, pronoun subject.
• They have four cars, don't they? (have = main verb → do)
• We have finished the work, haven't we? (have = helper)
• You read the paper today, didn't you? (past tense)

✨ Special tags

• I am late, aren't I? (never amn't I)
• Let's go, shall we?
• Open the window, will you? (imperative)
• Everyone / Somebody…, don't they? · Nothing…, is it?
• There is a problem, isn't there? · This is yours, isn't it? · Those are yours, aren't they?
• never, hardly, seldom, rarely, nobody make the statement negative → positive tag: He seldom comes, does he?

💡 The Indian 'isn't it?' everywhere is wrong: She is your sister, isn't she?

📝 From answer to question (your book)

• We have sent him three emails. → How many emails have we sent him?
• My parents will be back by 10 p.m. → When will your parents be back?
• That sports car belongs to my uncle. → Whom does that sports car belong to?
• I was going to get the medicine from the chemist's. → Where were you going to get the medicine from?

✨ Fun facts
• Amn't I? is still used in Ireland and Scotland, but standard English uses aren't I?
• Many languages use one fixed tag for everything — Hindi na?, French n'est-ce pas? English is unusual because the tag changes with every sentence.
• The tone of your voice changes a tag's meaning: falling tone = 'I'm sure, just agree'; rising tone = 'I really want to know'.
• 'Who' is slowly replacing 'whom' in everyday speech, but whom is still correct after a preposition: To whom did you write?

👀 Show answeraren't I?

👀 Show answerdid they? — nobody is negative, so the tag is positive.

🎯 Must-remember rules
• Helper before subject; no helper → do/does/did + base verb
• Who/What as subject → no do/did
• How much = amount/price · How many = number · How far = distance · How long = time
• I am… aren't I? · Let's… shall we? · Imperative… will you?
• never/hardly/seldom/nobody/nothing → positive tag
• have as main verb → don't/doesn't/didn't tag
