⚗️ Chemistry · Week 18 · Ch 3 Matter (Part 2)
Heat changes matter — three ways
Heat can do three things to matter: change its state, make it expand, or cause a chemical change. This week: the six change-of-state words, melting and boiling points, sublimation, thermal expansion, and why a burning candle gets shorter.

🔁 Interconversion of states

The process by which matter changes from one state to another and back without any change in chemical composition. It is a physical, reversible change. Two things cause it: changing temperature and applying pressure.
LPG and hospital oxygen are stored as liquids under high pressure.

🌡️ The six words

• Melting/fusion: solid → liquid on heating
• Freezing/solidification: liquid → solid on cooling
• Vaporisation/boiling: liquid → vapour on heating
• Condensation/liquefaction: gas → liquid on cooling
• Evaporation: liquid → vapour below boiling point, even at room temperature
• Sublimation: solid → vapour directly (camphor, iodine, naphthalene, dry ice)

💡 Evaporation happens at any temperature; boiling only at the boiling point.

📍 Fixed points

• Melting point = freezing point (ice/water: 0 °C)
• Boiling point = condensation point (water/steam: 100 °C at normal pressure)
• The temperature stays constant while the change is going on.
• A pure substance has fixed melting and boiling points — impurities change them, so they test purity.

💨 Gas vs vapour

• Gas: a substance that is gaseous at normal conditions (oxygen, hydrogen, nitrogen, CO2).
• Vapour: the gaseous form of a substance that is normally a liquid or solid (water vapour, iodine vapour).
Not every substance changes state on heating: paper and sugar burn or char instead.

📏 Thermal expansion

All three states expand on heating and contract on cooling — a physical, reversible change.
• Ball and ring: a heated ball no longer passes through the ring.
• Coloured water in a capillary tube (thin glass tube) rises when the flask is warmed.
• A thermometer works because mercury expands.
Different substances expand by different amounts.

🕯️ Chemical change

A permanent change in which new substances with different properties form; usually irreversible. Burning paper → ash; burning wood; cooking food.
Candle: melting wax = physical change. Burning wax vapour → carbon dioxide + water vapour = chemical change. That is why the candle gets smaller.

💡 A candle shows BOTH kinds of change at once.
✨ Fun facts
• Dry ice (solid CO2) sublimes at −78.5 °C — it makes the spooky 'fog' on stage.
• On Mount Everest water boils at about 70 °C because air pressure is low — tea there is never really hot.
• Railway tracks have small gaps between rails so they can expand in summer without bending.
• Naphthalene balls in wardrobes get smaller and smaller because they sublime into vapour.

🎯 Must-remember
• Melting = fusion · Freezing = solidification · Condensation = liquefaction
• Evaporation: below BP, any temperature · Boiling: at BP
• MP = FP (0 °C) · BP = condensation point (100 °C)
• Sublimation: camphor, iodine, naphthalene, dry ice
• Heat → change of state, expansion, chemical change
• Chemical change: new substance, permanent
