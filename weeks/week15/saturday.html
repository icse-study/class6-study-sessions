Revision 4 — Algebra, Equations, Data & Circles
Weeks 8–14 revision, and the year's grand mix: expressions, linear equations, data handling and Chapter 17 circles.
➗ The ideas

Algebraic expressions

A term is a single part of an expression; like terms have exactly the same letters with the same powers, and only like terms can be added.

3x + 5x = 8x, but 3x + 5y cannot be combined.
Coefficient of x in 7x is 7 · Degree of 4x³ is 3
Monomial (1 term) · Binomial (2) · Trinomial (3)
💡 Removing a bracket with a minus in front flips every sign inside: 5 − (x − 3) = 5 − x + 3.

Linear equations

Keep the equation balanced: whatever you do to one side, do to the other. Transposing a term across the = sign changes its sign.

2x + 5 = 13 → 2x = 8 → x = 4
3(x + 2) = 15 → x + 2 = 5 → x = 3
💡 Always check by putting your answer back into the original equation.

Data handling

Mean = sum ÷ number of values. Mode = the value that appears most often. Range = highest − lowest. Tally marks group in fives, with the fifth drawn across.

Mean of 4, 7, 9, 12 = 32 ÷ 4 = 8

Circles — the chapter just finished

Diameter = 2 × radius, and it is the longest chord
Chord = radius → 60° at the centre · Angle in a semi-circle = 90°
Sector = 2 radii + arc · Segment = chord + arc · Quadrant = 90°
Major segment contains the centre
🎉 Fun facts & trivia

The word algebra comes from al-jabr in the title of a book written in Baghdad around 820 CE by al-Khwarizmi. His own name, worn down over centuries, gave us the word algorithm.

The equals sign = was invented in 1557 by Robert Recorde, who chose two parallel lines because, he wrote, 'no two things can be more equal'.

Averages can mislead: if nine people earn ₹10,000 and one earns ₹10 lakh, the mean is ₹1,09,000 — a figure nobody actually earns. That is why the mode and median matter too.

📜 The Year in Numbers · Part 4 of 4 — Why Revision Works

In the 1880s the German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus tested his own memory for months and drew what he called the forgetting curve: most of what you learn today disappears within days, unless you meet it again.

He also found the cure. Each time you recall something — not re-read it, but actually pull it out of your memory — the curve flattens. After three or four spaced recalls, the material stops fading.

That is exactly what this week is for. Chapter 1 was taught in June; you are answering questions on it in September. Every question you get right today buys that idea several more months of life.

5️⃣ Decision chart — which method?

Ask these questions in order

❓ Is one part magnetic? → magnet
❓ Does one part sublime? → sublimation
❓ Are the pieces big and few? → hand-picking
❓ Different sizes? → sieving · Different weights? → winnowing
❓ Solid doesn't dissolve in the liquid? → sedimentation + decantation, or filtration
❓ Solid dissolves? → evaporation (or distillation to keep the liquid)
❓ Two liquids in layers? → separating funnel
