Circles — Chord, Secant & Tangent
Chapter 17 · Sections 17.4–17.5. Lines that cut a circle, lines that touch it, and the one chord that beats all the others.
➗ The ideas

Chord

A chord is a straight line joining any two points on the circumference.

A chord does not have to pass through the centre — but if it does, it is also a diameter.

1. A chord through the centre is a diameter.
2. The diameter is the longest chord of a circle.
💡 Every diameter is a chord, but most chords are not diameters.

Secant

A straight line that cuts the circle at two distinct points is a secant.

A secant is a full line that keeps going on both sides; the piece of it inside the circle is a chord.

Line AB meets the circle at M and N → AB is a secant, MN is a chord.

Tangent

A straight line that touches the circle at exactly one point is a tangent. That single meeting point is the point of contact.

A line at a distance exactly equal to the radius from the centre must be a tangent.
💡 From the centre: distance < radius → the line cuts (secant) · distance = radius → it touches (tangent) · distance > radius → it misses the circle.
🎉 Fun facts & trivia

The word tangent comes from the Latin tangere, 'to touch' — the same root as 'tangible'. A secant comes from secare, 'to cut', which also gives us 'scissors' and 'section'.

A tyre touching the road is a tangent in real life: at any instant the wheel meets the ground at essentially one point.

Longest chord challenge: slide a chord of a circle towards the centre and it grows; the moment it passes through the centre it is a diameter and can grow no more.

📜 Part 2 of 12 — Drawing Straight Lines With a Circle

Ancient builders had no laser levels. To mark a perfect right angle they used a rope with 12 equally spaced knots, forming a 3–4–5 triangle. But to mark a perfect circle they needed only a peg, a rope and a stick — one fixed point, one fixed distance.

That is why circles appear in the oldest surviving architecture, from stone circles to temple plans. The compass used in your geometry box is the same idea in metal: the sharp point is the centre and the pencil traces the circumference.

1️⃣ Area — the surface covered
ShapeFormula
rectanglelength × breadth
squareside × side = l²
triangle½ × base × height
circleπ r² (take π = 22/7)

Units of area

S.I. unit: m². Also cm², km².
1 are = 100 m² · 1 hectare = 100 are = 10 000 m² · 1 km² = 10 00 000 m²

💡 Area is always in square units. 2πr is the circumference (a length), not the area.

Area of an irregular shape — like a leaf

Trace it on graph paper. Count full squares and squares that are half or more; ignore squares less than half.

32 full + 11 half-or-more = 43 cm² (the 9 small bits are ignored).

🧮 Worked examples

Park 50 m × 30 m → 1500 m²
Triangle base 12 cm, height 7 cm → ½ × 12 × 7 = 42 cm²
Circle r = 7 cm → 22/7 × 7 × 7 = 154 cm²

2️⃣ Volume — the space occupied

Cuboid

Volume = length × breadth × height. S.I. unit: m³.

2 cm × 3 cm × 4 cm = 24 cm³ · Cube of side 3 cm = 3³ = 27 cm³

Liquids

1 litre (L) = 1000 millilitres (mL) · 1 mL = 1 cm³. Measured with a measuring cylinder.

3️⃣ Density — how tightly packed

Density = Mass ÷ Volume

Units: g/cm³ or kg/m³. Water has a density of 1 g/cm³.

50 g, 10 cm³ → 50 ÷ 10 = 5 g/cm³
160 g, 200 mL → 160 ÷ 200 = 0.8 g/mL → it floats on water
💡 Less dense than water → floats. More dense → sinks. Divide mass BY volume, never the other way.
4️⃣ Speed — how fast

Speed = Distance ÷ Time

Units: m/s (S.I.) or km/h.

120 km in 2 h → 120 ÷ 2 = 60 km/h
100 m in 20 s → 5 m/s
5️⃣ Heat and temperature

Not the same thing!

Heat is a form of energy (unit: joule). Temperature tells how hot or cold a body is (unit: kelvin, or °C).
Heat always flows from a higher temperature to a lower temperature.

CelsiusFahrenheitKelvin
Ice melts0 °C32 °F273 K
Human body37 °C98.6 °F310 K
Water boils100 °C212 °F373 K

Handy rule

Kelvin = Celsius + 273

6️⃣ Thermometers
Clinical thermometerLaboratory thermometer
Used forbody temperaturelab experiments
Range35 °C to 42 °C−10 °C to 110 °C
Kink (constriction)yes — stops the mercury falling backno
Before usejerk it to bring mercury downno jerking needed

Why not measure boiling water with a clinical thermometer?

Its scale only goes up to 42 °C. At 100 °C the mercury would expand past the end and the thermometer could burst.

🎯 Fun Trivia
• The Dead Sea is so salty (dense) that people float on it easily while reading a newspaper.
• Gold is about 19 times denser than water — a litre bottle of gold would weigh about 19 kg!
• The coldest possible temperature is 0 K (−273 °C), called absolute zero. Nothing can be colder.
