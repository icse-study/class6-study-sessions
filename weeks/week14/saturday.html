➗ The ideas

Reading a figure

Exam figures usually show one circle with several lines through it. Work through them in this order:
1. Find O, the centre.
2. Any line from O to the circle → radius.
3. Any line through O with both ends on the circle → diameter.
4. Ends on the circle but not through O → chord.
5. Crosses the circle twice and continues → secant. Touches once → tangent.

💡 Label every line before you answer anything. Most mistakes are naming mistakes, not calculation mistakes.

Assertion and reason questions

You are given a statement (A) and a reason (R), and asked which are true. Test them separately first, then ask whether R actually explains A.

A: A line meeting a circle at a distance equal to the radius is a tangent. R: Such a line touches the circumference at one point.
Both are true, and R explains A.

Quick facts that answer whole questions

• Longest chord = diameter.
• Chord through the centre = diameter.
• Chord = radius → 60° at the centre.
• Angle in a semi-circle = 90°.
• Major segment contains the centre.
• Quadrant = 90° sector.

🎉 Fun facts & trivia

A circle has infinite lines of symmetry — every single diameter is one. A square has 4, an equilateral triangle 3, and a scalene triangle none at all.

Round coins can be spent by feel, but Britain's 20p and 50p coins are seven-sided. They still roll in vending machines because they are curved shapes of constant width, just like a circle.

The Earth's equator is a circle roughly 40,075 km round. The metre was originally defined so that a quarter of the meridian through Paris would be exactly 10,000 km.

📜 Part 8 of 12 — Squaring the Circle

For more than 2,000 years mathematicians tried to build a square with exactly the same area as a given circle, using only a compass and an unmarked ruler. It became the most famous unsolved puzzle in geometry, and the phrase 'squaring the circle' passed into everyday English for an impossible task.

In 1882, Ferdinand von Lindemann finally proved it can never be done — because π is a transcendental number, a kind that no compass-and-ruler construction can ever produce.

Two thousand years of failure ended not with a construction, but with a proof that no construction exists.

Water & air — the two substances of life

1️⃣ Water — the facts

A compound

Formula H₂O: 2 hydrogen atoms + 1 oxygen atom, always 1 : 8 by mass. Boils at 100 °C, freezes at 0 °C.

💡 Hydrogen burns, oxygen helps burning — but water puts out fire! A compound has new properties.

3️⃣ The water cycle

Water going round and round

Evaporation (sea, lakes, plants) → condensation (clouds) → precipitation (rain, snow, hail) → infiltration (soaks into the ground) and run-off back to the sea.

💡 The water you drink today may once have been drunk by a dinosaur!
4️⃣ Saving water

Only a tiny part is usable

About 97% of Earth's water is salty sea water. Most fresh water is locked in glaciers and ice caps.

Rainwater harvesting · fix dripping taps · use a bucket instead of a shower · reuse RO waste water for plants
5️⃣ Air — a mixture

Why air is a mixture

Its gases are not chemically combined, their amounts vary from place to place, each gas keeps its properties, and air has no formula.

Air has mass and exerts pressure

A balloon full of air weighs more than an empty one. The air around us presses on everything — atmospheric pressure.

6️⃣ Uses of air's gases

7️⃣ Air pollution

What pollutes air

Burning fossil fuels (coal, petrol, diesel) is the chief cause. Pollutants: smoke, dust, carbon monoxide, sulphur dioxide.

💡 Carbon dioxide and water vapour trap heat — the greenhouse effect. Too much CO₂ warms the planet.

🎯 Fun Trivia
• A person can live weeks without food, but only a few days without water.
• Mount Everest's summit has only about one-third of the air pressure at sea level — climbers carry oxygen.
• Venus has so much carbon dioxide that its greenhouse effect makes it hotter than Mercury, even though Mercury is closer to the Sun.
