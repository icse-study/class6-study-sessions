Direct or inverse?

Direct proportion — more of one means more of the other (more kilos of sugar, more rupees).
Inverse proportion — more of one means less of the other (more workers, fewer days).

Inverse trick: the product stays fixed. 5 workers × 10 days = 50 worker-days, so 2 days needs 50 ÷ 2 = 25 workers.
💡 Profit % is always calculated on the cost price, never on the selling price.

Sharing in a given ratio

Add the parts, divide the total by that, then multiply back.

Split 96 in the ratio 5 : 7. Parts = 5 + 7 = 12. One part = 96 ÷ 12 = 8. So the numbers are 5 × 8 = 40 and 7 × 8 = 56 (and 40 + 56 = 96 ✓).
⚗️ Chemistry — Air and Atmosphere

Air is a mixture, not a compound

Air is a mixture of gases — its parts keep their own properties and can be separated physically. The blanket of air around the Earth is the atmosphere.

GasApprox. share by volumeMain use
Nitrogen78%Fertilisers; dilutes oxygen; keeps packed food from spoiling
Oxygen21%Respiration and combustion
Argon0.93%Filling electric bulbs
Carbon dioxide0.03–0.04%Photosynthesis; fire extinguishers; fizzy drinks
Water vapour & tracesvariesClouds, rain, humidity

Properties of air

Air has mass (an inflated balloon weighs more than a limp one), occupies space (an upturned glass pushed into water stays dry inside) and exerts pressure in all directions — atmospheric pressure.

💡 Oxygen supports burning but does not burn itself. Nitrogen neither burns nor supports burning — that is why it makes air safe to breathe.

Air pollution and the greenhouse effect

Air pollution is the addition of harmful substances — smoke, dust, sulphur dioxide, carbon monoxide — mostly from burning fossil fuels and from vehicles and factories.

The greenhouse effect: carbon dioxide and water vapour trap the Earth's heat. Some of it keeps us warm; too much of it causes global warming.

🇫🇷 French — Les Prépositions

Qu'est-ce qu'une préposition ?

A preposition links a noun to the rest of the sentence and shows place, direction, time or manner.

PrépositionMeaningExemple
suronLe livre est sur la table.
sousunderLe chat est sous le lit.
dansin / insideLes stylos sont dans la boîte.
devantin front ofIl est devant la porte.
derrièrebehindLe jardin est derrière la maison.
à côté denext toÀ côté de l'école.
chezat the home ofJe suis chez moi.

Places: à / en / au / aux

à + a city — à Paris, à Bangalore
en + a feminine country — en France, en Inde
au + a masculine country — au Canada, au Japon
aux + a plural country — aux États-Unis

💡 Most countries ending in -e are feminine and take en. (Le Mexique is the famous exception.)

Time: à / en / le

à + a clock time — à six heures
en + a month, year or season — en août, en 2026, en été
le + a day, to mean 'every' — le lundi = on Mondays

Exception to remember: au printemps (in spring), but en été, en automne, en hiver.
