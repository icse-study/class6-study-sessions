➗ The ideas

The relations to know by heart

These are the only numerical relations in the chapter — but they turn up in every question.

Diameter = 2 × Radius
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2
Longest chord = the diameter
A diameter splits the circle into 2 semi-circles
💡 If a question gives you a diameter, halve it before doing anything else.

Name that line

Radius — centre to a point on the circle.
Diameter — through the centre, both ends on the circle.
Chord — joins two points on the circle.
Secant — a line cutting the circle at two points.
Tangent — a line touching the circle at one point.

Distance of a line from the centre: less than r → secant · equal to r → tangent · more than r → misses the circle.

Mensuration formulas you already know

Circles arrive late in the year, so these earlier formulas still need to stay sharp.

Area of a rectangle = l × b · Perimeter = 2(l + b)
Area of a square = side × side · Perimeter = 4 × side
Area of a triangle = ½ × base × height
Area of a parallelogram = base × height
🎉 Fun facts & trivia

The Greek letter π was popularised by William Jones in 1706, from the Greek word periphereia, meaning circumference. Before that, mathematicians wrote it out as a whole phrase.

A circle can be thought of as a regular polygon with infinitely many, infinitely short sides. Archimedes used that idea to squeeze π between 3¹⁰⁄₇₁ and 3¹⁄₇ — more than 2,200 years ago.

The word radius is Latin for the spoke of a wheel. The plural is radii, not 'radiuses'.

📜 Part 3 of 12 — Archimedes and the Sand

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BCE) wanted to know how the circumference of a circle compares with its diameter. He could not measure a curve directly, so he trapped it: he drew a polygon inside the circle and another outside it, then doubled the number of sides again and again until he reached 96 sides.

The circle's boundary had to lie between the two polygons, which gave him π to two decimal places. Legend says he was still drawing circles in the sand when a soldier interrupted him — and that his last words were, 'Do not disturb my circles.'

La famille de Manuel

The father, Jérôme Dupont, is a well-known ingénieur civil. He développe des ponts, des routes et des bâtiments.
The mother, Sophie, is a médecin. She soigne et guérit des gens.
Manuel dreams of being an architecte — he loves design and construction.
His sister wants to be a journaliste; his brother dreams of being a médecin.
His uncle is a famous scientifique.
His aunt Marie is not a secretary — elle est avocate, known for her work on social justice.

Patterns that make the feminine easy

-eur → -euse: serveur → serveuse, vendeur → vendeuse, coiffeur → coiffeuse
-teur → -trice: acteur → actrice, directeur → directrice, instituteur → institutrice
-ier / -er → -ière / -ère: banquier → banquière, infirmier → infirmière, boulanger → boulangère
-ien → -ienne: pharmacien → pharmacienne, musicien → musicienne, informaticien → informaticienne
+ e: avocat → avocate, enseignant → enseignante

💡 In the textbook, * = same form for both (la journaliste, la pilote). ** = used only in the masculine (écrivain, ingénieur, médecin).

🎯 Fun Trivia
In French you say « Il est médecin » — with no un before the job. English says he is a doctor, French doesn't. Every line in the textbook table follows this: Elle est avocate, Il est pilote.

A French pharmacie is easy to spot: look for a glowing green cross outside.

Glass and safety

Glass is transparent and doesn't react. Pyrex hardly expands, so it doesn't crack. Never taste chemicals; if one spills on skin, wash with cold running water and tell your teacher. Robert Boyle first recorded apparatus, method and results.

Physical vs chemical change

Physical — reversible, no new substance (melting ice). Chemical — permanent, new substance (burning paper). A burning candle shows both: wax melts (physical) and burns (chemical). The ball-and-ring experiment shows solids expand on heating.

🎲 Fun Corner — Chemistry true or false
1. A wire gauze makes the flame hotter.
What does it spread?
👀 Show answer
False — it spreads heat evenly.
2. Brass is a compound.
Copper + zinc, any amount.
👀 Show answer
False — it's a mixture (an alloy).
3. Bromine is a liquid metal.
Metal or non-metal?
👀 Show answer
False — bromine is a liquid NON-metal; mercury is the liquid metal.
4. Burning paper is a chemical change.
Can you get the paper back?
👀 Show answer
True.
5. The symbol for potassium is P.
Latin: Kalium.
👀 Show answer
False — it is K. P is phosphorus.
6. Naphthalene balls get smaller because they melt.
They never become liquid.
👀 Show answer
False — they sublime.
🎯 Fun Trivia
• The word chemistry comes from alchemy — ancient alchemists tried (and failed) to turn lead into gold.
• There are more atoms in a glass of water than there are glasses of water in all the oceans!
• The newest elements, discovered in the 2010s, include nihonium (named after Japan) and tennessine (after Tennessee, USA).
