🌍 Geography · Ch 4 Agriculture · Part 1
Agriculture — Climate, Soil & Topography
Why does rice grow in Assam but wheat in Punjab? Why is tea planted on slopes? This week you learn the three geographical conditions that decide which crop grows where: climate, soil and topography.

🌾 What is agriculture?

The word comes from Latin ager (field) + cultura (art of cultivation). Agriculture is the science of cultivating land to grow crops and rearing livestock — sheep, goats, cows, buffaloes, ducks, chickens and even fish.

💡 Agriculture covers animals and fish too — not only crops.

🏹 From nomads to farmers

Early humans were nomads who hunted and gathered food in forests. Once they learnt to domesticate animals and grow crops, they began a settled lifestyle. Their first tools were the hoe and sickle. A growing population later pushed farmers to use tractors, combine harvesters, chemical fertilisers, pesticides and irrigation.

☀️ Condition 1 — Climate

Temperature and rainfall decide crop growth.
• Hot and humid lands: cotton, sugar cane, jute, rice.
• Cooler and drier places: wheat, corn, mustard.
• Very dry or very cold places are unsuitable — yet scientists have developed quick-growing seeds so wheat and vegetables can grow in greenhouses in the Arctic.

💡 A greenhouse has glass walls and roof that trap heat, so plants survive harsh cold.

🟫 Condition 2 — Soil

• Alluvial soil of river plains is extremely fertile — good for all crops.
• Chernozem of Russia is extremely fertile because it is rich in humus.
• Black soil of the Deccan Plateau → cotton.
• Terra roxa of Brazil → coffee.
• Laterite soil → tea.

💡 Humus = dead and decomposed remains of plants and animals that add nutrients.

⛰️ Condition 3 — Topography

Topography means relief features. Level land helps ploughing, irrigating and harvesting, so the world's major farming areas are in the plains. Tea and coffee grow better on hill slopes because they cannot withstand stagnant water. Water-loving crops like rice and jute suit the lowlands.

🪜 Terrace cultivation

On mountain slopes farmers cut broad steps to make flat land, so lowland crops can be grown on highlands. This is terrace cultivation. The steps also slow running water and reduce soil erosion.

💧 Irrigation

Irrigation is the supply of water to crops through pipes, ditches, canals, etc., over and above the water received from rain. It lets farmers grow crops even in the dry season.

🎯 Must-remember facts
• ager = field · cultura = cultivation
• Hot + humid → rice, jute, cotton, sugar cane
• Cool + dry → wheat, corn, mustard
• Chernozem (Russia) = humus-rich · Terra roxa (Brazil) = coffee · Black soil (Deccan) = cotton · Laterite = tea
• Tea & coffee on slopes — no stagnant water
• Terrace = steps cut on slopes

✍️ English Grammar · Ch 1 Articles
Articles — a, an, the (and when to use none)
Three tiny words — a, an, the — are the most used words in English. The alone is about 1 in every 16 words you read! This week you learn the sound rule, when the is a must, and the tricky places where no article is used at all.

🔤 A or an? Listen, don't look

A goes before a consonant sound; an before a vowel sound. The first letter does not decide — the first sound does.
• an hour, an honest man, an heiress, an MLA (silent h / letter M says "em")
• a European, a university, a uniform, a union, a unique idea, a one-rupee coin (they start with a "yoo" or "wun" sound)

💡 Say it aloud. If your mouth starts with a vowel sound, use an.

📦 Countable vs uncountable

A/an only go with singular countable nouns: a book, an orange, a year. They cannot go before uncountable nouns like ink, paper, sugar, tea, advice. Instead say: a bottle of ink, a piece of paper, a cup of tea.

✗ a sugar   ✓ a bag of sugar

👉 Uses of THE

• Something already mentioned: I saw a boy and a girl… The boy was sitting behind the girl.
• A particular thing: Give me the blue cup.
• A whole class: The cow is a useful animal.
• Superlatives: the highest mountain.
• One-of-a-kind things: the sun, the moon, the earth.
• Rivers, seas, oceans, canals, mountain ranges: the Ganga, the Suez Canal, the Himalayas.
• Some countries: the USA, the Netherlands, the Maldives.
• Holy books & newspapers: the Mahabharata, the Times of India.

🎩 Adjective + THE = a whole group

The rich ought to help the poor. The wounded were taken to hospital. Here an adjective becomes a plural noun — so the verb is plural too.

💡 The poor ARE suffering (not is).

👷 Professions and "kind of person" words

My brother is an engineer (not "is engineer"). Beware, he is a liar. Nouns like hero, genius, thief, liar, tool take a/an when they tell us what kind of person someone is.

❄️ such a … / so … a

Such comes before the article: such a cold winter, such an accident.
So pushes the article after the adjective: so cold a winter (not "a so cold winter").

What a pity!   She has a perfect knowledge of German.

📈 The more … the merrier

When two things grow together, use the with both comparatives: The more, the merrier. The sooner, the better.

🚫 When NOT to use THE

• Travel: by land, by sea, by air, by train, by bus.
• Places used for their purpose: go to school, church, market, work, prison, bed (but: I went to the hospital to visit a friend).
• all day, all night (not "all the day").
• Games: play chess, football (but play the piano, the violin).
• Meals: Breakfast is ready.
• Languages: English is spoken everywhere (but the English = the people).
• General plurals & materials: Apples are good. Gold is precious.

💡 Particular → the. General → nothing. Apples are good, but the apples you bought were sour.
✨ Fun facts
• The is the most common word in written English — about 6% of all words.
• Old English had no single 'the'. It had around a dozen forms, like se, seo, þæt, changing with gender and case!
• An is actually older than a. It comes from the word one; the n dropped before consonants to make speaking easier.
• Signs like 'Ye Olde Shoppe' are really 'The Old Shop'. The 'Y' was a printer's stand-in for the old letter þ (thorn) = th.
• Hindi, Kannada, Russian and Japanese have no articles at all, which is why learners of English find a/an/the so tricky.

👀 Show answerThe h in hour is silent, so the word begins with a vowel sound. House begins with a clear h sound.

👀 Show answera one-way street — 'one' begins with a 'w' sound.

Q. Which famous saying uses 'the' twice for things that grow together?
👀 Show answerThe more, the merrier.
🎯 Must-remember rules
• Sound decides a/an: an hour, an honest, a European, a university, a one-eyed man
• a/an only with singular countable nouns
• the + rivers, seas, oceans, mountain ranges, superlatives, unique things
• No article: by bus, go to school, all day, play chess, breakfast, English (language)
• such a cold day = so cold a day
• the rich / the poor = plural, take plural verb
