🔬 Science & Space

🔭 Roman Space Telescope launched — 30 Aug

NASA launched the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope on a SpaceX Falcon Heavy rocket from Kennedy Space Center, Florida. It will study dark energy, dark matter and exoplanets (planets around other stars). It is on a three-month, million-mile journey to its final orbit, and the rocket's two side boosters flew back to land for reuse.

💡 It is named after Nancy Grace Roman, NASA's first chief astronomer, called the 'Mother of Hubble'.

🦔 A 'new' pangolin from an old museum skin

DNA from a pangolin skin collected in Nepal in 1836 (kept at the Natural History Museum, London) proved that the Himalayan pangolin is a separate species. Its original 1836 name, Manis aurita, has been brought back. It lives in Nepal and northern India.

💡 Pangolins are the most trafficked mammals in the world — this DNA work can help trace smuggled scales.

🌕 Partial lunar eclipse — 28 Aug

About 93% of the Moon went into Earth's dark shadow (the umbra). It was seen in Europe, Africa and the Americas but not from India, where the Moon was below the horizon.

💡 A lunar eclipse happens when the Earth comes between the Sun and the Moon — only on a full moon night.
🎨 Arts & Culture

🌸 Onam — Thiruvonam on 26 Aug

Kerala's harvest festival welcomes back the legendary King Mahabali. Families make pookalam (flower rangoli), eat the Onasadya feast on banana leaves, and watch vallam kali (snake-boat races).

🪢 Raksha Bandhan — 28 Aug

Sisters tie a rakhi on their brothers' wrists. This year it fell on the same day as the lunar eclipse.

🏅 Sports

🏑 Women's Hockey World Cup — final in Amstelveen

Argentina won the title, beating the Netherlands 3–1 in a penalty shoot-out after a 1–1 draw — Argentina's first global title in 16 years. India finished 5th, beating Germany 3–1 (Navneet Kaur scored twice) — India's best World Cup finish since 1974. Hockey India announced a ₹50 lakh reward for the team (28 Aug).

🏏 India win the series in Sri Lanka — 27 Aug

The second Test at the SSC, Colombo was drawn, so India won the series 1–0. Devdutt Padikkal was Player of the Series.

🎯 Neeraj Chopra into the Diamond League Final

Neeraj Chopra qualified for the javelin Diamond League Final in Brussels even after missing the Zurich meet. Rumesh Pathirage of Sri Lanka topped the standings after a huge 92.07 m throw in Zurich.

🏅 National Sports Day — 29 Aug

India celebrates National Sports Day on the birthday of hockey wizard Major Dhyan Chand, who won three Olympic gold medals (1928, 1932, 1936).

Nationalités et adjectifs — révision
Workbook pages 21–27 and textbook pages 43–48. The same rules as Thursday, now inside full sentences.
✍️ Complete the sentence — two steps

Country first, then nationality

Step 1: en / au / aux + country. Step 2: nationality agreeing with the subject.

Marie et Valérie habitent au Portugal ; elles sont portugaises.
Nous habitons aux États-Unis ; nous sommes américains.
Patrice et Jean habitent au Danemark ; ils sont danois.
💡 Danois already ends in s, so the masculine plural doesn't change: ils sont danois.
🔁 Mettez au féminin / au pluriel

Worked examples from the workbook

Voici un petit garçon canadien. → Voici une petite fille canadienne.
Le père de Marc est gentil. → La mère de Marc est gentille.
L'étudiant belge est gros. → L'étudiante belge est grosse.
Il a un ami russe. → Ils ont des amis russes.
L'enfant de M. Hanks est beau et amusant. → Les enfants de M. Hanks sont beaux et amusants.

bel, nouvel, vieil — again

Voici une nouvelle étudiante (feminine → nouvelle).
C'est un nouvel étudiant (masculine + vowel → nouvel).
C'est un bel homme (masculine + silent h → bel).

Which word agrees?

L'homme est gros (not grande, not jolis).
Il a les cheveux courts — cheveux is masculine plural.
Les femmes sont heureuses — feminine plural.

🎯 Fun Trivia
The textbook poem « Un monde uni » gives each nation a colour: le violet royal des Français, les Italiens verts… — a reminder that nationalities, like adjectives, change to match the people they describe.
