➗ The ideas

The relations to know by heart

These are the only numerical relations in the chapter — but they turn up in every question.

Diameter = 2 × Radius
Radius = Diameter ÷ 2
Longest chord = the diameter
A diameter splits the circle into 2 semi-circles
💡 If a question gives you a diameter, halve it before doing anything else.

Name that line

Radius — centre to a point on the circle.
Diameter — through the centre, both ends on the circle.
Chord — joins two points on the circle.
Secant — a line cutting the circle at two points.
Tangent — a line touching the circle at one point.

Distance of a line from the centre: less than r → secant · equal to r → tangent · more than r → misses the circle.

Mensuration formulas you already know

Circles arrive late in the year, so these earlier formulas still need to stay sharp.

Area of a rectangle = l × b · Perimeter = 2(l + b)
Area of a square = side × side · Perimeter = 4 × side
Area of a triangle = ½ × base × height
Area of a parallelogram = base × height
🎉 Fun facts & trivia

The Greek letter π was popularised by William Jones in 1706, from the Greek word periphereia, meaning circumference. Before that, mathematicians wrote it out as a whole phrase.

A circle can be thought of as a regular polygon with infinitely many, infinitely short sides. Archimedes used that idea to squeeze π between 3¹⁰⁄₇₁ and 3¹⁄₇ — more than 2,200 years ago.

The word radius is Latin for the spoke of a wheel. The plural is radii, not 'radiuses'.

📜 Part 3 of 12 — Archimedes and the Sand

Archimedes of Syracuse (c. 287–212 BCE) wanted to know how the circumference of a circle compares with its diameter. He could not measure a curve directly, so he trapped it: he drew a polygon inside the circle and another outside it, then doubled the number of sides again and again until he reached 96 sides.

The circle's boundary had to lie between the two polygons, which gave him π to two decimal places. Legend says he was still drawing circles in the sand when a soldier interrupted him — and that his last words were, 'Do not disturb my circles.'
