➗ Maths — Adding & Subtracting Polynomials
➗ Adding

Like terms combine, unlike terms don't

To add like terms, add the coefficients and keep the variable part unchanged:

3x + 5x = 8x  ·  −7xy + 4xy = −3xy

Unlike terms can only be written side by side — 2a + 3b stays 2a + 3b.

💡 7x + 8x = 15x, not 15x². Adding does not change the power — only multiplying does.
➗ Subtracting

Subtract the coefficients

8y − 3y = 5y  ·  −5ab − 2ab = −7ab  ·  8y − (−3y) = 8y + 3y = 11y

Opening brackets — the sign trap

A minus sign in front of a bracket changes the sign of every term inside it:

(3x + 2y) − (x − 5y) = 3x + 2y − x + 5y = 2x + 7y
8 − (4a − 5) = 8 − 4a + 5 = 13 − 4a
💡 The commonest mistake in the whole chapter is changing only the first term inside the bracket. Change them all.

Reading the instruction

"Subtract (5a + 3b) from (8a − 2b)" means (8a − 2b) − (5a + 3b) = 8a − 2b − 5a − 3b = 3a − 5b.

For longer sums, arrange like terms under each other in columns before you add.

Before the quiz
Underline like terms in the same colour before combining — x terms once, y terms twice. Then add each group separately.
🏛️ History — Knowing the Past

Pre-history and history

History is the systematic study of past events, societies and civilisations.
Pre-history is the long period before writing was invented — there are no written records, so we depend entirely on material remains.
History proper begins with the invention of writing, about 5,000 years ago.

💡 Herodotus, a Greek writer, is called the ‘father of history’ because he was the first to investigate and record events rather than simply retell legends.
Type of sourceWhat it isExamples
ArchaeologicalMaterial remains dug up or still standingInscriptions, coins, monuments, tools, pottery, artefacts
LiteraryWritten textsReligious — Vedas, Puranas, Tripitakas · Secular — biographies, travelogues, dramas, law books

The specialists and their tools

Epigraphy — the study of inscriptions engraved on rocks, pillars and copper plates.
Numismatics — the study of coins, which reveal rulers, dates, the economy and trade routes.
Monuments — temples, stupas, forts and palaces tell us about architecture, beliefs and art.
Carbon-14 dating — measures how much radioactive carbon is left in organic remains (bone, wood, cloth) to estimate their age.

💡 Carbon-14 only works on things that were once alive. It cannot date a metal coin or a stone slab.

Geography shapes history

Every early civilisation grew along a river — the Indus, the Nile, the Tigris and Euphrates, the Huang He. Rivers gave water for irrigation, drinking, and transport for trade. Natural resources decided which empires grew rich, and changes in climate pushed whole populations to migrate.

🏛️ Civics — Rural Local Self-Government

Panchayati Raj — three tiers

Given constitutional status in 1992. The three tiers are:

Tier 1 — Gram Panchayat (village) · Tier 2 — Panchayat Samiti (block) · Tier 3 — Zila Parishad (district)

Gram Sabha — the villagers themselves

Every villager aged 18 and above is a member. It meets at least twice a year, elects the Gram Panchayat, supervises its work, and can remove it through a no-confidence vote.

💡 The Gram Sabha is not elected — it is the whole adult village. That is what makes it the watchdog of the Panchayat.

Gram Panchayat — village level

Elected by the Gram Sabha for 5 years; headed by the Sarpanch (or Pradhan). A salaried Panchayat Secretary, a government employee, keeps the records.

Functions: help farmers with seeds, fertilisers and cattle care; provide civic amenities — drinking water, wells, primary health centres; maintain birth and death records; run primary schools.

Income: taxes on houses and shops, on fairs and markets, cattle registration, sale of public property — but most of its money comes from state grants.

Panchayat Samiti — block level

Members are the Sarpanches of the villages in that block, along with MPs, MLAs and special representatives for SC/ST and women. Headed by the Block Pramukh; the Block Development Officer (BDO) is its executive officer.

Functions: coordinate development programmes, promote agriculture and small industries, spread literacy, obtain funds from the state.

Zila Parishad — district level

The highest tier. Members include the chairpersons of the block samitis, MPs, MLAs, MLCs, representatives of cooperatives, and members on reserved seats. Headed by a Chairperson, assisted by the Deputy Commissioner and a Secretary.

Functions: prepare the overall development plan for the district, distribute and coordinate grants, and implement Five Year Plan schemes.

Nyaya Panchayat — the village court

Covers three or four villages and settles minor civil and criminal cases, saving villagers the time and money of going to a distant court. Appeals against its decisions go to the district court.

✍️ Describe two differences between archaeological and literary sources.
Model answer →
First, archaeological sources are material objects — coins, pottery, tools and monuments — while literary sources are written texts such as the Vedas or a traveller's account. Second, archaeological sources exist for periods before writing was invented and so are our only evidence for pre-history, whereas literary sources begin only after writing appeared. Archaeological remains are also usually free of the writer's opinion, while a written text may reflect the beliefs of the person who wrote it.
✍️ How does the Panchayati Raj system help villagers take part in their own development?
Model answer →
Every adult villager is a member of the Gram Sabha, so ordinary people — not distant officials — elect the Gram Panchayat and decide which works matter most. The Gram Sabha meets regularly to examine the Panchayat's accounts and plans, and can even remove it by a no-confidence vote. Because seats are reserved for women and for Scheduled Castes and Tribes, groups that were once left out now help make the decisions. Problems such as a broken well or a closed school are solved by people who live with them daily.
🎯 Fun Trivia
History: Herodotus, a Greek scholar of about 450 BCE, is called the father of history — not because he was the first to write about the past, but because he was the first to collect and check his facts systematically instead of simply retelling legends.
🎯 Fun Trivia
Civics: Panchayats are far older than modern India. In ancient villages, five respected elders — the panch — would sit together to settle disputes. That is literally where the word panchayat comes from, and the idea survived for well over two thousand years before becoming law.
📜 The Story of Algebra · Part 4 of 16 — Europe Invents the Symbols

Centuries of writing maths in words

Even after al-Khwarizmi, mathematicians still wrote equations as sentences. Renaissance Europe finally built the compact notation we use now. François Viète pushed symbolic algebra forward, and René Descartes settled the habit of using x, y, z for unknowns and a, b, c for known values.

There is no single reason why x won. Any letter works — x simply became the convention for the mysterious thing we are trying to find.

The equals sign has an author

The = sign was invented by the Welsh mathematician Robert Recorde in 1557. He was tired of writing is equal to over and over, and chose two parallel lines because, in his words, no two things can be more equal than two parallel lines.

Before 1557, every single equation ever written used words for equals.
💡 Bracket signs, +, − and = were all invented by somebody. The rules for opening brackets you are practising today are only possible because those symbols exist.

Descartes joins algebra to geometry

Descartes showed that an equation can be seen as a shape. y = 2x + 3 is not just a rule — it is a straight line. This became coordinate geometry, and later powered physics, engineering and computer graphics.

Linear equation → straight line  ·  Quadratic equation → parabola (the curve of a thrown ball)
