📖 English Literature · Week 16
Ch 5 · The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn — Part 1
From Huck’s escape to Jackson’s Island, meeting Jim, the flood and Huck’s disguise. Told as a graphic novel from Mark Twain’s classic. Theme: freedom, friendship and conscience.
🧭 Background

Huck Finn, about 13–14, lives in St. Petersburg, Missouri. With Tom Sawyer he found a box of gold in a cave; Judge Thatcher invests it. The kind Widow Douglas tries to educate him, but he hates that life and runs off. His cruel father, Pap, kidnaps him to a cabin in the woods to get the money. When Pap is away, Huck fakes his own death and escapes downriver — “a plan worthy of Tom Sawyer’s thinking”.

🏝️ Part 1 in order

1. Huck hides on Jackson’s Island, about 12 miles downriver, and feels truly free for the first time.
2. A ferry fires a cannon to make his “dead body” rise. Aboard: Judge Thatcher, the Widow, Tom Sawyer, Pap and Aunt Polly. He feels bad, but is too happy to care.
3. Tent of blankets, catfish for supper — then loneliness.
4. A smouldering campfire; Huck hides in a tree for two hours. It is Jim, Miss Watson’s slave, who thinks Huck is a ghost.
5. Jim ran away because Miss Watson meant to sell him downriver in New Orleans for $800. He tried to swim to Illinois, a Free State, but reached only the island. They swear an oath to keep each other’s secrets.
6. Rain: they shelter in a cave. In the flood they capture a raft of logs; a floating house holds silver coins, a lantern, girls’ dresses, a hatchet and candles — and a murdered man Jim won’t let Huck see.
7. Dressed as a girl, “Sarah Williams”, Huck learns that people think Jim murdered Huck, a $300 reward is out, and men are heading to Jackson’s Island.

💭 Huck’s dilemma

Huck was raised to believe slavery is right, so helping Jim feels like breaking the law. He doesn’t want to be called an abolitionist (someone who wants slavery banned) — “I had a reputation to uphold” — yet he doesn’t want Jim caught. This inner conflict drives the whole story.

✏️ Language from the chapter

Tenses: simple present — “You’re dead, Huck!” · present perfect — “Your ghost has come back to haunt me” · simple past — “It was all a trick.”
Homophones: by/buy, pair/pear, flea/flee, cite/site, main/mane.
Dialect: “I ain’t dead”, “We’s nice and dry” — Twain writes the way people really spoke.
Words: cannon, catfish, supper, smouldering, pounding, hiked, oath, debris, hatchet, grim, sock.

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Mark Twain (1835–1910)

Pen name of Samuel Langhorne Clemens, one of the most important American writers.

🌟 Fun facts
• “Mark twain” was a call on Mississippi riverboats meaning the water is two fathoms (12 feet) deep — safe to sail. Twain himself worked as a licensed riverboat pilot.
• He was born in 1835 when Halley’s Comet passed Earth, predicted he would “go out with it”, and died in 1910 when it returned.
• The Adventures of Tom Sawyer (1876) came first; Huckleberry Finn (1885) is its sequel, narrated by Huck himself.
• Ernest Hemingway said all modern American literature comes from this one book.
• He also wrote travel books, short stories, essays and poems.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. What were Huck’s feelings when he escaped and watched the ferry search for his body?
👀 Show model answer
He felt free for the first time in his life and was delighted that his trick had worked. Watching the Judge, the Widow, Tom and Aunt Polly searching for him, he felt a little bad that they were grieving, but his joy at being free of Pap and the Widow’s rules was stronger. Soon, though, his freedom turned into loneliness.
2. Why is Huck torn about helping Jim?
👀 Show model answer
Huck has been brought up to believe that helping a slave escape is wrong and that people who do so are shameful abolitionists. But Jim is kind, frightened and alone, and Huck has sworn an oath to keep his secret. His upbringing pulls him one way and his feelings for Jim pull him the other.
