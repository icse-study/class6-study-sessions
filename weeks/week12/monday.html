Simple Linear Equations — The Balanced Scale
Chapter 14 · An equation is a balance. Whatever you do to one side, do to the other — and the scale stays level.
➗ Expression vs equation

The equals sign is the difference

An expression has no equals sign and cannot be solved (3x + 5). An equation says two things are equal (3x + 5 = 20) and has a solution.

Expression: 2x − 7  ·  Equation: 2x − 7 = 9  ·  Solution: x = 8
💡 You simplify an expression but you solve an equation. If there is no "=", there is nothing to solve.

What "solution" means

The solution (or root) is the value of the letter that makes the left side equal the right side.

Is x = 4 a solution of 3x − 2 = 10? LHS = 3(4) − 2 = 10 = RHS. Yes.
💡 Checking is free marks: substitute your answer back and see whether both sides match.
➗ Solving by balancing

Do the same to both sides

Add, subtract, multiply or divide both sides by the same number, until the letter stands alone.

x + 7 = 12 → subtract 7 from both sides → x = 5
5x = 45 → divide both sides by 5 → x = 9
x/4 = 3 → multiply both sides by 4 → x = 12

Undo in reverse order

Remove the term that is added or subtracted first, then deal with the number multiplying the letter.

3x + 5 = 20 → 3x = 15 (subtract 5) → x = 5 (divide by 3)
💡 Dividing before subtracting is the usual wrong turn: from 3x + 5 = 20 you cannot jump to x + 5 = 20 ÷ 3.
➗ Checking and vocabulary

Every equation deserves a check

Substitute the answer into the original equation, work out both sides separately, and confirm they are equal.

2x − 3 = 11 gives x = 7. Check: LHS = 2(7) − 3 = 11 = RHS. ✔
💡 LHS means Left Hand Side, RHS means Right Hand Side. Use those labels in your answer — ICSE examiners expect them.

📜 The Story of Algebra · Part 13 of 16 — A New Question in the 19th Century

The question changed

For most of history mathematicians asked: how do I solve this equation? By the 19th century they began asking something deeper: what are the underlying rules that make a whole mathematical system work?

That shift created abstract algebra — the study of structure itself, rather than of individual answers.

Groups, rings and fields

Abstract algebra studies sets of things together with the operations allowed on them: groups (the simplest structure), rings, fields, and symmetries — the ways an object can be turned or flipped and still look the same.

Mathematicians such as Évariste Galois, Niels Henrik Abel and Emmy Noether built this new world.
💡 It sounds far from x + 8 = 15 — but it grew directly out of asking why the balancing rules you are using today always work.

The balance that never lies

Solving an equation is keeping a balance level. Whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other. That single rule is the whole method, and it is the small seed that abstract algebra grew from.

x + 11 = 20 → subtract 11 from both sides → x = 9

A day at the ಜಾತ್ರೆ 🎡
India is a land of ಮಠಗಳು (monasteries) and ಜಾತ್ರೆಗಳು (fairs). Join the students of Adaragunji school on their trip to one of Karnataka's most famous fairs.
📍 The place

Hubballi and the ಮಠ

Hubballi in Dharwad district is a busy centre of trade and education. It is home to the Siddharoodha Matha. Every year a grand fair is held there on Mahashivaratri.

🏛️ What is a ಮಠ?

The teacher's answer

A ಮಠ is a social system that helps people in hard times — it can give food and shelter to students, comfort to the troubled, run religious programmes and spread the teachings of great people and epics.

💡 The teacher calls the ಮಠ "a lighthouse (ದೀಪಸ್ತಂಭ) standing on the shore of the ocean called society".
🎡 At the fair

Sights and sounds

Devotional songs (ಭಜನೆ) while cooking and serving food · volunteers serving meals · the evening ರಥೋತ್ಸವ (chariot festival): devotees pull the chariot shouting "ಹರ ಹರ ಮಹಾದೇವ" and throw dry dates (ಉತ್ತತ್ತಿ) and bananas onto it · stalls of small traders and artisans.

Why fairs matter

They bring people together in harmony (ಸೌಹಾರ್ದ), give work to artisans and traders, and teach children about culture and tradition.

📖 English Literature · Week 12
Ch 1 · The Most Beautiful Thing & The Sailor
Prose: an excerpt from Rupa Bajwa’s novel The Sari Shop. Poem: “The Sailor” by Rabindranath Tagore. Theme: self, family, home and imagination.
📖 The story — The Most Beautiful Thing

Setting: a tiny room behind a small grocery shop in Amritsar. One corner is curtained off with Ma’s old red sari with yellow flowers (the bathroom); another is the kitchen with a kerosene stove.

1. Ma gives five-year-old Ramchand, who looks a little mutinous, a lump of dough: “Go and make the most beautiful thing in the world.”
2. She often made him sparrows, rabbits (just a blob with a bobtail — “it is running away”) and a mouse called Choo Hoo, who has no whiskers because she went near the stove and they got singed.
3. Ramchand thinks the most beautiful thing is his mother or father — but he can’t make them from dough, and they are not really things.
4. Ma goes to the shop to help Pa find a misplaced tin of black pepper. She returns to find Ramchand truly crying: “I don’t know what is the most beautiful thing in the world.”
5. She does not laugh. She tells Pa the truth with composure. Pa says: “But I don’t know that either.”
6. Warm milk, an expensive sugar-coated biscuit, and from that day Ramchand loved his parents much more.

🔑 What examiners look for

Ma’s wisdom: she takes a child’s feelings seriously — no laughing, no lie (“He just fell down”).
Pa’s honesty: admitting he doesn’t know makes Ramchand’s question feel important, not silly.
Choo Hoo story: began because whiskers were hard to make — Ma turned it into a safety warning.
Timeline: Ramchand “had been born twenty-six years ago” and was five at the time, so the memory is from about 21 years earlier.
Theme: love, respect and honesty within a family.

⛵ The poem — The Sailor (Tagore)

Madhu’s boat lies idle at the wharf of Rajgunj, “uselessly laden with jute”. The child dreams of a hundred oars and five, six or seven sails; he would never steer her to “stupid markets”, but sail the seven seas and thirteen rivers of fairyland with his friend Ashu, past the ford of Tirpurni and the desert of Tepantar.
He reassures his mother with a simile: he is not going “like Ramachandra” for fourteen years — he will set off at early morning light and be back when it is getting dark.

✏️ Language from the chapter

Nouns: common (boy), proper (Amritsar), collective (a pride of lions), abstract (love, freedom), material (wood, clay).
Countable vs uncountable: book, guava (countable) · advice, honey, salt (uncountable — “a bottle of water” makes it countable).
Silent gh: dough · gh = /f/: laugh.
Odd one out: murmured, whispered, crooned are soft — wailed is loud.
Words: mutinous (refusing to obey), deftly, singed, crooned, composure, apprehensively, wharf, moored, laden, ford.

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Rupa Bajwa

Indian novelist, born 1976 in Amritsar, Punjab, where she still lives.

🌟 Fun facts
• Her first novel, The Sari Shop, won the Commonwealth Award (2005) and the Sahitya Akademi Award (2006).
• In the novel, grown-up Ramchand is a shop assistant in a sari shop in Amritsar — our chapter is a memory from his childhood.
• Her second novel, Tell Me a Story, came out in 2012.

✍️ Rabindranath Tagore (1861–1941)

Poet, novelist, playwright, songwriter, musician and painter from Bengal.

🌟 Fun facts
• First non-European to win the Nobel Prize in Literature (1913), for Gitanjali.
• He wrote the national anthems of two countries — India’s Jana Gana Mana and Bangladesh’s Amar Shonar Bangla.
• He was knighted in 1915 and gave the knighthood back in 1919 in protest against the Jallianwala Bagh massacre.
• He founded Visva-Bharati University at Shantiniketan, and took up painting only in his sixties.
• Other works: the novels Gora and Ghare-Baire (The Home and the World).

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. Was Ramchand’s mother wise in the way she reacted when she found him crying? Give reasons.
👀 Show model answer
Yes. She did not laugh or dismiss his tears; she hugged him, asked him seriously “the way one asks an adult”, and waited for him to speak. She then told her husband the truth instead of hiding it. By treating his feelings with respect, she made him feel safe and understood, which is why he was grateful to her for the rest of his life.
2. Why do you think this incident left a lasting impression on Ramchand?
👀 Show model answer
His question was a big one for a five-year-old, and his parents did not make fun of it. His mother’s silence and his father’s honest “I don’t know that either” showed him that grown-ups take him seriously and do not have all the answers. The peace and love he felt that evening made him love his parents even more.
3. The speaker of “The Sailor” is a highly imaginative child. Do you agree?
👀 Show model answer
Yes. He looks at an idle jute boat and imagines a hundred oars and seven sails, the seven seas and thirteen rivers of fairyland, a strange king’s land and the desert of Tepantar. He even plans to be “the prince of the story”, yet he is practical enough to promise his mother he will be home by dark.
