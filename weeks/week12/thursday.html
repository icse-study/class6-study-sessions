Word Problems — From Sentence to Equation
Chapter 14 · The hard part is never the solving. Name the unknown, write one equation, solve it, then answer the question that was actually asked.
➗ A method that always works

Four steps, in order

1. Let the unknown be x (state the unit). 2. Write every other quantity in terms of x. 3. Form one equation from the sentence that says "is" or "equals". 4. Solve, check, and write the answer in words.

Five times a number, increased by 3, is 38. Let the number be x → 5x + 3 = 38 → 5x = 35 → x = 7. The number is 7.
💡 The last step is the one students drop. If the question asks for the father's age, do not stop at x — x may be the son's age.

Spotting the equation

The words "is", "equals", "gives", "becomes" and "altogether" mark the place where the equals sign goes.

The sum of two consecutive numbers is 45 → x + (x + 1) = 45 → 2x + 1 = 45 → x = 22, so the numbers are 22 and 23.
➗ Common problem types

Age problems

Everyone ages by the same amount. Add the same number of years to every person.

A mother is 4 times as old as her daughter. In 6 years she will be twice as old. Let the daughter be x → 4x + 6 = 2(x + 6) → 4x + 6 = 2x + 12 → 2x = 6 → x = 3.

Money and cost problems

Cost = number of items × price of one item. Total spent plus money left equals money started with.

3 pens at ₹x each and a ₹40 book cost ₹115 → 3x + 40 = 115 → 3x = 75 → x = 25. Each pen costs ₹25.

Number and perimeter problems

For rectangles use P = 2(l + b); write the width in terms of the length, or the other way round.

The length of a rectangle is 5 cm more than its width and the perimeter is 46 cm. Let the width be x → 2(x + x + 5) = 46 → 4x + 10 = 46 → x = 9, so the width is 9 cm and the length 14 cm.

📜 The Story of Algebra · Part 15 of 16 — Algebra Describes the Universe

Two of the most famous sentences ever written

Physics is written in algebra. Newton's F = ma and Einstein's E = mc² are just equations — relationships between quantities, exactly like the word problems you solved today.

F = ma tells you: force is mass times acceleration, for every object in the universe.
💡 Give any two of F, m and a, and you can find the third — by solving a linear equation, precisely the skill in this session.

A word problem is a model

When you turn Anita is 5 times as old as her son into 5x, you are doing what physicists do: building a model. The letters stand for real quantities, and the equation captures how they are tied together.

Read → name the unknown → write the relationship → solve → check against the story.

From counting grain to landing rockets

The Egyptians used unknowns to divide bread and beer. Today the same idea puts spacecraft into orbit. The equations grew; the method — name what you do not know, then pin it down — never changed.

💡 Always check your answer inside the original sentence, not just in the equation. An age of −7 solves the maths and fails the story.
