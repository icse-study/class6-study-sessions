🧪 Ch 2 in one card

Experiment = controlled conditions · Porcelain: evaporating dish, crucible, mortar & pestle · Measuring cylinder: volume · Reagent bottle: storage · Wire gauze: even heat · Water bath: indirect heat · Glass: transparent, heat-proof, unreactive · Pyrex hardly expands

⚛️ Ch 3 in one card

Matter: mass + space · Force: S > L > G · Gaps: G > L > S · Free surfaces: many / one / none · Diffusion fastest in gases · Melting = fusion, freezing = solidification · MP = FP, BP = condensation point · Sublimation: camphor, iodine, naphthalene, dry ice · Heat: change of state, expansion, chemical change

🔩 Ch 4 — elements

118 elements (92 natural) · Metals ~93: Na soft, Hg liquid, Zn brittle, W poor conductor · Non-metals 18: Br liquid, I and diamond/graphite shine, graphite conducts · 7 metalloids · 6 noble gases · Latin symbols Na, K, Fe, Cu · crust O, atmosphere N, universe H & He

🧬 Ch 4 — compounds & formulae

Compound: fixed ratio (water 1:8), new properties, chemical separation · Atomicity: He 1, O2 2, O3 3, P4 4, S8 8 · Valency: H1 O2 N3 C4 Na1 Cl1 Mg2 Ca2 Al3 · Criss-cross then cancel

🧠 Linking ideas

• Why is a gas jar covered with a watch glass? Gases diffuse and escape in all directions.
• Why is sodium stored in kerosene? It reacts fast with air and water.
• Why use a crucible for strong heating? Porcelain withstands high temperature.
• Why does a mercury thermometer work? Liquids expand on heating.

📝 Exam tips

Read every option — many traps differ by one word (hard/soft, liquid/solid, capital/small letter). In formula questions, always cancel equal subscripts. In assertion–reason, test each statement separately before asking whether R explains A.

✨ Fun facts
• The first periodic table was made by Dmitri Mendeleev in 1869, with gaps for elements not yet discovered — and he predicted their properties correctly.
• Helium was discovered in the Sun (from its light) before it was found on Earth.
• A single drop of water contains about 1.5 sextillion (1.5 × 1021) molecules.
• Diamond and graphite are both pure carbon — just arranged differently.
