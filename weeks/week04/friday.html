🧬 Science Diagram Recall
15 diagrams and the labels that earn the marks
Draw each diagram in your notebook, then check the label list here. The quiz tests whether you can recall the labels — because in an exam the labels carry the marks, not the artwork.
✏️ How to draw for marks
Use a sharp pencil and single clean lines · draw it large enough to label · pointer lines must be straight, not crossing, and touch the part · write labels horizontally on the right wherever possible · always give the diagram a title.
⚗️ Physics & Chemistry diagrams

1 · Three states of matter

Three boxes side by side showing particle arrangement.

Labels: Solid — particles closely packed in a regular pattern · Liquid — particles close but irregular, able to slide · Gas — particles far apart, moving freely

2 · Laboratory thermometer

A long narrow glass tube with a bulb at the bottom.

Labels: Bulb · Mercury / thermometric liquid · Capillary tube · Stem · Scale (−10 °C to 110 °C)

3 · Filtration of chalk and water

Funnel resting in a conical flask, filter paper folded inside.

Labels: Stand · Funnel · Filter paper · Residue (chalk) · Filtrate (clear water) · Beaker / conical flask

4 · Evaporation using a china dish

China dish on a wire gauze over a tripod stand and burner.

Labels: China dish · Solution · Wire gauze · Tripod stand · Bunsen burner · Water vapour escaping

5 · Reflection from a plane mirror

A mirror with a dotted line standing upright at the point of incidence.

Labels: Plane mirror · Incident ray · Reflected ray · Normal (dotted, at 90°) · Angle of incidence (i) · Angle of reflection (r) — and i = r

6 · Bar magnet with field lines

Curved lines leaving one pole and entering the other.

Labels: North pole (N) · South pole (S) · Magnetic field lines — they run from N to S outside the magnet and never cross

7 · Simple electric circuit

A closed loop drawn with straight lines and right-angled corners.

Labels: Cell (long line = positive, short thick line = negative) · Switch / key · Bulb · Connecting wires

8 · The water cycle

Sun, sea, clouds, rain and a river running back to the sea.

Labels: Evaporation (arrows up) · Condensation (cloud forming) · Precipitation (rain falling) · Collection / run-off · Transpiration from plants

14 · Human digestive system

Order: Mouth → Oesophagus (food pipe) → Stomach → Small intestine → Large intestine → Rectum → Anus. Glands: liver, pancreas, salivary glands

15 · Human respiratory system

Nose, a central tube, and two lungs branching out.

🧬 Biology · Cells & Plant Life
The cell, its organelles, tissues & plants
Who discovered the cell, what each organelle does, how plant and animal cells differ, and the basics of roots, leaves and photosynthesis. Read the cards, then take the quiz.
🔬 The cell

Discovery

Robert Hooke discovered the cell in 1665, looking at a thin slice of cork through his own microscope. The empty boxes he saw reminded him of the small rooms in a monastery, so he called them cells.

The cell is the structural and functional unit of life — every living thing is made of one or more cells.
OrganelleNicknameFunction
NucleusControl centre / brainDirects all activities; holds the DNA
MitochondriaPowerhouseReleases energy from food (respiration)
LysosomeSuicide bagDigests waste and worn-out parts
ChloroplastKitchenHolds chlorophyll; carries out photosynthesis
VacuoleStorage tankStores water, food and waste (very large in plant cells)
Cytoplasm—The jelly-like fluid matrix inside the cell membrane, holding the organelles
Cell membraneGatekeeperControls what enters and leaves
Cell wall—Outermost rigid covering of a PLANT cell, made of cellulose

Plant cell vs animal cell

A plant cell has a cell wall, chloroplasts and one large central vacuole. An animal cell has none of these — its outermost layer is the cell membrane, and its vacuoles are small and many.

💡 That is why plant cells look rectangular and rigid, while animal cells are rounded and irregular.

Unicellular and multicellular

Unicellular organisms are made of a single cell that performs all life processes — for example Amoeba, Paramecium, bacteria and yeast. Multicellular organisms are made of many cells — plants, animals, humans.

A tissue is a group of similar cells that work together to do the same job. Tissues → organs → organ systems.

Photosynthesis in one line

Carbon dioxide + Water — sunlight & chlorophyll → Glucose (food) + Oxygen

Chlorophyll is the green pigment inside the chloroplasts that captures sunlight. That is why leaves are green.
