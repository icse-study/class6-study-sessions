🔁 Quick recap

Little Women (Louisa May Alcott) — on Christmas morning the four March sisters start reading the books Marmee gave them, prepare gifts for her (slippers, handkerchiefs, cologne), and then give their breakfast to a starving German family. They are the merriest people in the city.
The Arrow and the Song (Longfellow) — an arrow and a song vanish into the air; long afterwards the arrow is found unbroken in an oak, and the song in the heart of a friend.

🧩 Linking ideas

Both texts show that what we give out comes back: the girls give their breakfast and receive joy; the speaker gives a song and finds it treasured by a friend. Both reward generosity and kind words.
Character arcs: Amy grows from vain to unselfish; Jo’s restlessness turns into warmth and humour; Meg leads by example; Beth gives quietly.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. “The March sisters were obedient, caring and kind.” Explain with examples.
👀 Show model answer
They are obedient: when Meg says Mother wants them to read their books, the others follow her example, and they agree at once to Marmee’s request. They are caring: Beth marks the handkerchiefs “Mother” and Amy spends all her money on a better gift. They are kind: though very hungry, they give their whole breakfast to a poor family and feed the children themselves.
2. How do “Little Women” and “The Arrow and the Song” share a message?
👀 Show model answer
Both show that generosity and kindness are never lost. The March girls give away their breakfast and feel happier than anyone in the city. Longfellow’s speaker gives a song and later finds it treasured in a friend’s heart. What we give freely returns to us as joy and friendship.
