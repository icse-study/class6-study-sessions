Binary Numbers & How Computers Store Data
Inside a computer, everything — numbers, words, pictures, music — is stored as 1s and 0s.
💡 Why binary?

Computer chips are made of billions of tiny switches that are either on (1) or off (0). Counting with only two digits is called binary (base 2). We normally count in decimal (base 10).

🔢 Counting to 10

Binary place values double: 16 · 8 · 4 · 2 · 1.
0 = 0 · 1 = 1 · 2 = 10 · 3 = 11 · 4 = 100 · 5 = 101 · 6 = 110 · 7 = 111 · 8 = 1000 · 9 = 1001 · 10 = 1010

To read 1011: 8 + 0 + 2 + 1 = 11.

📦 Bits and bytes

A bit is one binary digit. 1 byte = 8 bits, which can store 2⁸ = 256 values (0–255). Then: kilobyte (about 1,000 bytes) → megabyte → gigabyte → terabyte.

🖼️ Storing text, pictures and sound

Text: each letter has a number — in ASCII, “A” = 65 = 01000001.
Pictures: a grid of pixels, each with numbers for its red, green and blue light.
Sound: the sound wave is measured thousands of times a second (sampling), and each measurement is stored as a number.

print(bin(5))          # 0b101
print(int("101", 2))   # 5
print(ord("A"))        # 65
✨ Fun facts
• Your name in binary: “A” = 01000001, “B” = 01000010 — try your first letter!
• There are 10 types of people in the world: those who understand binary and those who don’t. (10 in binary = 2.)
• Gottfried Leibniz wrote about binary numbers more than 300 years ago, long before computers.
