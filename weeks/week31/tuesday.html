Cybersecurity & Digital Citizenship
Staying safe and being kind online: strong passwords, spotting tricks, protecting personal information, and standing up to cyberbullying.
🔐 Strong passwords

Long (12+ characters) · a mix of capital and small letters, numbers and symbols · no names, birthdays or pet names · different for every account · never shared, even with friends. A passphrase of several random words (e.g. Blue!Kite7Mango) is strong and easy to remember. Turn on two-factor authentication (a code sent to a phone) where you can.

🎣 Phishing

Fake messages that try to trick you into giving information or clicking a bad link. Warning signs: urgent threats (“your account will be closed!”), prizes you never entered, spelling mistakes, and lookalike addresses such as amaz0n-prizes.com. Don’t click — tell a trusted adult.

🛡️ Safe browsing

Never share your home address, school, phone number or photos in uniform with strangers. The HTTPS padlock means the connection is encrypted — but it does not prove the site is honest. Keep apps updated (updates fix security holes). Malware is harmful software such as viruses. Everything you post leaves a digital footprint.

💛 Being kind online

Before posting, THINK: is it True, Helpful, Inspiring, Necessary, Kind? If you are bullied online: don’t reply, take a screenshot as evidence, block and report, and tell a trusted adult. If a stranger asks to meet, say no and tell an adult.

✨ Fun facts
• “123456” and “password” have been among the most common — and most hacked — passwords for years.
• The word “phishing” is spelled with “ph” because it is “fishing” for your information.
• A computer can try billions of guesses per second, so every extra character in a password makes it much harder to crack.
