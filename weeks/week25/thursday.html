⚗️ Chemistry · Week 25 · Ch 7 Water (Part 1)
Water — the liquid of life and the universal solvent
Water is the second most important substance for life after air. This week: water as a compound, where it is found, its three states, the water cycle, fog/mist/dew/frost/hail, and why water is the universal solvent — solute, solvent, saturated solutions and solubility.

💧 Water is a compound

Henry Cavendish (1781) proved water is a compound of hydrogen and oxygen, fixed proportion → H2O.
Pure water: colourless, odourless, tasteless, transparent; freezes at 0 °C, boils at 100 °C; density 1 g/cc at 4 °C.

🌊 Where water is found

~70% of the earth's surface is water: 97% in oceans, about 2% frozen in glaciers and ice caps, only about 1% usable (rivers, lakes, ponds, groundwater).
Living things: human blood 90%, human body 70%, elephant 80%, fish 67%, plants 60%. Watermelon 97%, tomato 95%, egg 75%, bread 25%, butter 17%.
Foods with <14% water are non-perishable; >14% are perishable.

🚰 Sources compared

• Rain water: purest natural water; only dust and dissolved air gases (O2, N2, CO2)
• Sea water: highest salt content
• River water: sand, organic matter, bacteria — unfit to drink directly, but the main source for homes and industry
• Underground (wells, springs): filtered by soil — dissolved impurities but no suspended ones or germs. The groundwater level is the water table.

💡 Evaporate equal samples: rain water leaves no residue, tap water a little, sea water a lot.

🔄 States & the water cycle

Ice →(heat)→ water →(heat)→ steam. Ice, water and steam have different physical states but are chemically the same.
Water cycle: continuous circulation of water between the earth's surface and the atmosphere — evaporation, condensation into clouds, rain or snow. It refills groundwater and controls climate.

🌫️ Fog, mist, dew, frost, hail

• Fog: vapour condenses into droplets near the ground
• Mist: tiny droplets suspended in air
• Dew: vapour condenses on cold objects like grass on winter mornings
• Frost: frozen dew
• Hail: drops freeze in cold upper clouds and fall as ice balls

🧂 Solutions & solubility

Water dissolves so many solids, liquids and gases that it is called the universal solvent.
• Solution = solute + solvent. Soluble: salt, sugar, copper sulphate. Insoluble: sand, chalk, wood, plastic.
• Saturated: can't dissolve any more solute at that temperature · Unsaturated: can dissolve more
• Solubility: amount of solute that saturates a given amount of solvent at a given temperature; usually rises with temperature
• Faster dissolving: stirring, smaller particles, heating

💡 More sugar than salt dissolves in the same water — solubility depends on the solute.
✨ Fun facts
• If all the earth's water fitted in a 1-litre bottle, the fresh water we can actually use would be about one teaspoon.
• A single large hailstone recorded in the USA in 2010 was about 20 cm across.
• Your body loses about 2.5 litres of water a day through breathing, sweating and urine.
• Water expands by about 9% when it freezes — that is why a full bottle in the freezer can crack.
