➗ Maths — Factors, Multiples, HCF & LCM

Factor or multiple?

A factor divides a number exactly, leaving no remainder. Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. A multiple is what you get by multiplying: multiples of 4 are 4, 8, 12, 16 …

Memory hook: factors are few and small; multiples are many and large.

Prime factorisation — the method that does both jobs

Break each number into primes. For the HCF take the common factors with the lowest powers; for the LCM take all factors with the highest powers.

24 = 2³ × 3 and 36 = 2² × 3². HCF = 2² × 3 = 12. LCM = 2³ × 3² = 72.

The rule worth memorising

HCF × LCM = the product of the two numbers.

If HCF = 4, LCM = 48 and one number is 12: the product is 4 × 48 = 192, so the other number is 192 ÷ 12 = 16. Also: the HCF of any two consecutive numbers is always 1.

📖 English — A Gift of Chappals

The story

R. K. Narayan's tale, set in the small town of Malgudi, follows Ravi, a poor boy who longs for a pair of chappals he cannot afford. He takes them — and is immediately weighed down by guilt. In the end he returns them, and finds peace in having done the right thing.

Themes & words

Conscience — the inner struggle after the theft. Honesty — doing right even when it is hard. Poverty and desire — wanting what you cannot have.

Words to know: chappals (sandals), envy (resenting what others have), conscience (inner sense of right and wrong), guilty (feeling regret for a wrong), resolve (to decide firmly).
