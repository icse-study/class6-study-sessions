🧬 Friday · Week 20 · Chapter 1 revisited
Be a leaf scientist 🧪🍃
You know leaves make food and lose water. Today you learn to prove it — the classic experiments, what each part of the set-up is for, and how to read the result.
1️⃣ Is sunlight needed? — the black-strip test

Method

Keep a potted plant in the dark for 2 days (destarching). Cover part of one leaf on both sides with a strip of black paper. Keep it in sunlight for a day, then do the iodine test.

💡 Only the uncovered part turns blue-black. The covered part stays brown — no light, no starch.
2️⃣ Is chlorophyll needed? — the variegated leaf

Method

Take a destarched variegated leaf (green and white patches, e.g. croton or money plant) after a day in sunlight. Draw its pattern, then test with iodine.

💡 Only the green parts turn blue-black — the white parts have no chlorophyll.
3️⃣ Is oxygen given out? — the Hydrilla test

Method

Place a water plant such as Hydrilla under an inverted funnel in a beaker of water, with a water-filled test tube over the funnel. Keep it in sunlight.

💡 Bubbles rise and collect. A glowing splinter bursts into flame in the gas — it is oxygen.

Stomata

Tiny pores, each guarded by two bean-shaped guard cells that open and close it. They let in CO₂, let out O₂ and water vapour.

💡 Guard cells contain chloroplasts; ordinary skin cells of the leaf do not.

🎲 Fun Corner — what went wrong?
1. A student forgot to destarch the plant before the black-strip test. The whole leaf turned blue-black. Why?
Old starch.
👀 Show answer
Starch made before the test was still in the leaf, so the result proves nothing.
2. Why is the leaf dipped in warm water after boiling in alcohol?
Alcohol makes it brittle.
👀 Show answer
To soften it so iodine can soak in evenly.
3. Bubbles from Hydrilla slow down when the lamp is moved away. Why?
Less light.
👀 Show answer
Less light → slower photosynthesis → less oxygen.
🎯 Fun Trivia
• 🌊 Tiny ocean plants and algae make about half of the world's oxygen.
• 🔬 A single leaf can have tens of thousands of stomata on each square centimetre of its lower surface.
• 🍂 In 1779, Jan Ingenhousz showed that plants give out oxygen only in light — using water plants and bubbles, just like the Hydrilla test.
