➗ Maths — Area of Triangles & Parallelograms
ShapeAreaWorked example
Squareside × side9 × 9 = 81 cm²
Rectanglelength × breadth15 × 8 = 120 cm²
Parallelogrambase × height10 × 6 = 60 cm²
Triangle½ × base × height½ × 8 × 5 = 20 cm²

Why the triangle has a ½

Two identical triangles fit together to make a parallelogram. So a triangle is exactly half of a parallelogram on the same base and height — their areas are in the ratio 1 : 2.

💡 Forgetting the ½ is the single most common mistake in this chapter. Write the formula down before you substitute.

Working backwards

If you are given the area and asked for the base or the height, rearrange the formula.

Triangle: area 45 cm², height 9 cm → base = (2 × 45) ÷ 9 = 10 cm.
Parallelogram: area 72 cm², base 12 cm → height = 72 ÷ 12 = 6 cm.

The height must be perpendicular

In both formulas, the ‘height’ is the perpendicular distance to the base — never a slanting side. In a right-angled triangle the two legs are the base and the height, which is why its area is simply ½ × leg × leg.

🏛️ History — The Gupta Empire

The dynasty

Founded by Chandragupta I around 320 CE, with Pataliputra (modern Patna) as its capital. His son Samudragupta was such a successful conqueror that historians call him the Indian Napoleon. Chandragupta II, titled Vikramaditya, brought the empire to its height.

💡 Do not confuse Chandragupta I of the Guptas (320 CE) with Chandragupta Maurya (322 BCE) — different dynasties, over six centuries apart.

Why it is called the Golden Age

Literature: Kalidasa wrote Shakuntala and Meghaduta.
Mathematics: the decimal system and the concept of zero were developed.
Astronomy: Aryabhata said the Earth rotates on its axis and calculated the value of π; Varahamihira wrote on astronomy and weather.
Medicine: Sushruta's surgical tradition flourished.
Art: the Ajanta cave paintings and the rust-free Iron Pillar of Delhi.

🏛️ Civics — The State Government

ExecutiveChief Minister and the Council of Ministers (the Governor is the constitutional head)Runs the government and carries out the laws
JudiciaryThe High CourtInterprets the laws and settles disputes

Union, State and Concurrent Lists

Union List — only Parliament can legislate (defence, foreign affairs, currency).
State List — only the state legislature (police, public health, agriculture).
Concurrent List — both may legislate (education, forests). If the two laws clash, the central law prevails.

⚡ Physics Recall — Electricity & Circuits

Circuit basics

An electric circuit is a closed path along which electricity flows. A simple circuit needs a cell (supplies energy), connecting wires, a bulb (converts electrical energy into light and heat) and a switch (opens or closes the path). A battery is two or more cells joined together.

Closed circuit → current flows → the bulb glows. Open circuit (switch off, or a broken wire) → no current → no glow.

Conductors and insulators

Conductors let current through — copper, aluminium, silver, gold, iron.
Insulators do not — rubber, plastic, wood, glass, dry cloth.

💡 That is why the wire is copper but the covering around it is plastic.

Current, and things that go wrong

Current is the rate of flow of charge, measured in amperes (A) with an ammeter. Conventional current is taken to flow from the positive to the negative terminal outside the cell. The bulb's filament is tungsten, chosen for its very high melting point. A short circuit is an accidental low-resistance path that lets a dangerously large current flow.

⚗️ Chemistry Recall — Air and Atmosphere

The 30-second version

Air is a mixture: nitrogen 78%, oxygen 21%, argon 0.93%, carbon dioxide 0.03–0.04%, plus water vapour and traces. Oxygen supports respiration and combustion; nitrogen dilutes it and makes fertilisers; carbon dioxide feeds photosynthesis and fills fire extinguishers.

Air has mass, occupies space and exerts pressure. Pollution comes mainly from burning fossil fuels; too much carbon dioxide strengthens the greenhouse effect and causes global warming.
