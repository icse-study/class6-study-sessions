Habitat & Adaptation — built for where they live 🐪🐟🦅
Can you spot a caterpillar that looks exactly like a twig? That disguise is an adaptation. Today we tour four habitats — water, desert, mountain and air — and see how plants and animals are made to survive in each.

1️⃣ Habitat and environment

Habitat

The natural home of an organism, where it gets food, water, shelter and a place to breed.

Biotic componentsAbiotic components
living — plants, animals, decomposersnon-living — soil, rocks, air, water, temperature, light
2️⃣ Adaptation

Changes that help survival

An adaptation is any feature of body or behaviour that helps an organism survive comfortably in its habitat. Adaptations develop over thousands of years, not in a day or two — organisms that can't adapt die out.

Organisms adapt to find food · escape enemies · breed successfully · cope with heat, cold or dryness

Floating plants

Some plants float freely on the surface, such as water hyacinth and duckweed; their leaf-stalks are swollen with air.

🐋 Whales

Whales are marine mammals. They have a streamlined body and front legs changed into flippers; hind limbs are absent. They have lungs, not gills, so they must come up to the surface to breathe air.

🌵 Cactus

A succulent — stores water in fleshy tissue. Leaves reduced to spines (less transpiration); the green, fleshy stem does photosynthesis and has a thick waxy coat; long, deep roots reach water.

Other desert animals

Desert rats and snakes hide in burrows by day and come out at night.

8️⃣ Seeds that fly — dispersal by wind

Why spread out?

If all seeds fell under the parent, there wouldn't be enough space, water and minerals; most would die. Dispersal carries them far away. Wind-dispersed seeds are light with silky hairs (cotton) or wings (drumstick); poppy seeds are tiny and light.

🎲 Fun Corner
1. Does a camel's hump store water?
Think again!
👀 Show answer
No — it stores fat. Oxidising the fat releases energy and some water.
2. Why can't a whale stay under water forever?
Lungs or gills?
👀 Show answer
It is a mammal with lungs — it must surface to breathe air.
3. Where are the stomata on a water lily leaf?
Which side touches air?
👀 Show answer
Only on the upper surface.
🎯 Fun Trivia
• 🚤 Vallisneria's tiny male flowers break free and float like little boats until they drift into the female flowers.
• 🐪 A thirsty camel can drink over 100 litres of water in about 10 minutes.
• 🌵 The saguaro cactus of America can grow taller than a four-storey building and live for over 150 years.
• 🦅 Some birds, like swifts, can stay in the air for months, even sleeping while they fly.
