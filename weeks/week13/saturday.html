Circles — Weekly Problem Solving
Chapter 17 · Exercise 17 style. Constructions in your head, multi-step reasoning, and the trick questions.
➗ The ideas

How to attack a circle problem

1. Draw it. Even a rough circle with the centre marked sorts out half the confusion.
2. Turn diameters into radii (or the other way round) before you start.
3. Name every line you are given — radius, chord, diameter, secant or tangent.
4. Compare distances with r whenever a point's position is asked.

💡 In construction questions, the phrase 'circle with A as centre passing through B' means AB is a radius.

Constructions worth practising

With a compass and ruler:
• Draw a circle of radius 4.2 cm; mark A on it, join AO and extend to meet the circle at B → AB is a diameter, 8.4 cm.
• Draw a circle of radius 6 cm and a chord AB = 6 cm; join OA and OB → triangle AOB is equilateral, so ∠AOB = 60°.
• Draw a line AB = 8.4 cm as a diameter, mark C on the circle and join AC, BC → ∠ACB = 90°.

A chord equal to the radius always gives a 60° angle at the centre.

The traps

• A chord of 11 cm is impossible in a circle of radius 5 cm — the diameter is only 10 cm.
• 'Twice the radius' and 'half the diameter' describe the same length.
• The major segment is the one that contains the centre.

🎉 Fun facts & trivia

An angle drawn in a semi-circle is always a right angle — a fact known as Thales' theorem, after the Greek thinker Thales of Miletus, around 600 BCE. Mark any point on a semi-circle, join it to both ends of the diameter, and you get exactly 90°.

Bicycle wheels were once made in wildly different sizes — the penny-farthing had a front wheel of nearly 1.5 m across, because a bigger wheel travelled further per pedal turn before gears were invented.

The Romans laid out their army camps using a circle-based instrument called a groma; straight roads came from carefully placed circles.

📜 Part 4 of 12 — Why Wheels Beat Rollers

Before wheels, heavy stones were moved on rollers — loose logs placed under a sledge. It worked, but someone had to keep running to the back to fetch the log that popped out and carry it to the front.

The wheel-and-axle fixed that by keeping the round thing attached to the cart. The catch was friction at the axle, which is why early carts were slow. The real leap came thousands of years later with the ball bearing — tiny spheres that let the axle roll instead of rub.

Every one of those improvements depends on the same property: constant distance from a centre.

Filtration, evaporation & distillation
Concise Chemistry pages 68–73. Filtering out solids, recovering a dissolved solid, getting pure water, removing a dissolved gas — and choosing the right method for any mixture.

1️⃣ Filtration

Passing a mixture through a filter

For an insoluble solid in a liquid. The solid left on the filter is the residue; the liquid that passes through is the filtrate.

Filters: filter paper, cotton, a layer of sand or charcoal, glass wool, muslin cloth, a tea strainer

🧪 Activity — chalk and water

Fold a round filter paper in half, then half again, open it into a cone and fit it in a funnel. Pour chalky water down a glass rod. Chalk stays as residue; clear filtrate drips into the beaker.

2️⃣ Evaporation

Liquid → vapour, leaving the solid behind

For a soluble solid (a solution). Heat it in an evaporating dish on a wire gauze and tripod stand over a Bunsen burner.

Salt from sea water — collected in shallow beds and dried by the sun.
💡 Evaporation is faster on heating. Disadvantage: the liquid is lost to the air.
3️⃣ Distillation

Evaporate, then condense

Boil the solution → vapour → cool it back into liquid in another container. Both the solid and the pure liquid are recovered.

Tap water → distilled water, used by doctors for medicines, chemists for solutions, in industries and car batteries.

4️⃣ A gas dissolved in a liquid

Just heat it

Dissolved gas escapes on heating. Boiled water tastes flat because the dissolved air has escaped. Warm soda water fizzes out its CO₂.

Two liquids that don't mix

Oil and water form layers. A separating funnel lets the lower layer drain out through the tap.

6️⃣ Multi-step puzzles

Sand + salt + water

1. Filter → sand is the residue. 2. Evaporate (or distil) the filtrate → salt.

Case study — Zainab's rice

Rice + small stones + spilled salt.
1. Hand-pick the stones.
2. Add water — salt dissolves, rice doesn't.
3. Decant or filter to get the rice.
4. Evaporate the salty water to get the salt back.

5. Why can't sand and sugar be separated by distillation?
Distillation needs a liquid.
👀 Show answer
Both are solids. Add water, filter out sand, then evaporate to get sugar.
6. Why is distilled water not good for drinking every day?
What's been removed?
👀 Show answer
It has no dissolved minerals or air, so it tastes flat and lacks minerals.
🎯 Fun Trivia
• Crude petroleum is separated by fractional distillation in tall towers into LPG, petrol, kerosene, diesel and wax.
• Many countries in the Middle East get drinking water by desalination — removing salt from sea water.
• Cream is separated from milk in dairies by spinning it very fast in a machine called a centrifuge.
