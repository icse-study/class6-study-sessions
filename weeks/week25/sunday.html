Rivers of North America & Lumberjacks of Canada — think and write

1. Describe the specialised roles and physical hazards faced by workers such as high riggers and haulers in the Canadian lumbering industry.
👀 Show model answer
High riggers climb tall conifers in spiked shoes with a safety belt and cut off branches and the top — the riskiest job (falls from great height). Sawyers/buckers cut logs to size (4.5–5 m for saw mills, 1–1.5 m for pulp). Skidders drag logs to roads, railways or river banks. Haulers float logs downstream in spring, walking on floating logs in spiked boots and clearing jams with spiked poles; loose logs can roll over them, often fatally, so they must be quick, daring and vigilant.
2. What does the term "lumbering" mean? Why is lumbering a major occupation in Canada?
👀 Show model answer
Harvesting timber and cutting it into required shapes and sizes. Canada has vast coniferous forests (60% of land) of softwood, plus hydroelectric power, mechanised mills, good transport and a small population — making it the leading wood exporter (~31% of world forest products).
3. Why do you think a lumberjack's life is full of hardships?
👀 Show model answer
They live in isolated shanties for months in extreme cold; permanent frost makes building hard; climbing, sawing and log-floating are dangerous; they were far from family and once walked long distances home.
4. What are the favourite pastimes of a lumberjack after a hard day's work?
👀 Show model answer
Swapping stories and socialising with fellow workers in the camp.
5. How has the transport system for lumberjacks improved over the years? What other changes have occurred in their lives?
👀 Show model answer
Earlier they walked many kilometres home; now they use planes, snowmobiles and buses. Houses are more comfortable than crude log cabins; chainsaws, tractors and dams make work easier; most now live in settled communities; some became wealthy lumber barons.
6. Classify the rivers of North America by the water body they drain into.
👀 Show model answer
Gulf of Mexico — Mississippi (Missouri, Arkansas, Ohio), Rio Grande. Hudson Bay/Arctic — short Shield rivers, Mackenzie. Pacific — Columbia, Colorado. Atlantic — St Lawrence, short Appalachian rivers.
7. Describe the Grand Canyon and Niagara Falls.
👀 Show model answer
Grand Canyon: deep, steep-sided valley cut by the Colorado River through the Colorado Plateau, over 1,500 m deep; famous for scenic beauty. Niagara Falls: on the Niagara River between Lake Erie and Lake Ontario.
8. Give five reasons for Canada's highly developed lumbering industry.
👀 Show model answer
Abundant hydroelectric power; enormous coniferous softwood resources; modern mechanised mills; excellent rail and water transport; relatively small population.
9. What similarities do you see between the forests of Canada and the rainforests of Brazil? (Preview of South America)
👀 Show model answer
Both are huge forests vital for timber, wildlife and absorbing carbon dioxide, and both face over-exploitation (Nova Scotia deforested; Amazon logging and fires).
10. Why is the Mississippi called the most important river of North America?
👀 Show model answer
It drains a huge part of the fertile Great Plains with major tributaries (Missouri, Ohio, Arkansas), flows to the Gulf of Mexico and supports farming, navigation and settlements; the Mississippi region also gave rise to Blues music.
