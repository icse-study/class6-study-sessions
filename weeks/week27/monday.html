📖 English Literature · Week 27
Ch 8 · What Is This Game? — and the language of Chapter 8
Javed Akhtar’s thoughtful poem about a chess player lost in questions, plus the chapter’s grammar: conjunctions, the hyphen, -tion/-sion/-cion/-cian, phrasal verbs, repetition and notice writing.
♟️ The poem

The speaker’s opponent has moved and “awaits mine”. But he stares at the black and white pieces / that sit on the white and black squares and wonders: What are these pieces?

• If they are only wooden toys, what is the point of victory or defeat? No pleasure in a win, no sorrow in a loss.
• If he must play, he must convince himself they are a real King and Queen, foot soldiers, with an enemy army arrayed before them that plans to destroy him.
• But then it is no longer a game — it is a war that must be won, “in which all is fair”. A war, but one between players; a game, but one like war.
• He questions the rules: why must the King be protected at all times? Why can only the Queen move in any direction? Why can a pawn that leaves home never return? “If this is the rule / Then what are rules?”
• He has been wrestling with these questions — and meanwhile his opponent has made another move, and awaits his.

Form: free verse (no rhyme scheme, lines of different length). Repetition: “Then what is this game?”, “This is a war”, “This game” — to stress the thoughts that fill his mind.

♞ Chess facts

64 squares — 32 black and 32 white · each side: king, queen, 2 rooks, 2 bishops, 2 knights, 8 pawns · a pawn moves one square forward (two on its first move) and never backwards · the queen is the most powerful piece · checkmate = the king is attacked and cannot escape, which ends the game · chess began in India as chaturanga.

✏️ Grammar of Chapter 8

Coordinating conjunctions join equals — FANBOYS: for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so.
Subordinating join a dependent clause: when, while, because, after, before, as, since, though, although, if, that, till, unless.
Correlative (pairs): either…or, neither…nor, both…and, not only…but also, whether…or, no sooner…than, scarcely…when. The verb agrees with the nearer subject: “Either my uncle or my parents come.”
Hyphen: joins words (kind-hearted, sister-in-law, do-or-die) · numbers 21–99 in words (eighty-nine) · splits a word at the end of a line.
Endings: -tion (sanitation, exhibition, tuition) · -sion (tension, conversion) · -cion (suspicion, coercion) · -cian for people (musician, politician, magician, physician).
Phrasal verbs: break off (stop speaking) · come after (chase to punish) · go through (examine carefully) · let out (allow to leave) · drop out (leave school early) · run out of (use up).
Notice: short, formal, for a target group — title, event, date, time, venue, who to contact, issuer’s name and designation.

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Javed Akhtar (born 1945)

Indian poet, lyricist and screenwriter.

🌟 Fun facts
• With his partner Salim Khan (“Salim–Javed”) he wrote hit films such as Zanjeer, Deewaar, Sholay, Don and Mr. India.
• His Urdu poetry collection Lava won the Sahitya Akademi Award in 2013.
• He has written hundreds of film songs (Lagaan, Kal Ho Naa Ho, Veer-Zaara, Jodhaa Akbar) and won five National Film Awards.
• Padma Shri (1999) and Padma Bhushan (2007).
• His father, Jan Nisar Akhtar, was also a poet; his children Farhan and Zoya Akhtar are film-makers.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. When, according to the speaker, does a game of chess become “a war”?
👀 Show model answer
It becomes a war when he convinces himself that the pieces are not wooden toys but a real king, queen and foot soldiers, facing an enemy army that wants to destroy him. Then winning matters so much that “all is fair”. He realises that to care about the game he must take it seriously — yet then it is no longer just a game.
