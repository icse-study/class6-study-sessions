Circles — Segment, Semi-circle, Interior & Exterior
Chapter 17 · Sections 17.8–17.9. What a chord cuts off, and how the plane splits into inside and outside.
➗ The ideas

Segment

Every chord divides a circle into two parts, and each part is a segment.

A segment is the region enclosed by a chord and an arc. The smaller part is the minor segment, the larger the major segment.

The major segment always contains the centre O.
💡 That is the quickest test in an exam: find the centre, and the part holding it is the major segment.

Semi-circle

When the chord happens to be a diameter, the two segments are equal, and each is a semi-circle.

If AB is a diameter, then APB and AQB are semi-circles.
💡 So a semi-circle is the borderline case: neither piece is bigger.

Interior and exterior

The plane around a circle splits into three parts:
• the interior — all points inside (distance from centre less than r),
• the circle itself — the points at distance exactly r,
• the exterior — all points outside (distance more than r).

The centre O always lies in the interior.
🎉 Fun facts & trivia

Stadium running tracks are built from two straights and two semi-circular ends, which is why runners in outer lanes start further ahead — the outer semi-circles are longer.

The rainbow you see is part of a circle. From the ground you usually catch an arc, but from an aeroplane a rainbow can appear as a complete circle.

A circular arch is stronger than a flat beam because the load is pushed outward along the curve — the reason Roman aqueducts still stand after 2,000 years.

📜 Part 6 of 12 — The Circle in the Sky

Ancient astronomers believed the heavens were made of perfect circles, because a circle is the one shape that never changes as it turns. Every planet, they said, must move on a circle.

The trouble was that Mars sometimes appeared to go backwards. To rescue the theory, astronomers added circles rolling on circles, called epicycles — dozens of them.

Only in 1609 did Johannes Kepler admit the awkward truth: planetary orbits are ellipses, not circles. A circle is simply the special case of an ellipse whose two focus points sit on top of each other.

1️⃣ Force

Definition

Force is that cause which changes the state of rest or motion of a body, or its direction of motion, or its size or shape.

push · pull · stretch · squeeze
2️⃣ The six effects of force
EffectNew example
moves a body at resta goalkeeper's kick sends the ball flying
stops a moving bodya fielder catches a ball
speeds it upa cyclist pedals harder
slows it downa parachute opens
changes its directiona batsman hits a ball to the boundary
changes its shape or sizekneading dough; pressing clay; stretching a rubber band

What force can't do

A force never changes the mass of a body. And we can't see a force — only its effect.

Units

S.I. unit: newton (N). Gravitational units: kgf, gf.
1 kgf = 1000 gf · 1 kgf ≈ 10 N. Weight is a force → kgf. Mass → kg.

5️⃣ Friction

Definition and cause

Friction opposes the relative motion between two surfaces in contact. It is caused by the interlocking of irregular projections on the surfaces, and always acts opposite to the motion.

EffectsDepends onDoes not depend on
opposes motionsmoothness of surfacesarea of contact
produces heatweight of the body
causes wear and tearmedium: solid > liquid > gas
6️⃣ Kinds of friction

Static > Sliding > Rolling

Static — maximum force while the body stays still; self-adjusting.
Sliding — while it slides; less than static.
Rolling — while it rolls; the least.

💡 F₁ to start sliding, F₂ to keep sliding, F₃ to roll → F₁ > F₂ > F₃.
7️⃣ More friction or less?
Reduce frictionIncrease friction
polish the surfacesmake surfaces rough (treads, spikes, sand)
lubricants: oil, grease, powderkeep surfaces dry

streamlining

Necessary evil

We need it to walk, write, brake, hold things, light a match, and keep a ladder against a wall.
It costs us efficiency, heat and wear and tear.

1️⃣ Algorithms

A step-by-step recipe to solve a problem

A good algorithm has clear steps, a definite start and end, and always gives an answer.

Algorithm: find the bigger of two numbers
1. Start
2. Read A and B
3. If A > B, print A
4. Otherwise print B
5. Stop
2️⃣ Flowcharts — algorithms as pictures
SymbolNameUsed for

Flowchart: is a number even?

💡 A diamond always has two exits — Yes and No. That is exactly an if / else in Python!

5️⃣ Debugging tips

How to fix bugs like a pro

1. Read the error message — it tells you the line number and the type.
2. Check brackets, quotes, colons and indentation.
3. Add print() lines to see what your variables hold.
4. Test with simple values you can check by hand.
5. Change one thing at a time.
