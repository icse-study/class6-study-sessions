🔢 Numbers & factors

Smallest & greatest numbers

The smallest 5-digit number is 10,000 — but if all digits must be different, you cannot repeat the zeros. Start with the smallest allowed leading digit (1), then use the remaining smallest digits in order.

Smallest 5-digit number with different digits = 10,234. (Not 01234 — a number cannot begin with 0.)

Prime factorisation

Keep dividing by the smallest prime that fits until you reach 1, then write the factors as a product of primes.

36 ÷ 2 = 18 → 18 ÷ 2 = 9 → 9 ÷ 3 = 3 → 3 ÷ 3 = 1
So 36 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 = 2² × 3².

Place value × face value

Face value is the digit itself. Place value is the digit × the value of its place. A question can ask you to multiply the two.

In 4,52,100 the 5 is in the ten-thousands place → place value 50,000, face value 5.
Product = 50,000 × 5 = 2,50,000.

LCM

The LCM is the smallest number that every one of them divides into. Use prime factors and take the highest power of each.

8 = 2³ and 12 = 2² × 3 → take 2³ × 3 = 24.
🍕 Fractions & decimals

Fraction → decimal

Make the denominator 10, 100 or 1000 if you can, then read off the decimal.

7/20 → multiply top and bottom by 5 → 35/100 = 0.35.

Decimal → fraction in lowest terms

Write the digits over 10, 100 or 1000 (one zero per decimal place), then divide top and bottom by their HCF.

0.65 = 65/100. HCF of 65 and 100 is 5 → 13/20.

Equivalent fractions & like fractions

To build an equivalent fraction, multiply the numerator and denominator by the same number. To add like fractions (same denominator), add the numerators only and keep the denominator — then reduce.

3/5 with denominator 25: 5 × 5 = 25, so 3 × 5 = 15 → 15/25.
4/9 + 2/9 = 6/9 = 2/3 (divide both by 3).
➕ Integers, BODMAS & more

Adding integers & additive inverse

Same signs → add and keep the sign. The additive inverse of a number is the number that adds to it to give 0 — just flip the sign.

(−8) + (−12) = −20 (both negative, so add and stay negative).
Additive inverse of −78 = +78, because −78 + 78 = 0.

Nested brackets

Work from the innermost bracket outwards: round ( ) first, then curly { }, then square [ ].

18 − [5 + {6 − (5 − 4)}]
= 18 − [5 + {6 − 1}] = 18 − [5 + 5] = 18 − 10 = 8

Roman numerals

I = 1, V = 5, X = 10, L = 50, C = 100. A smaller symbol before a larger one means subtract.

94 = 90 + 4 = XC + IV = XCIV. (Not LXXXXIIII — you may not repeat a symbol more than three times.)

📜 ಸಂದರ್ಭ · Reference to context

The four-part answer

A ಸಂದರ್ಭ question gives you one line from the lesson and asks you to place it. A full answer always has four parts:

1. ಆಯ್ಕೆ — which lesson and who wrote it.
2. ಸಂದರ್ಭ — what is happening at that moment in the story.
3. ಯಾರು ಯಾರಿಗೆ — who says it, and to whom.
4. ಸ್ವಾರಸ್ಯ — why the line matters, or what it reveals.

💡 Never answer a ಸಂದರ್ಭ question with just the meaning of the line — the marks are for the context around it.

ಗದ್ಯ and ಪದ್ಯ

ಗದ್ಯ (gadya) = prose, written by a ಲೇಖಕ (lekhaka, author). ಪದ್ಯ (padya) = poetry, written by a ಕವಿ (kavi, poet). The lesson's message is its ನೀತಿ (neeti, moral).

✍️ ವ್ಯಾಕರಣ · Grammar the questions test

ಲಿಂಗ · Opposite gender pairs

ರಾಜ → ರಾಣಿ (king → queen) · ಹುಡುಗ → ಹುಡುಗಿ · ಅಣ್ಣ → ಅಕ್ಕ · ತಂದೆ → ತಾಯಿ · ಗಂಡ → ಹೆಂಡತಿ

ವಚನ · Singular → plural

Things take -ಗಳು; people usually take -ರು.

ಮರ → ಮರಗಳು · ಪುಸ್ತಕ → ಪುಸ್ತಕಗಳು · ಹೂವು → ಹೂವುಗಳು
ಹುಡುಗ → ಹುಡುಗರು · ಶಿಕ್ಷಕ → ಶಿಕ್ಷಕರು

ವಾಕ್ಯ ರಚನೆ · Building a sentence

Kannada runs subject → object → verb, so the verb comes last.

ನನಗೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಸಂತೋಷವಾಯಿತು. — I felt very happy.
ಭಾರತ ನನ್ನ ಮಾತೃಭೂಮಿ. — India is my motherland.
ಶುಭೋದಯ, ಗುರುಗಳೇ. — Good morning, teacher.

📕 Textbook questions — not scored
These depend on the lesson your class is on right now, so they are not in the quiz. Answer them in your notebook with the textbook open, then tap each one to check how a full-mark answer should be built.
1. Name the author or poet of your current prose / poetry lesson.
How to answer ▾
Give the full name and say whether they are a ಲೇಖಕ (prose) or a ಕವಿ (poetry). Add one line about them if your textbook gives it.
2. What is the central moral theme (ನೀತಿ) of the lesson studied this week?
How to answer ▾
State the moral in one sentence, then give one incident from the lesson that shows it. A moral without evidence loses marks.
3. Who is the central character in the current prose lesson?
How to answer ▾
Name the character, say what their role is, and give two qualities with a moment from the story that proves each one.
4. Why did the main character undertake that journey or action? (one sentence)
How to answer ▾
Give the reason plus what they hoped would happen — a cause and a purpose, joined in one sentence.
5. Fill in the blank from the poem: "ಕನ್ನಡ ನಾಡು ________ ಬೀಡು."
How to answer ▾
Check the exact word in your textbook — the wording differs between readers. ಬೀಡು means abode or dwelling place, so the missing word describes what kind of home the Kannada land is.
6. Identify the speaker of the line: "ನಾನು ನಾಳೆ ಖಂಡಿತ ಬರುತ್ತೇನೆ."
How to answer ▾
Use the four-part ಸಂದರ್ಭ answer above: lesson and author, what is happening, who says it to whom, and why it matters.
7. What lesson do we learn from the character's behaviour in the chapter?
How to answer ▾
Name the behaviour, name the lesson, and connect it to your own life in the last sentence.

📖 English Literature
RTC, character sketches & poetic devices
The tools you need for any prose or poem: how to answer Reference to Context, how to build a character sketch, the figures of speech, and rhyme schemes. Chapter-specific questions are in the practice list at the bottom.
🔍 Reference to Context (RTC)

The four things every RTC answer needs

1. The source — the title of the story or poem and its author.
2. The speaker and listener — who says it, and to whom.
3. The context — what is happening just before this line.
4. The meaning or significance — what the line tells us about the character or the plot.

For a line such as "He looked at the sky and wondered if it would hold", identify who 'he' is, then explain that he is anxious about the weather — and say why that matters for what he is about to do.
💡 Pronouns like he, she, it, they in an RTC line are always a hint: your first job is to name who they refer to.

Character sketch

A good sketch names traits, not events. For each trait, give one piece of evidence from the text. Then describe the change — how the character is different at the end from the beginning, and what caused it.

Setting, plot and conflict

Setting = the time and place in which the story happens. Plot = the sequence of events. Conflict = the main problem the protagonist faces. Climax = the turning point of highest tension. Theme = the central message.

The protagonist is the main character; the antagonist is whoever or whatever opposes them.

Rhyme scheme

Label the last word of each line with a letter. Lines that rhyme get the same letter; a new sound gets the next letter.

AABB — lines 1&2 rhyme, lines 3&4 rhyme (couplets).
ABAB — alternate lines rhyme.
ABCB — only lines 2 and 4 rhyme.
A group of lines in a poem is a stanza.

📝 From your own chapter
📕 Textbook questions — not scored
These depend on the story or poem your class is reading now, so they are not in the quiz. Write the answers in your notebook, then tap each one for how to structure it.
1. What is the title of the current story or poem, and who wrote it?
How to answer ▾
Title in quotation marks or underlined, then the author's full name. Say whether it is prose or poetry.
2. Describe the setting — the time and the place.
How to answer ▾
Give both: when (period, season, time of day) and where (country, town, indoors/outdoors). Add one detail from the text that shows it.
3. What is the main conflict the protagonist faces?
How to answer ▾
Name the protagonist, state the problem in one sentence, and say whether it is against a person, nature, or their own feelings.
4. How does the character's attitude change from the beginning to the end?
How to answer ▾
Three steps: how they were at the start → the event that changed them → how they are at the end.
5. Why did the character make that choice at the climax?
How to answer ▾
Give the motive, not just the action. Link it back to a trait you would put in their character sketch.
6. What is the significance of the title?
How to answer ▾
Explain both meanings if there are two — what the title says literally, and what it suggests about the theme.
7. Identify the rhyme scheme of the first stanza of your poem.
How to answer ▾
Write the last word of each line, mark rhyming ones with the same letter, then give the pattern — AABB, ABAB, ABCB and so on.
8. Write a 3-sentence summary of the middle section of the prose lesson.
How to answer ▾
One sentence per event, in order, past tense, no dialogue and no opinions. Only what happened.
