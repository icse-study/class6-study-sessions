🌍 Geography · Ch 5 Minerals · Part 1
Minerals — Rocks, Minerals & Ores
Everything from your pencil "lead" to the steel in your school bus comes from the Earth's crust. This week: what rocks, minerals and ores are, how humans have used them since the Stone Age, and why they must be conserved.

🪨 Geology & geologists

Geology is the study of rocks and minerals that form the Earth. Scientists who study the Earth's physical structure, its history and the processes acting on it are geologists. A geologist is consulted when building houses, bridges and towers or digging a tube well.

🧱 Rocks

Rocks are the solid materials that make up the crust. A rock is made of minerals — either one mineral (e.g. limestone) or, more commonly, two or more (e.g. granite).

💎 Minerals

Minerals are naturally occurring useful substances with definite physical properties and chemical composition. They are inorganic compounds or elements — the building blocks of the Earth.
Common rock-forming minerals: iron, silica, quartz, feldspar, mica, nickel, magnesium, sulphur, calcium.

💡 Physical properties: colour, hardness, lustre (shine), texture.

⛏️ Ores

Ores are rocks containing metallic minerals from which metals are extracted — e.g. iron ore, manganese ore.

🕰️ Minerals through the ages

Stone Age — flint stones for tools and fire.
Bronze Age — the age of metals began with alloys of copper and tin.
Iron Age followed.
Mining moved from small-scale hand mining to large-scale machine mining, which has diminished reserves considerably.

⚡ Why minerals matter

They are important sources of energy; used in electrical and engineering industries, as fuel in transport, and in buildings and construction.

♻️ Non-renewable!

Minerals are non-renewable — they take millions of years to form again. So they must be used judiciously and preserved.

🏛️ Limestone → marble

Under heat and pressure, limestone changes into marble, a more compact rock. The Taj Mahal was built by Shah Jahan of white marble.

🎯 Must-remember facts
• Rock = made of minerals (limestone: one; granite: many)
• Mineral = natural, inorganic, definite properties & composition
• Ore = rock with metallic minerals → metal extracted
• Stone Age (flint) → Bronze Age (copper + tin) → Iron Age
• Non-renewable → use judiciously

✍️ English Grammar · Ch 8 Question Tags
Question Tags — isn't it? don't you? shall we?
A question tag is a mini-question stuck to the end of a statement to check or confirm: You're a dancer, aren't you? Master three steps — flip positive/negative, copy the helping verb, use a pronoun — and watch out for the famous exceptions.

🔄 The flip rule

• Positive statement → negative tag (you expect 'Yes'): She likes coffee, doesn't she?
• Negative statement → positive tag (you expect 'No'): She doesn't like coffee, does she?
The tag is in the same tense and is separated by a comma.

💡 Plus statement → minus tag. Minus statement → plus tag.

🔧 Copy the helping verb

• You'll be there, won't you?
• They've already seen it, haven't they?
• Ram won't talk, will he?
• We should meet, shouldn't we?
No helping verb? Use do/does/did:
• Maya writes novels, doesn't she?
• Raghu missed his flight, didn't he?
• Foxes don't eat vegetables, do they?

👤 Always a pronoun

The tag uses a pronoun, never the name: Mrs Rao will help us, won't she? My cousin and you are engaged, aren't you? (you + someone = you) · There wasn't enough paper, was there? · Nobody came, did they? · Everyone is here, aren't they? · This is yours, isn't it?

🚫 Hidden negatives

Words like never, hardly, seldom, scarcely, barely, no, nobody, nothing, few, little make the statement negative → the tag is positive.
• She never loses her temper, does she?
• He hardly works, does he?
• Nothing happened, did it?

💡 Never = negative → positive tag.

⭐ Famous exceptions

• I am late, aren't I? (not amn't I)
• Let's meet in the evening, shall we?
• Imperative: Keep quiet, will you? / Open the door, won't you?
• Don't be late, will you?
• I can't now, can I?

🇬🇧 HAVE as main verb

Leena has two dogs, doesn't she? (modern usage) — older British English also allows hasn't she. We have plenty of time, don't we?
But when have is a helping verb: We have finished the work, haven't we?

✨ Fun facts
• In Indian English people often use one tag for everything: 'You are coming, isn't it?' Correct English needs 'aren't you?'
• Many languages use one fixed tag: French says n'est-ce pas?, German nicht wahr?, Hindi na?
• 'Aren't I?' is unusual because 'am not' never contracted neatly. 'Ain't I' was once common but is now seen as informal.
• Your voice matters: a tag said with falling tone just wants agreement; with rising tone it is a real question.

🎯 Must-remember rules
• Positive statement → negative tag; negative statement → positive tag
• Copy the helping verb; if none, use do/does/did
• Pronoun only: Mrs Rao → she; there → there; nobody/everyone → they
• never, hardly, seldom, nobody = negative → positive tag
• I am → aren't I · Let's → shall we · Imperative → will you
