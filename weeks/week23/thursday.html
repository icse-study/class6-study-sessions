⚗️ Chemistry · Week 23 · Ch 6 Air and Atmosphere (Part 1)
Air — the invisible mixture that keeps us alive
We can live without food or water for some time, but not without air for even a few minutes. This week: the atmosphere, what air is made of, the experiments that prove each component is present, and oxygen's two big jobs — respiration and burning.

🌍 The atmosphere

A thick blanket of air up to about 320 km; most of it lies within 15 km of the surface, and it thins with height. It protects us from harmful rays and stops the earth getting too hot by day or too cold by night.
Air is colourless, odourless, tasteless and transparent. Moving air = wind.

📊 Composition (by volume)

Nitrogen 78% · Oxygen 21% · Inert gases 0.9% · Carbon dioxide 0.03–0.04% · water vapour, dust and impurities vary.
Lavoisier (1774) showed air is a mixture, not an element.
Properties: occupies space, has mass, easily compressed, presses equally in all directions.

🧾 Proof: air is a mixture

1. Composition varies (more water vapour in the rainy season)
2. No energy change when its gases mix
3. Each gas keeps its own properties
4. No formula
5. Can be separated by physical methods

🕯️ Experiments for each component

• Candle in a jar over water: flame goes out, water rises about 1/5 → oxygen is the active part; the remaining 4/5 (mainly nitrogen) is inactive
• Lime water turns milky when air is bubbled → carbon dioxide (lime water = calcium hydroxide; milkiness = calcium carbonate)
• Ice-cold glass gets droplets outside → water vapour
• Sunbeam in a dark room shows floating specks → dust

💡 Nitrogen doesn't support burning but controls it — without it, everything would burn fiercely.

🫁 Respiration

A chemical process in all living things: oxygen reacts with digested food to release energy.
Food (glucose) + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water + energy
Plants respire too (germinating seeds turn lime water milky). Fish use oxygen dissolved in water; earthworms use air trapped in soil — they surface in heavy rain. Mountaineers, divers, miners and astronauts carry oxygen.

🔥 Burning & fuels

Burning (combustion): a substance reacts with oxygen, releasing heat and light — fast. A candle under a glass goes out once its oxygen is used up.
Fuels: wood, charcoal, coal, petrol, kerosene, diesel, CNG (compressed natural gas, mostly methane; cheap, low-polluting), LPG (liquefied petroleum gas, butane + isobutane; cooking gas). LPG and CNG leave no residue.
Respiration vs burning: both need O2 and make CO2 + water. Respiration is slow, natural, continuous, at body temperature, gives small energy as heat; burning is fast, needs ignition, gives lots of heat and light.

✨ Fun facts
• At the top of Mount Everest, each breath carries only about one-third of the oxygen you get at sea level.
• A typical adult breathes in and out about 11,000 litres of air every day.
• Doctors once sent patients to hill stations to recover — the air there has more oxygen and fewer pollutants.
• Lime water turning milky is still used in labs today as the standard test for CO2.
