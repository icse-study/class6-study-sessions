⚗️ Chemistry · Week 26 · Ch 7 Water (Part 2)
Safe water — purify it, protect it, save it
Water looks clean but may hide germs and dissolved salts. This week: why ice floats, what makes water potable, how cities and homes purify water, water-borne diseases, pollution, and how each of us can conserve water.

🧊 Anomalous expansion

Most substances are heavier as solids, but ice is lighter than water, so it floats. Water is heaviest at 4 °C; below that it gets lighter, and at 0 °C it freezes.
Ponds and lakes freeze only at the top; water below stays liquid at about 4 °C, so fish survive winter.

🚰 Potable water

Potable = fit for drinking. It should be colourless and odourless, transparent, free from germs and suspended impurities, and contain some minerals, salts and dissolved gases for taste (CO2 gives taste).
Sea water is not potable — too much salt, causes vomiting.

🦠 Water-borne diseases & sterilisation

Cholera, typhoid, dysentery, gastroenteritis, jaundice spread through germ-laden water.
Sterilisation = removing germs: boiling · air and sunlight · chemicals: chlorine, ozone (ozonisation), bleaching powder in tanks, chlorinated swimming pools

🏙️ City water works

1. Loading & sedimentation — potash alum settles suspended matter
2. Filtration — through fine sand, charcoal, fine gravel, coarse gravel, stones
3. Aeration — air blown in under pressure kills microbes
4. Chlorination — kills remaining germs
5. Storage — tank, then pipes to homes
Tap water is still a mixture: it leaves rings of dissolved solids when evaporated on a watch glass.

🏠 At home & distillation

Boil it · filter through fine muslin · water purifiers and R.O. systems (carbon powder traps impurities) · 1 chlorine tablet per 25 litres · potassium permanganate crystals or ozone for stored water.
Distilled water (Liebig condenser) is the purest, but not good for drinking: no minerals and tasteless. Used for medicines, labs and car batteries.

🌏 Pollution, conservation, floods & drought

Pollution: domestic sewage, farm chemicals (pesticides, fungicides), industrial effluents. Lack of dissolved oxygen that kills fish = eutrophication. Control: effluent and sewage treatment plants, cover wells, no washing or bathing animals near sources, biodegradable detergents, trees along banks.
Conserve: close taps, fix leaks, drip irrigation, reuse water, rainwater harvesting (roof water piped to underground tanks). World Water Day: 22 March. UN minimum: 50 L per person per day.
Flood = overflow from heavy rain (soil erosion, damage). Drought = long dry spell (crops fail, water table falls).

💡 Reduce, reuse, recycle.
✨ Fun facts
• A dripping tap can waste over 20 litres of water a day.
• Cherrapunji and Mawsynram in Meghalaya are among the wettest places on earth, yet face water shortages in winter.
• Chennai made rooftop rainwater harvesting compulsory for buildings in 2003.
• In a village an Indian uses about 12 litres of water a day; in some cities it can be over 200 litres.

🎯 Must-remember
• Ice floats; water densest at 4 °C
• Potable = drinkable; CO₂ adds taste
• Sterilise: boil, sunlight, chlorine, ozone
• City: alum → sand filter → aeration → chlorination → storage
• Distilled water: purest, tasteless, for labs & batteries
• Effluents = industrial waste; eutrophication = O₂ loss
