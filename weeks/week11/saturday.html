🏛️ History — The Early Vedic Period

Who the Aryans were

The Aryans migrated from Central Asia into India around 1500 BCE. They first settled in the Sapta Sindhu — the land of seven rivers, roughly modern Punjab — and later moved east into the Ganga–Yamuna plains, which they called Aryavarta.

The Vedas

The Vedas are our oldest literary source. The Rigveda is the oldest of the four, with 1,028 hymns; the others are the Yajurveda, the Samaveda and the Atharvaveda.

The period takes its name from these texts: everything we know about it comes from what the hymns describe.

Political life

The tribe was led by the rajan, chosen for his strength and wisdom rather than by birth alone. He was assisted by the senani (commander of the army), the purohita (chief priest and adviser) and the gramani (village head).

Two popular assemblies, the sabha and the samiti, advised the rajan and checked his power. A third assembly, the vidhata, included women.

Social life

People lived in joint families (kula), which were patriarchal. Women were respected — some composed hymns of the Rigveda, took part in assemblies and debates, and chose their own husbands through swayamvara. There was no child marriage and no sati in this period.

The varna system had four groups — Brahmins (priests), Kshatriyas (warriors), Vaishyas (traders and farmers) and Shudras (servants) — but it was flexible: occupation was not fixed by birth, and people of different varnas could marry and dine together.

💡 Flexible in the Early Vedic period, rigid and birth-based in the Later Vedic period. That change is the exam question.

Economy and religion

Cattle were wealth — a rich man was one with many cows, and even wars were often cattle raids. People farmed, made crafts and traded. Metals in use were copper and bronze; iron came into common use only in the Later Vedic period.

The chief gods were Indra (rain and war), Agni (fire) and Varuna. Worship took the form of yajnas, or sacrifices, with no temples and no idols.

What they share

Both are elected bodies with reserved seats for women and for SC/ST. Both carry out the same obligatory duties — sanitation, water, roads, health and primary education — and both depend heavily on grants from the state government on top of their own local taxes.

✍️ Compare the role of a Gram Panchayat with that of a Municipal Corporation.
Model answer →
Both are elected local bodies that look after the everyday needs of the people — water, cleanliness, roads, health and primary schools — and both reserve seats for women and for Scheduled Castes and Tribes. The Gram Panchayat serves a single village or a small group of villages, is headed by a Sarpanch, and concentrates on farming, wells, cattle care and village records. A Municipal Corporation serves a city of more than ten lakh people, is headed by a Mayor with a state-appointed Commissioner as its real executive, and handles much larger tasks such as city water works, sewage, street lighting, traffic and building regulations. The Panchayat answers to the Gram Sabha; the Corporation answers to the state government.
✍️ Why was the varna system flexible in the Early Vedic period, and how did it change later?
Model answer →
In the Early Vedic period the four varnas described what people did, not what they were born as. A man's son could take up a different occupation, families of different varnas could marry, and people ate together freely; the hymns themselves mention a family with a priest, a physician and a grinder of corn. In the Later Vedic period the divisions hardened. Birth, rather than work, decided a person's varna; marriage and dining between varnas were forbidden; the Brahmins and Kshatriyas gained privileges while the Shudras were pushed to the bottom. What began as a division of labour became the rigid caste system.
