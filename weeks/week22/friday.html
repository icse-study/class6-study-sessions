Driving the microscope 🔬
A cell scientist is only as good as her microscope skills. Learn the parts, how to focus, how to make a slide — and then meet the tiny organisms that are just one cell.
1️⃣ Parts of a compound microscope
PartJob
eyepiece (ocular lens)the lens you look through; usually 10×
objective lenseslow power (10×) and high power (40×) lenses near the slide
stage and clipshold the slide flat
coarse adjustment knobbig movements to bring the object into view
fine adjustment knobsmall movements for a sharp image
light source / mirrorsends light up through the slide

Magnification

Total magnification = eyepiece × objective. A 10× eyepiece with a 40× objective magnifies 400 times.

💡 Always focus on low power first, then switch to high power and use only the fine knob.
2️⃣ Making a good slide
SpecimenStainWhat you see
cheek cells (scraped gently)methylene blueirregular flat cells, blue nucleus, no cell wall
onion peeldilute iodinebrick-like rectangular cells with a cell wall and a clear nucleus

Coverslip trick

Lower the coverslip at an angle, touching one edge first, so that air bubbles are pushed out. Air bubbles look like thick-edged circles and can be mistaken for cells.

Why stain? Most cells are colourless; a stain colours the nucleus so you can see it.

🎲 Fun Corner — microscope trouble-shooter
1. You see thick black rings on your slide.
Not cells!
👀 Show answer
Air bubbles — lower the coverslip at an angle next time.
2. The image is blurred on high power.
Which knob?
👀 Show answer
Turn the fine adjustment knob gently.
3. Your cheek cells are almost invisible.
Colourless.
👀 Show answer
Add a drop of methylene blue.
🎯 Fun Trivia
• 🔍 Leeuwenhoek called the tiny living things he saw "animalcules" — little animals.
• 🍞 The holes in bread are made by carbon dioxide gas from yeast cells.
• 🧫 A single drop of pond water can hold thousands of single-celled organisms.
