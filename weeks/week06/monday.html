➗ Maths — The Four Operations

Adding & subtracting

Like fractions (same denominator) — just add or subtract the numerators. Unlike fractions — find the LCM of the denominators first, convert both, then add.

1/4 + 2/3: LCM of 4 and 3 is 12 → 3/12 + 8/12 = 11/12. Never add denominators.

Multiplying & dividing

Multiply: top × top, bottom × bottom, then simplify. Divide: flip the second fraction (its reciprocal) and multiply.

5/6 ÷ 5/12 = 5/6 × 12/5 = 60/30 = 2.

Decimals

Add / subtract: line the decimal points up, filling gaps with zeros. Multiply: ignore the points, multiply, then count the total decimal places in both numbers and put the point back that far from the right.

3.2 × 1.5 → 32 × 15 = 480; two decimal places → 4.80. To divide, shift both points equally: 3.5 ÷ 0.7 = 35 ÷ 7 = 5.
🗣️ ಕನ್ನಡ — ಕೃಷ್ಣ ಸುಧಾಮ

ಕಥೆಯ ಸಾರಾಂಶ — the story

ಸುಧಾಮ (also called ಕುಚೇಲ) is a poor Brahmin and Krishna's childhood friend from ಗುರು ಸಾಂದೀಪನಿ's ashram. He travels to ದ್ವಾರಕೆ to seek help, carrying only a small bundle of ಅವಲಕ್ಕಿ (beaten rice) as a gift, and is too shy to ask for anything.

Krishna welcomes him with great honour, eats the poha with joy, and Sudhama returns home having asked for nothing — only to find his hut turned into a palace. Krishna's wife in Dwarka is ರುಕ್ಮಿಣಿ.

ಪದಗಳು & ಸಂದೇಶ

ಅವಲಕ್ಕಿ (beaten rice) · ಗುರು (teacher) · ಆಶ್ರಮ (hermitage) · ಭಕ್ತಿ (devotion) · ಸ್ನೇಹ (friendship) · ದಾನ (gift) · ನೆನಪು (memory).

The message: God values love and devotion above wealth. True friendship makes no distinction between rich and poor.
📖 English — The Selfish Giant

The story, in short

Oscar Wilde's giant returns after seven years to find children playing in his garden. He drives them out and builds a high wall — and spring never comes to his garden again. When the children creep back through a hole in the wall, spring returns with them. Moved by a small boy who cannot climb a tree, the giant knocks the wall down. Years later the boy, now bearing wounds, returns to take the old giant to Paradise.

Themes & symbols

Redemption — the giant changes from selfish to loving. Love and compassion open the way to happiness. Innocence — the children, and especially the little boy, melt the hardest heart.

The endless winter symbolises the giant's selfishness; spring is love returning. Words to know: selfish, trespass, hail, frost, bore. The tale is an allegorical fairy tale, not a fable.
