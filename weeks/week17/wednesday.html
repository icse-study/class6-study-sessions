🌍 Geography · Ch 5 Minerals · Part 3
Energy Minerals — Coal, the Black Gold
Coal powered steam engines, the Industrial Revolution and still lights many Indian homes through thermal power. Learn how it forms, its four types, where it is mined and how it is used.

🌿 How coal forms

Coal forms from decomposed remains of plants buried under high heat and pressure for millions of years beneath beds of sedimentary rocks. It is an inflammable organic substance rich in carbon and gives huge heat when burnt.

🚂 Coal and industry

• Long the power source of steam engines.
• Since the Industrial Revolution (18th century), used to generate electricity.
• Early 20th century: steel industries ran on coal-fired furnaces, so they were located near coal mines.
• Thermal power stations supplied electricity to homes and industry.
So coal, the "black gold", became the backbone of industrial growth.

📊 Four types of coal

1. Peat — very little carbon
2. Lignite
3. Bituminous
4. Anthracite — more than 90% carbon, the best coal.

💡 More carbon = more heat, less smoke.

🌏 Where is coal mined?

World: USA, China, India lead. India: Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Maharashtra.

🔥 Uses

• Thermal power generation.
• Large amounts used in cement, chemical and steel industries.

⛏️ Mined by shaft mining

Coal usually lies at great depths, so it is extracted by shaft (underground) mining: a long vertical shaft is dug and miners go down in lift cages.

🌡️ The downside

Burning coal releases carbon dioxide and smoke, causing air pollution and adding to global warming. Coal is also non-renewable.

🎯 Must-remember facts
• Plants + heat + pressure + millions of years under sedimentary rock
• Peat < lignite < bituminous < anthracite (>90% C, best)
• Black gold · backbone of industry · Industrial Revolution 18th c.
• World: USA, China, India · India: Jharkhand, WB, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, MP, Maharashtra
• Uses: thermal power, cement, chemicals, steel

✍️ English Grammar · Ch 10 Adverbs & Adverbials (Revision)
Adverbs — how, when, where, how often, how much
Adverbs add detail to verbs, adjectives and other adverbs. They answer how? when? where? how often? how much? This week: the five kinds, words that are both adjective and adverb, where to place an adverb, and one-word adverbs that replace whole phrases.

🗂️ Five kinds

• Manner (how): She sings beautifully. He works carefully.
• Degree (how much): She is extremely happy. It was too late.
• Place (where): Play outside. I looked everywhere.
• Time (when): Now we live in Pune.
• Frequency (how often): I usually go on Saturday. I never scribble.

💡 Degree adverbs can modify adjectives (extremely happy) and other adverbs (very quickly).

🔁 Same word, two jobs

fast, hard, late, early, daily stay the same as adjective or adverb.
• a fast car (adj) · he runs fast (adv)
• hard work (adj) · she works hard (adv)
⚠️ hardly ≠ hard. Hardly = almost not: I could hardly hear her.
⚠️ lately ≠ late. Lately = recently.

good (adj) → well (adv): He is a good player. He plays well.

📍 Where do adverbs go?

• Frequency adverbs go before the main verb but after am/is/are/was: He sometimes makes mistakes. She is always late.
• With a helping verb, between helping and main verb: I have often told her.
• Degree adverbs go before the word they modify: He did fairly well.
• Only goes right before the word it limits: Only three or four boys were late.

✨ Placement practice from your book

• She spoke so quietly that we could hardly hear her.
• We frequently visited her while in Delhi.
• Nothing ever happens by chance.
• We seldom see ourselves as others see us.

🎯 One word for a phrase

in a short time → shortly · in a few words → briefly · without doubt → undoubtedly · without being compelled → voluntarily · without anyone differing → unanimously · in an instant → instantaneously · at the same time → simultaneously · without stopping → continually · without special cultivation → naturally · by spoken words → orally

🚫 Retire 'very'

'Very' is the most overused adverb. Try a stronger one:
very afraid → exceedingly afraid · very tired → extremely tired · very pretty → really pretty.
Other degree adverbs: absolutely, completely, entirely, fairly, fully, greatly, highly, intensely, nearly, perfectly, quite, rather, thoroughly, totally, utterly.

✨ Fun facts
• Not every -ly word is an adverb! Friendly, lovely, lonely, ugly, silly, costly are adjectives.
• Writers often say: cut very and choose a stronger word. Not 'very big' but enormous; not 'very tired' but exhausted.
• Unanimously comes from Latin unus (one) + animus (mind) — 'of one mind'.
• The position of 'only' can change meaning: 'Only I ate the cake' vs 'I ate only the cake'.

🎯 Must-remember rules
• Manner (how), degree (how much), place (where), time (when), frequency (how often)
• fast, hard, late, early, daily: same form as adjective and adverb
• hardly = almost not; lately = recently
• Frequency adverb: before main verb, after am/is/are/was
• shortly, briefly, undoubtedly, voluntarily, unanimously, simultaneously, orally
