Grand Revision 1 — Globes, Maps & Directions
Back to where the year began! Nine revision weeks will take you through every chapter — with stories, trivia and fresh challenges. This week: globes, maps, directions, scale and symbols.

🌐 Globe vs map

A globe is a true 3-D model of the Earth — correct shapes, sizes and directions, but hard to carry and cannot show details. A map is a flat drawing of the whole Earth or a part of it on a scale; easy to carry and can show much detail, but always has some distortion.

🧭 Five elements of a map

1. Title 2. Direction (north arrow) 3. Scale 4. Conventional symbols with a key/legend 5. Grid (latitudes & longitudes).

➡️ Directions

Cardinal: N, E, S, W. Intermediate: NE, SE, SW, NW (NE is halfway between north and east). A compass needle always points to the north.

📏 Scale

Scale = ratio between distance on a map and on the ground. Large-scale maps show small areas in great detail (village, town plans). Small-scale maps show large areas with less detail (atlas maps of countries). A map with 1 cm = 100 km is small-scale because a big area is squeezed into a small space.

🎨 Symbols & colours

Conventional symbols are agreed worldwide. Colours: blue = water, green = plains/forests, yellow/brown = plateaus and mountains, white = snow. Contour lines join places of equal height; close lines = steep slope.

🗺️ Map types

Physical/relief maps show landforms; political maps show countries, states, cities; thematic maps show one theme (rainfall, crops, minerals). A sketch is a rough drawing not to scale; a plan is a large-scale drawing of a small area. Map-making is cartography; a map-maker is a cartographer; a book of maps is an atlas.

📖 Story time
Eratosthenes measures the Earth with a stick
Around 240 BCE, a Greek librarian in Egypt named Eratosthenes heard that at noon on midsummer day, the Sun shone straight down a well in the town of Syene — no shadow at all. On the same day in Alexandria, a stick cast a small shadow. He measured the shadow's angle (about 7°, roughly 1/50 of a circle), knew the distance between the towns, and multiplied by 50. His answer for Earth's circumference was astonishingly close to the true ~40,000 km — with just a stick, a shadow and brilliant thinking!
📖 Story time
Why is the atlas called an "atlas"?
In 1595 the map-maker Gerardus Mercator published a book of maps with a picture of the Greek giant Atlas, who was said to hold up the heavens on his shoulders. The name stuck — every book of maps is now an atlas.
🤔 Did you know? Trivia corner
• The oldest known world map is a Babylonian clay tablet about 2,600 years old.
• On Mercator-style maps Greenland looks as big as Africa, but Africa is about 14 times larger!
• Google Maps and phone GPS use satellites orbiting about 20,000 km above us.
• Ancient sailors in the Pacific made "stick charts" from palm ribs and shells to map ocean swells.
🎯 Must-remember facts
• Globe = true model · Map = flat, scaled drawing
• Title, direction, scale, symbols/key, grid
• Large scale = small area, more detail · Small scale = large area, less detail
• Cartography · cartographer · atlas
• Contours close together = steep slope
🗺️ Map Revision · 15 minutes
Find it on the map
Open your atlas (or an outline map). Point to each place before you open the answer. Say the answer out loud.
1. On your school map, find the north arrow and the key. What colour shows plateaus?
👀 Show model answer
Usually yellow/light brown; mountains darker brown; plains green; water blue.
2. Your home is north-east of school. Point the direction on the map.
👀 Show model answer
Halfway between north and east.
3. On an atlas map, find the scale. Is it large- or small-scale?
👀 Show model answer
Atlas maps of countries/continents are small-scale.
4. Mark the longest river in the world and the longest river in China.
👀 Show model answer
World — Nile (Africa); China — Chang Jiang (Yangtze).
5. Mark the Atlas Mountains and the narrow channel separating Europe and Africa.
👀 Show model answer
Atlas Mountains — NW Africa (Morocco–Algeria); Strait of Gibraltar.
