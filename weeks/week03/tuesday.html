🔢 Place Value & Rounding

Place value vs face value

Face value = the digit itself. Place value = digit × the value of its place. In the Indian system the places are: Ones, Tens, Hundreds, Thousands, Ten-thousands, Lakhs, Ten-lakhs.

In 4,72,153 → the 7 sits in the ten-thousands place, so its place value is 7 × 10,000 = 70,000. Its face value is just 7.

Greatest & smallest numbers

To build the greatest number, arrange the digits in descending order. For the smallest, use ascending order — but if 0 is one of the digits, it cannot come first, so swap it with the next digit.

Digits 2, 0, 5, 8, 3 → greatest = 85320, smallest = 20358.

Rounding off

Look at the digit just to the right of the place you are rounding to. If it is 5 or more, round up; if it is 4 or less, round down. Everything to the right becomes 0.

4,682 to the nearest hundred → the tens digit is 8 (≥5), so round up → 4,700.
🧮 Factors, Multiples, HCF & LCM

HCF and LCM

HCF (Highest Common Factor) — the biggest number that divides all of them. LCM (Lowest Common Multiple) — the smallest number that all of them divide into.

12 = 2×2×3, 18 = 2×3×3 → common = 2×3 = HCF 6.
4 = 2², 6 = 2×3, 8 = 2³ → take highest powers 2³×3 = LCM 24.
💡 HCF ≤ the smallest number; LCM ≥ the largest number. Use this to check your answer instantly.

Prime numbers

A prime has exactly two factors — 1 and itself. The first primes are 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13… Note that 1 is not prime (it has only one factor) and 2 is the only even prime.

Sum of the first four primes = 2 + 3 + 5 + 7 = 17.
🍕 Fractions, Percentages & Integers

Fraction → percentage

Multiply the fraction by 100 and add the % sign.

¾ × 100 = 75% · ½ = 50% · ⅕ = 20% · ⅛ = 12.5%

Improper → mixed fraction

Divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the whole number, the remainder becomes the new numerator, and the denominator stays the same.

23 ÷ 5 → quotient 4, remainder 3 → 4 ³⁄₅

Adding integers

Same signs → add the numbers, keep the sign. Different signs → subtract the smaller from the larger, and keep the sign of the larger number.

(−15) + (+8): 15 − 8 = 7, and 15 is bigger and negative → −7.
Successor of −99 = −99 + 1 = −98 (successors always move right on the number line).
📐 BODMAS, Shapes & Angles

BODMAS order

Brackets → Orders (powers) → Division & Multiplication (left to right) → Addition & Subtraction (left to right).

24 ÷ 6 × 2 + 3 → (24 ÷ 6) = 4 → 4 × 2 = 8 → 8 + 3 = 11. Do ÷ and × left to right — not × before ÷.

⚡ Speed tips
• Converting to a smaller unit always multiplies; to a bigger unit always divides.
• The mean of consecutive numbers starting at 1 is just the middle number.
• Check integer answers on a number line — left is smaller, right is bigger.

⚡ Physics · Day 1 · Matter
Matter, its particles & solids
Matter is anything that occupies space and has mass. Learn what it is made of, how its particles behave, and the properties of solids. Read the cards, then take the quiz.
🧱 What is Matter?

Matter

Anything that occupies space and has mass. It can be sensed (smell, touch, sight…). Everything living and non-living is made of matter.

Examples: air, water, sugar, sand, iron, wood, ice, milk, oil, food, plants, animals.

Atom vs Molecule

Atom — the smallest particle of matter. Molecule — the smallest particle of a substance that can exist freely in nature (made of one or more atoms). Molecules are extremely small: about 10⁻⁹ m to 10⁻¹⁰ m.

💡 A single drop of water holds hundreds of millions of atoms!
🔎 Properties of Matter Particles

The four key properties

1. Very small: a few KMnO₄ crystals colour a huge volume of water on repeated dilution.
2. Spaces between them: 20 g salt dissolves in 100 ml water without raising the level — salt fills the inter-molecular space.
3. Constant random motion: dust dances in sunlight; perfume spreads across a room (zig-zag motion).
4. Attract each other: the intermolecular force is strongest in solids, less in liquids, least in gases.

Cohesive vs Adhesive force

Cohesive — attraction between particles of the same substance. Adhesive — attraction between different substances.

Water sticks to glass (adhesive > cohesive). Mercury does NOT stick to glass (adhesive < cohesive).
🧊 States of Matter & Solids

Three states (+ plasma)

Matter exists as solid, liquid and gas (water can be all three: ice, water, steam). Plasma is the fourth state, found at very high temperature. The state depends on inter-molecular space, force of attraction and molecular motion.

Solids

Strong intermolecular force, negligible space, molecules only vibrate about fixed positions → definite shape and volume, cannot be compressed, cannot flow, highly rigid and dense.

💡 A rubber band changes shape but springs back — it is elastic, yet still a solid.

IDLE, print, variables & operators
Everything you need for the quiz: how Python is written, the data types, the operators, input/output and the two kinds of loops. Read the cards, then take the quiz.
🖥️ Getting started

IDLE

IDLE stands for Integrated Development and Learning Environment. It is the editor that comes free with Python. It has two parts — the shell (types results straight away) and the script editor (where you save programs).

A saved Python program has the extension .py — for example myfirst.py.

Comments

A comment is a note for humans — Python ignores it. Start a single-line comment with the hash symbol #.

# this line is ignored by Python print("Hello") # so is this part

Case sensitivity & indentation

Python is case-sensitive: Print, PRINT and print are three different things — only print works. Python also uses indentation (spaces at the start of a line, normally 4) instead of curly braces to show which lines belong inside a block.

if age >= 18: print("Adult") # indented → inside the if print("Done") # not indented → outside
📤 Input & Output

print() and input()

print() displays something on the screen. input() reads what the user types — and it always returns a string, so wrap it in int() when you need a number.

name = input("Your name: ") age = int(input("Your age: ")) print("Hi", name)

Strings repeat with *

Multiplying a string by a number repeats it. Adding two strings joins them (concatenation).

print("3 * 'A'") # prints the text itself print(3 * 'A') # AAA print('AB' + 'CD') # ABCD
🔁 Decisions & loops

if / elif / else

A decision block always starts with the keyword if, ends the line with a colon : and indents the lines inside.

if marks >= 90: print("A grade") elif marks >= 50: print("Pass") else: print("Try again")

for vs while

Use a for loop when you know how many times to repeat. Use a while loop when you only know the condition to stop.

for i in range(5): # exactly 5 times print(i) while guess != 7: # unknown number of times guess = int(input())
💡 range(5) gives 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 — it starts at 0 and stops before 5.
