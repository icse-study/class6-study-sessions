Grand Revision 3 — Mountains, Plateaus & Plains
Revise how mountains, plateaus and plains form, and how they shape people's lives — with the story of the first climb of Everest.

🔥 Inside & outside forces

Endogenous (internal) forces — folding, faulting, volcanoes, earthquakes — build landforms. Exogenous (external) forces — rivers, wind, glaciers, waves — wear down and reshape them.

⛰️ Fold mountains

Formed when layers of rock are squeezed and folded. Young fold: high, sharp peaks — Himalayas, Alps, Rockies, Andes. Old fold: worn, rounded — Aravallis, Appalachians, Urals.

🧱 Block mountains

Formed by faulting — a block rises (horst) or sinks (graben/rift valley) between two faults; flat-topped. Examples: Vindhyas, Satpuras (India), Harz (Germany), Vosges (France).

🌋 Volcanic mountains

Built by lava and ash piling up around a vent: Mt Kilimanjaro (Africa), Mt Fuji (Japan), Cotopaxi, Popocatépetl.

🟫 Plateaus

Raised, flat-topped highlands with steep sides. Deccan (lava, India), Tibet (highest, "Roof of the World"), intermontane Bolivian, Meseta (dissected, Spain). Rich in minerals, waterfalls, cool climate; pastures.

🌾 Plains

Low, flat land. Alluvial plains are formed by rivers depositing silt — Indo-Gangetic, Mississippi, Amazon. Most crowded areas: fertile soil, easy farming, roads and towns. Others: Great Plains, Pampas.

🏡 Life in mountains vs plains

Mountains: cold, steep, thin soil — terrace farming, herding, tourism, scattered population, hard transport. Plains: fertile, level — intensive farming, industries, dense population, good transport.

📖 Story time
Tenzing and Hillary on the roof of the world
At 11:30 AM on 29 May 1953, Tenzing Norgay, a Sherpa from the Himalayas, and Edmund Hillary, a beekeeper from New Zealand, stepped onto the summit of Mount Everest (8,849 m) — the first people known to do so. They stayed just 15 minutes. Tenzing buried sweets and a pencil from his daughter as an offering; Hillary left a cross. When asked who stepped on top first, they always said: "We reached it together, as a team."
📖 Story time
Mountains that grow
The Himalayas are so young that they are still rising — by about 4–5 mm every year — because the Indian plate keeps pushing into Asia. Sea shells and fish fossils found high on Himalayan peaks show these rocks once lay under an ancient sea, the Tethys!
🤔 Did you know? Trivia corner
• Mauna Kea in Hawaii is taller than Everest when measured from its base on the ocean floor (over 10,000 m).
• The Aravallis are among the oldest fold mountains on Earth.
• The Tibetan Plateau averages over 4,500 m high.
• Chimborazo in Ecuador is the point on Earth farthest from its centre, thanks to Earth's bulge at the Equator.
🎯 Must-remember facts
• Endogenous build · exogenous wear down
• Fold (Himalayas, Alps) · Block (Vindhyas, Harz) · Volcanic (Kilimanjaro, Fuji)
• Plateaus: Deccan, Tibet, Meseta · minerals & waterfalls
• Plains: alluvial, fertile, crowded
• Everest 8,849 m · 29 May 1953
