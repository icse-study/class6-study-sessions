Networks & the Internet
A network is computers connected to share data. The Internet is “a network of networks” spanning the whole world.
🔗 Kinds of networks

LAN (Local Area Network) — a small area like a home, school or office; lets you share printers and files. WAN (Wide Area Network) — across cities or countries; the Internet is the biggest WAN. Wi-Fi is wireless; an Ethernet cable is wired. Bluetooth is very short-range.

🏠 IP addresses

Every device on a network has an IP address — like a home address for computers, e.g. 192.168.1.5. The older IPv4 uses four numbers from 0 to 255 (each is 8 bits). Because IPv4 addresses ran out, the much bigger IPv6 was created.

📦 How a message travels

Your message is split into small packets. They go: your device → router → modem → your ISP (Internet Service Provider, e.g. Jio or Airtel) → across the Internet (often through undersea cables) → your friend’s ISP → your friend’s device, where the packets are put back in order. Packets can take different routes.

🌐 Internet vs Web

The Internet is the network of cables and computers (it grew from ARPANET, 1969). The World Wide Web — websites and links — runs on top of it and was invented by Tim Berners-Lee in 1989.

✨ Fun facts
• Most data between continents travels through cables on the ocean floor, not through satellites.
• Internet speed is measured in megabits per second (Mbps). 8 bits = 1 byte, so 100 Mbps downloads about 12.5 megabytes per second.
• The first message sent on ARPANET, in 1969, was meant to be “LOGIN”, but the system crashed after “LO”.
