📖 English Literature · Week 25
Ch 7 · The Mountain and the Squirrel — and the language of Chapter 7
Emerson’s witty poem in which a tiny squirrel answers a proud mountain, plus the chapter’s grammar: reporting verbs, phrases and clauses, -ance/-ence, word stress, assonance and message writing.
🐿️ The poem

The mountain and the squirrel have a quarrel. The mountain (the former) calls the squirrel (the latter) “Little Prig” — someone who thinks himself superior. “Bun” (an old word for squirrel) replies:

• “You are doubtless very big” — he admits it.
• All sorts of things and weather must be taken together “to make up a year / And a sphere” — the world needs variety.
• “I think it no disgrace / To occupy my place.”
• “If I’m not so large as you, / You are not so small as I, / And not half so spry.”
• “You make / A very pretty squirrel track” — the mountain is a fine path for him!
• “Talents differ; all is well and wisely put; / If I cannot carry forests on my back, / Neither can you crack a nut.”

Theme: everyone has their own talents; size does not decide worth; no one is superior — we are just different. The squirrel answers an insult calmly and wisely, without insulting back.

🎵 Sound devices

Assonance — repeated vowel sound anywhere in words: “If I cannot carry forests on my back, / Neither can you crack a nut” · “a fleet of sleeping geese”.
Alliteration — repeated first sound: “The sheep should stay in the shed.”
Rhyme: squirrel / quarrel · big / Prig · year / sphere · disgrace / place · track / back.

✏️ Grammar of Chapter 7

Reporting verbs report speech: said, asked, raced, replied, demanded, exclaimed, sighed, added.
Phrase — a group of words without its own subject and finite verb: “returning home from the cricket field”, “in the kitchen”.
Clause — has a subject and a finite verb: “he threw down the coins”, “because she knew what to expect”.
-ance / -ence: ignorance, acquaintance, maintenance, resemblance · conscience, adolescence, convenience, dependence, inheritance, intelligence.
Word stress: JUNC/tion, QUER/u/lous, a/PPEAL/ing, dip/lo/MAT/ic, be/FORE, be/SIDE.
Message: heading MESSAGE · date and time · addressee · essential details · what to do · your name · under 50 words.
Former / latter: the first / the second of two things mentioned.

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Ralph Waldo Emerson (1803–1882)

American poet, essayist, lecturer and philosopher — one of the most important US writers of the 19th century.

🌟 Fun facts
• He wrote about freedom, the individual’s place in the world, and the link between the soul and nature.
• Famous essays: “Self-Reliance”, “Nature”, “The Poet”, “Circles”, “Experience”.
• Poems: “Concord Hymn” (with the line about “the shot heard round the world”), “Brahma”, “Uriel”.
• He lived in Concord, Massachusetts, where his neighbours included the Alcott family and the writer Henry David Thoreau.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. The mountain insults the squirrel, but the squirrel does not get angry. What does this tell us about the squirrel?
👀 Show model answer
The squirrel is calm, confident and wise. It agrees that the mountain is big, but explains that the world needs all kinds of things, and that it feels no disgrace in its own place. It even compliments the mountain. By answering with reason instead of rudeness, it wins the argument and shows true self-respect.
2. You are Saira. Your mother has gone to the market. Mrs Singh calls to say she will visit at 6:00 p.m. You are leaving for your music class. Write a message.
👀 Show model answer
MESSAGE — 14 March, 4:30 p.m. — Mummy, Mrs Singh called. She will come to meet you at 6:00 p.m. today. I have gone to my music class and will be back by 7:00 p.m. — Saira
