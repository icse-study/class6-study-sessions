🌍 World & India

🕊️ Gandhi Jayanti — 2 Oct

India celebrated the 157th birth anniversary of Mahatma Gandhi, born on 2 October 1869 in Porbandar, Gujarat. The United Nations marks this day as the International Day of Non-Violence. 2 October is also the birthday of Lal Bahadur Shastri, India's second Prime Minister, who gave the slogan “Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan”.

💡 Gandhi Jayanti is one of India's three national holidays, with Republic Day and Independence Day.

🗳️ Brazil votes — 4 Oct

Brazil, the largest country in South America, held the first round of its presidential election. No candidate won more than half the votes, so President Lula and Flávio Bolsonaro will face each other in a runoff (second round) on 25 October.

💡 Brazil's capital is Brasília, not Rio de Janeiro.

🌫️ Delhi plans ahead for winter smog — 28 Sep

Delhi announced a six-week ban on construction from 10 December to 20 January. Construction dust adds to the thick smog that covers the city every winter, when cold, still air traps pollution near the ground.

🪖 US troops leave Iraq — 30 Sep

The United States completed the withdrawal of its troops from Iraq, ending a military presence that began in 2003 — about 23 years.

🔬 Science & Space

🚀 Starship reaches orbit — 28 Sep

On Flight 14, SpaceX's Starship — the biggest rocket ever built — reached orbit for the first time. It launched from Starbase, Texas, released 26 Starlink internet satellites, and came back early as a safety precaution. Its Super Heavy booster made a soft splashdown in the sea.

💡 Being “in orbit” means moving sideways so fast (about 28,000 km/h) that you keep falling around the Earth instead of into it.

👩‍🚀 Crew-13 flies to the Space Station — 1 Oct

A Falcon 9 rocket launched a Dragon capsule from Cape Canaveral, Florida, carrying four astronauts to the International Space Station (ISS) for about six months. They come from three space agencies: NASA (commander Jessica Watkins and Luke Delaney), the Canadian Space Agency (Joshua Kutryk) and Russia's Roscosmos (Sergey Teteryatnikov).

💡 The ISS circles the Earth about 16 times a day — its crew sees around 16 sunrises every 24 hours.

🌊 Hawaii's Hōlei Sea Arch collapses — 1 Oct

The famous Hōlei Sea Arch in Hawaiʻi Volcanoes National Park fell into the Pacific Ocean. The arch, about 27 m (90 ft) tall, was made of lava rock about 550 years old and had been carved by waves.

💡 Differential erosion: softer rock wears away faster than harder rock, leaving arches, caves and stacks — until they fall.

☔ Tokyo's rainiest streak — 29 Sep

Tokyo, Japan, recorded 34 days of rain in a row — the longest streak since records began in 1886.

🛰️ World Space Week begins — 4 Oct

World Space Week runs from 4 to 10 October; the 2026 theme is “Rocket Revolution”. It starts on 4 October because on that day in 1957 the Soviet Union launched Sputnik 1, the world's first artificial satellite.

💡 Sputnik 1 was a metal ball only 58 cm wide that sent out simple “beep-beep” radio signals.

🦎 A new lizard from Peru — 3 Oct

Scientists described a new lizard species from Peru, Liolaemus archipensis. New species are still being discovered every week!

🎨 Arts, Culture & Special Days

🎆 Asian Games close in Nagoya — 4 Oct

The 2026 Asian Games in Aichi–Nagoya, Japan ended with a closing ceremony in Nagoya. The Asian Games flag was handed to Doha, Qatar, host of the 2030 Games. India's flag-bearer was shooter Neeru Dhanda.

🐾 World Animal Day & Wildlife Week

4 October is World Animal Day, and India celebrates Wildlife Week from 2 to 8 October to spread awareness about protecting animals and their habitats.

👑 A medieval king comes home — 3 Oct

The remains of Tsar Samuel, who ruled Bulgaria about 1,000 years ago, were returned to Bulgaria from Greece, where they had been found.

🏅 Sports — Asian Games final week

🏏 Men's cricket gold — 3 Oct

India beat Pakistan by 19 runs in the final at Nisshin, Japan. India made 211/6 — Abhishek Sharma 61 off 28 balls and Tilak Varma 51* off 22 — and Pakistan finished on 192/6. It was India's second straight Asian Games men's cricket gold, after Hangzhou 2022.

💡 India won both cricket golds in 2026 — the women won on 22 September.

🏑 Hockey double gold — 2 & 3 Oct

The women's team beat China 1–0 with a goal by Lalremsiami. The next day the men thrashed Malaysia 5–1; captain Harmanpreet Singh scored twice. It was the men's second Asian Games gold in a row.

💡 India's men have won 8 Olympic gold medals in hockey — more than any other country.

🎯 Neeru Dhanda's golden week

Shooter Neeru Dhanda won gold in women's trap (29 Sep), gold in mixed trap team with Kynan Chenai (30 Sep) and a silver in the women's trap team event.

💡 In trap shooting, clay discs are flung into the air and the shooter must hit them with a shotgun.

🏹 Archery bonanza

Indian archers won five golds: women's compound team (Jyothi Surekha Vennam, Chikitha Taniparthi, Prithika Pradeep), mixed compound team, men's compound team, women's recurve team, and Kumkum Mohod's women's individual recurve gold on 2 Oct.

💡 A compound bow uses pulleys (cams) to make it easier to hold at full draw; a recurve bow has tips that curve away from the archer.

🥊 Golden Gandhi Jayanti — 2 Oct

India won six golds in one day, including boxers Lovlina Borgohain (75 kg), Parveen Hooda (65 kg) and Ankush Panghal (80 kg), and wrestler Sujeet Kalkal (65 kg). On 3 Oct wrestler Aman Sehrawat (57 kg) and golfer Pranavi Urs also won gold.

🏃 Relay gold & the final tally

The women's 4×400 m relay team (Kiran Pahal, Poovamma Machettira, Prachi Choudhary, Vithya Ramraj) won gold on 29 Sep. Athletics gave India 24 medals, the most of any sport. India finished 4th with 85 medals: 21 gold, 27 silver, 37 bronze — its second-best Asian Games ever, after 2022. China topped the table.

🤓 Fun Facts & Trivia
⭐ Sputnik 1 orbited the Earth once every 96 minutes and burned up after about three months.
⭐ Of the 21 golds, six came on one day — 2 October, Gandhi Jayanti.
⭐ Japan has hosted the Asian Games three times: Tokyo 1958, Hiroshima 1994 and Aichi–Nagoya 2026.
⭐ New Delhi hosted the first Asian Games, in 1951.

Types of Minerals — Metallic & Non-metallic — think and write
Wednesday's topic, one level deeper. Write each answer in your notebook first (4–6 lines), then open the model answer and compare. Then take the 10-question Geo Quiz.
1. Differentiate between ferrous and non-ferrous metallic minerals with specific examples of their industrial applications.
👀 Show model answer
Ferrous minerals contain iron, are heavier and reddish. Iron ore → iron and steel for machines, automobiles, consumer goods; manganese → steel making, bleaching powder, insecticides, paints. Non-ferrous minerals have no iron and are not magnetic. Copper → electrical wires, bronze and brass; bauxite → aluminium for aircraft, automobiles, cables and utensils.
2. What are metallic and non-metallic minerals? Give four differences.
👀 Show model answer
Metallic contain metals; non-metallic do not. Differences: (1) shine vs dull; (2) can be made into wires/sheets vs brittle; (3) good vs poor conductors of heat and electricity; (4) produce sound vs no sound.
3. What are fossil fuels? Why are they called energy minerals?
👀 Show model answer
Fuels formed from the dead and decomposed remains of plants and animals buried for millions of years — coal, petroleum, natural gas. They are energy minerals because energy (heat, electricity) is generated from them.
4. Name the major producers of copper in India and the world. What are its uses?
👀 Show model answer
India: Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand. World: China, Mexico, Chile, Peru, Congo, Zambia. Uses: electrical wires (good conductor), alloys bronze and brass.
5. Describe the grades of iron ore.
👀 Show model answer
Good grades: magnetite (deep brown) and hematite (reddish). Inferior: limonite (yellowish) and siderite.
6. Write the properties and uses of bauxite and manganese.
👀 Show model answer
Bauxite: soft; white, grey or reddish-brown; main ore of aluminium; used for aircraft, automobiles, cables, utensils. Manganese: grey-white, hard, brittle; used in steel, bleaching powder, insecticides, paints.
7. Why is mica important to the electrical industry?
👀 Show model answer
Mica is fireproof and an insulator, so it is used in electrical equipment to stop heat and current from passing where they should not.
8. Name the minerals required for: iron and steel, electrical, cement.
👀 Show model answer
Iron & steel: iron ore, manganese, coal, limestone. Electrical: copper, aluminium (bauxite), mica. Cement: limestone (plus gypsum and clay).
9. Make a list of daily-life items made from metallic minerals. Do they shine? What can you conclude?
👀 Show model answer
Steel spoons, aluminium vessels, copper wires, gold/silver jewellery, iron gates, brass lamps. Most shine (unless rusted or painted). Conclusion: metallic minerals have lustre, are strong and malleable, and are essential to daily life.
10. Name the Indian states that produce gold, silver, uranium and mica.
👀 Show model answer
Gold — Karnataka; silver — Rajasthan; uranium — Jharkhand; mica — Jharkhand, Andhra Pradesh.
