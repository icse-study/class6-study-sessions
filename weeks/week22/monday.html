📖 English Literature · Week 22
Ch 6 · Little Women — Christmas Morning
An excerpt from Louisa May Alcott’s Little Women. Four sisters, a mother called Marmee, and the Christmas breakfast they give away. Themes: kindness, unselfishness and family. Note: the PDF begins on page 90, so the opening of the excerpt is summarised from the novel.
👭 Meet the March family

Meg — the eldest, responsible and gentle; she leads by example.
Jo — restless, lively, impetuous and funny; called a “Sancho” all her life.
Beth — shy, kind and thoughtful.
Amy — the youngest, a little vain and “lazy”, but trying hard not to be selfish.
Marmee (Mrs March) — their generous mother. Father is away in the American Civil War (1861–1865).
Hannah — has lived with them since Meg was born; “more as a friend than a servant”.

🎄 Christmas morning, in order

1. Jo wakes first in the grey dawn and finds a little book under her pillow; each sister has one (Amy’s is blue).
2. Meg: Mother wants us to read, love and mind these books — we have neglected things since Father went away. She will read every morning. Jo reads cheek to cheek with her, with “the quiet expression so seldom seen on her restless face”. Beth offers to help Amy with the hard words.
3. Marmee has gone out — “some poor creeter come a-beggin’”, says Hannah.
4. Gifts for Marmee in a basket under the sofa: Jo’s army-slippers, Beth’s handkerchiefs marked “Mother” (not “M. March”, since Meg’s initials are also M.M.), and Amy’s cologne.
5. Amy has secretly swapped the little bottle for a big one with all her money: “I’m truly trying not to be selfish anymore.” Meg hugs her, Jo calls her “a trump”, Beth adds her finest rose.
6. Marmee asks: a poor woman with a newborn baby and six children huddled in one bed, no fire, nothing to eat — will you give them your breakfast? After one silent minute Jo cries, “I’m so glad you came before we began!” Amy gives up the cream and muffins.
7. Through the back streets to a bare room with broken windows. “Ach, mein Gott! It is good angels come to us!” — “Funny angels in hoods and mittens,” says Jo. Hannah makes a fire; Marmee feeds the mother and dresses the baby; the girls feed the children “like so many hungry birds”.
8. “Das ist gute!” “Der angel-kinder!” The girls go home to bread and milk — the merriest four people in the city.

✏️ Words to know

vittles (food) · cologne · abashed (embarrassed) · earnest (sincere) · stately (impressive) · impetuously (without thinking) · muffins · buckwheat · ragged · mittens · gruel · blaze · contented.
A trump = a fine, generous person. Sancho = Sancho Panza from Don Quixote, an uneducated but wise peasant.

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Louisa May Alcott (1832–1888)

American writer of poems, short stories and novels.

🌟 Fun facts
• Little Women (1868) is loosely based on Alcott and her three sisters — Jo, the restless would-be writer, is Louisa herself.
• She wrote two sequels, Little Men and Jo’s Boys.
• Under the pen name A. M. Barnard she secretly wrote thrillers about spies and revenge.
• During the American Civil War she worked as a nurse, and wrote about it in Hospital Sketches.
• Her family were friends with Ralph Waldo Emerson (Chapter 7’s poet!), and young Louisa borrowed books from his library.
• In the full novel the poor German family the March girls help is called the Hummels.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. What qualities of Marmee do you admire most, and why?
👀 Show model answer
Marmee is generous and compassionate — she rushes out early on Christmas morning to help a poor family. She is also a wise mother: instead of ordering her daughters, she asks them whether they will give away their breakfast, so the kindness becomes their own choice. She leads by example, comforting the sick mother and dressing the baby “as tenderly as if it had been her own”.
2. Imagine you are Amy. Write a short diary entry about that Christmas morning.
👀 Show model answer
Dear Diary, This morning I felt ashamed of my little bottle of cologne, so I ran out early and spent all my money on a big one. Meg hugged me and Jo called me a trump! Then Marmee asked us to give our breakfast to a poor family. I gave up the cream and muffins I love best. Their room was so cold, but the children called us angel children. I was hungry all morning, yet I have never felt happier. Amy
