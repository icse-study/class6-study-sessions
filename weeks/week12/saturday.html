📌 Things to Remember
⚠️ Don't confuse these

Epigraphy vs numismatics. Epigraphy is inscriptions; numismatics is coins.

Carbon-14. Works only on things that were once alive — bone, wood, cloth. Not on coins, not on stone.

Gram Sabha vs Gram Panchayat. The Sabha is every adult villager and elects the Panchayat; the Panchayat is the small elected body that does the work.

Mayor vs Commissioner. Mayor = elected political head. Commissioner = state-appointed real executive.

Harappan horse. The horse does not appear on Indus seals. It arrives with the Aryans.

Varna. Flexible and occupation-based in the Early Vedic period; rigid and birth-based later.

Iron. Early Vedic people used copper and bronze. Iron becomes common only in the Later Vedic period.

Income tax is central, never municipal. Octroi, toll and property tax are the local ones.

✍️ How do historians and archaeologists work together to reconstruct the past?
Model answer →
Archaeologists excavate sites and recover material remains — tools, pottery, coins, seals, buildings and bones — and use methods such as Carbon-14 dating to work out how old they are. Historians study written sources such as inscriptions, religious texts and travellers' accounts, and interpret what the remains mean. Neither is enough alone: for the Indus Valley there are no readable texts, so almost everything comes from archaeology, while for the Vedic age there are hymns but very few remains. Putting the two kinds of evidence side by side gives the fullest and most reliable picture of the past.
✍️ Why is local self-government important in both rural and urban areas? Give two reasons.
Model answer →
First, local problems are best solved by local people. Those who live in a village or a ward know which well has dried up or which street is unlit far better than an official sitting in the state capital, and they can act quickly. Second, local self-government trains ordinary citizens in democracy. Because every adult votes, and because seats are reserved for women and for Scheduled Castes and Tribes, people who would otherwise have no voice take part in real decisions — and they can hold their representatives to account at the next election, or through the Gram Sabha.
