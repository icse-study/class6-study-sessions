🇮🇳 India

♿ Record haul at the Asian Para Games — 18–24 Oct

At the Asian Para Games in Aichi-Nagoya, Japan, India won a record 114 medals, including 30 gold, finishing 4th. Para athletics gave 65 medals, and Sumit Antil led an all-Indian javelin podium. China topped the table.

💡 India’s previous best was 111 medals at Hangzhou in 2023.
🌍 World

🇧🇷 Lula wins again — 25 Oct

Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva won Brazil’s presidential run-off by 52% to 48%, for a fourth term.

✈️ Sydney’s second airport — 25 Oct

Western Sydney Airport in Australia welcomed its first passengers, becoming Greater Sydney’s second airport.

📱 No social media under 15 — 23 Oct

Greece passed a law banning children under 15 from social media from 1 January 2027.

🍁 Alberta stays in Canada — 19 Oct

Voters in the Canadian province of Alberta rejected holding an independence referendum; more than three-quarters wanted to stay in Canada.

⚖️ Sakharov Prize — 22 Oct

The European Parliament gave its Sakharov Prize for Freedom of Thought to the International Criminal Court.

🔬 Science & Nature

🚀 Off to a moon of Mars — 19 Oct

Japan launched the Martian Moons eXploration (MMX) spacecraft on an H3 rocket from the Tanegashima Space Center. It will scoop at least 10 grams of soil from Phobos and bring it back to Earth in 2031.

💡 Mars has two moons, Phobos (“fear”) and Deimos (“dread”), named after the sons of the Greek war god Ares.

☢️ A safe home for nuclear waste — 22 Oct

Finland licensed Onkalo, the world’s first deep underground store for used nuclear fuel, built in solid rock about 400 m down.

🌏 Earthquake in Tibet — 22 Oct

A magnitude 7.0 earthquake struck Tibet, centred near Nagqu.

🌀 Typhoon Champi — 23–24 Oct

Typhoon Champi swept near Taiwan, and more than 2,600 people were moved out of mountain areas.

💡 Typhoon, hurricane and cyclone are the same kind of storm, named differently in different oceans.
🎨 Arts & Culture

🎵 Mercury Prize — 22 Oct

British singer Raye won the Mercury Prize for the best UK album, for This Music May Contain Hope.

🏆 Princess of Asturias Award — 23 Oct

Lionel Messi received Spain’s Princess of Asturias Award for Sports, honouring his career at FC Barcelona.

🏅 Sports

🤸 Hashimoto’s fourth world title — 22 Oct

Japan’s Daiki Hashimoto won his fourth men’s all-around title in a row at the World Artistic Gymnastics Championships in Rotterdam, Netherlands, ahead of China’s Zhang Boheng.

⚾ An unusual World Series — 20 Oct

The Milwaukee Brewers and Tampa Bay Rays reached the World Series — neither club has ever won it.

⛳ First LPGA title — 25 Oct

Japan’s Yuri Yoshida won her first LPGA Tour title, at the BMW Ladies Championship in South Korea.

🤓 Fun Facts & Trivia

🔴 The Paralympic symbol

The Paralympic symbol is called the Agitos (Latin for “I move”) — three curved shapes in red, blue and green.

Methods of Mining, Hazards & Conservation — think and write
Wednesday's topic, one level deeper. Write each answer in your notebook first (4–6 lines), then open the model answer and compare. Then take the 10-question Geo Quiz.
1. Explain, with examples, the important methods of mining.
👀 Show model answer
Opencast — surface mining for minerals near the surface; top soil and rock stripped; strip/open pit; e.g. manganese, copper, bauxite. Shaft — underground mining for deep minerals; vertical shaft and lift cages; e.g. coal. Alluvial — sifting mud, sand, gravel with water; heavy minerals left behind; small-scale; e.g. gold, tin.
2. Mention any three hazards of mining.
👀 Show model answer
1. Mine dust pollutes air and causes respiratory diseases. 2. Mine waste pollutes water and soil. 3. Deforestation and loss of biodiversity; scarred land. (Also accidents — tunnel collapse, flooding, gas.)
3. State any three methods of conservation of minerals.
👀 Show model answer
1. Use minerals judiciously and avoid waste. 2. Recycle metals. 3. Use substitutes and renewable energy. 4. Improve mining technology and reclaim mined land.
4. Differentiate between opencast mining and shaft mining.
👀 Show model answer
Opencast: surface; minerals near surface; strip/open pit; manganese, copper, bauxite; cheaper and safer but scars land. Shaft: underground; minerals at great depth; vertical shaft and lift cages; coal; costly and risky.
5. Look at a picture of miners in lift cages and coal seams at two levels: name the mineral, method, two uses and where found in India.
👀 Show model answer
Mineral: coal; method: shaft mining. Uses: thermal power; steel, cement, chemical industries. India: Jharkhand, West Bengal, Odisha, Chhattisgarh, MP, Maharashtra.
6. Why is alluvial mining called small-scale?
👀 Show model answer
It is done by hand or with simple equipment, sifting river sand and gravel with water; it recovers small quantities of heavy minerals (gold, tin), not huge deposits.
7. "Mining damages the environment." Choose a mining region and explain how biodiversity is harmed.
👀 Show model answer
E.g. iron-ore mining in Goa/Odisha: forests are cleared for pits and roads; animals lose habitat; red dust and waste wash into rivers, killing fish; topsoil is lost so plants cannot regrow; noise disturbs wildlife.
8. How does recycling conserve minerals? Give an example.
👀 Show model answer
Recycling reuses metal already extracted, so less new ore is mined. E.g. melting old aluminium cans makes new cans using far less bauxite and energy.
9. Why do mining towns often have many people with breathing problems?
👀 Show model answer
Blasting, digging and transport create fine dust that stays in the air; long-term inhalation damages lungs, causing respiratory diseases.
10. Why must we conserve minerals even though mining gives jobs and wealth?
👀 Show model answer
Minerals are non-renewable and take millions of years to form; if wasted they will run out for future generations, and careless mining harms people and the environment. Careful use balances jobs today with resources for tomorrow.
