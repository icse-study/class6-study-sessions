🔬 Science & Space

🦧 Baby orangutans rescued in Odisha — 9 Sep

Five baby orangutans (6–8 months old) were found abandoned in a plantation in Balasore district, Odisha. Orangutans are not native to India — they live only in the rainforests of Borneo and Sumatra — and are critically endangered. Officials suspect wildlife smuggling. The babies were taken to Nandankanan Zoological Park, Bhubaneswar.

💡 India's Wildlife Crime Control Bureau (WCCB) is investigating with the state police.

🦖 A feather in dinosaur poop — 11 Sep

Scientists found a feather inside 66-million-year-old fossilised dinosaur dung. It may help explain why birds survived the asteroid that wiped out the other dinosaurs.

💡 Fossilised dung is called a coprolite. Birds are living descendants of dinosaurs.

🦇 Why bats live so long — 11 Sep

Long-lived bats have very strong immune defences and special ways of clearing out damaged cells, which may help them avoid cancer and disease for decades.

🎨 Arts & Culture

📖 International Literacy Day — 8 Sep

Started by UNESCO, it reminds the world that reading and writing are a right for everyone.

📚 Gomti Book Festival — from 12 Sep

The National Book Trust opened the Gomti Book Festival in Lucknow (12–20 Sep) with author talks, book launches and cultural shows.

💡 Lucknow stands on the banks of the Gomti river.
🏅 Sports

🎾 US Open champions — 12–13 Sep

Alexander Zverev (Germany) beat Ben Shelton (USA) 6–3, 7–6, 5–7, 6–2 — his second Grand Slam, after the 2026 French Open. Elena Rybakina beat two-time defending champion Aryna Sabalenka 6–4, 5–7, 6–2 to win the women's title.

🏃 First-ever World Athletics Ultimate Championship — 11–13 Sep

A brand-new event in Budapest, Hungary. Melissa Jefferson-Wooden (USA) won both the 100 m (10.62 s) and 200 m (21.47 s). Kenneth Bednarek won the men's 200 m in 19.52 s and Josh Kerr the men's 1500 m.

🏏 India vs Afghanistan — 13 Sep

India won the first T20I in Delhi by 7 wickets, chasing 157 with 38 balls to spare.
