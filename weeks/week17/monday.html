📖 English Literature · Week 17
Ch 5 · The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn — Part 2
Life on the raft, the wrecked steamboat, missing Cairo in the fog, Huck’s great struggle with his conscience, and the Grangerfords.
🛶 Part 2 in order

1. Huck lights a fire as a decoy while Jim loads the raft; they set off with the canoe in tow, keeping to the middle of the river.
2. At daybreak they tie up to a sand bar and cover the raft with cottonwood branches. Jim builds a tepee with a dirt floor for a cooking fire. They sail at night and tie up every morning — “a pretty good life!”
3. On a wrecked steamboat they find cigars, a spyglass and blankets, and nearly get caught. Huck begins to see Jim “wasn’t just a slave but a real person, like me”.
4. They head for Cairo, Illinois, where the Ohio joins the Mississippi — “The Ohio runs north to freedom.” In the fog they pass it in the dark. Jim is upset; Huck is secretly relieved.
5. Jim plans to save money to buy his wife and child — or steal them. Huck’s conscience troubles him. He paddles ashore to turn Jim in.
6. Jim calls: “Huck, you’re my friend. You’re the only white man who ever kept a promise to me.” The words “cut me like a knife”. When men in a boat ask who is on the raft, Huck stutters — and lies to protect Jim. “Miss Watson was right about me — I was a liar and doomed forever.”
7. A steamboat runs over the raft. Huck swims ashore to the Grangerfords, a well-off family. He wears their son Buck’s clothes, eats his first home-cooked meal in a month (corn pone, buttermilk, corned meat) — and wonders whether Jim is alive.

🔑 What examiners look for

Huck’s character: resourceful (decoy, disguise), independent, loyal, kind-hearted — and conflicted.
Irony: Huck thinks he is “doomed” for lying, but readers see that protecting Jim is the right thing to do.
Theme: true friendship and conscience matter more than unjust laws and prejudice.

✏️ Language from the chapter

Uses of the comma: in a list · to address someone (“Stay away, Huck.”) · after an interjection (“Oh, you poor thing!”) · to join two complete parts (“It looked like rain, so we moved…”) · after an opening phrase (“At the same time, I…”) · after a dependent part (“As I paddled downriver, I hoped…”).
Syllable stress: ’campfire, ’surface, ’company · con’vince, a’cross, ca’noe.
Dialogue writing: name + colon (:), new line for each speaker.
Words: decoy, in tow, sand bar, cottonwood, tepee, wrecked, snuck, spyglass, sheriff, ashore, stuttered, well off, corn pone.

✍️ Meet the authors — fun facts & other works

✍️ Mark Twain (1835–1910)

Pen name of Samuel Langhorne Clemens, one of the most important American writers.

🌟 Fun facts
• “Mark twain” was a call on Mississippi riverboats meaning the water is two fathoms (12 feet) deep — safe to sail. Twain himself worked as a licensed riverboat pilot.
• He was born in 1835 when Halley’s Comet passed Earth, predicted he would “go out with it”, and died in 1910 when it returned.
• The Adventures of Tom Sawyer (1876) came first; Huckleberry Finn (1885) is its sequel, narrated by Huck himself.
• Ernest Hemingway said all modern American literature comes from this one book.
• He also wrote travel books, short stories, essays and poems.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. What did Huck mean when he said that Jim’s words “cut me like a knife”?
👀 Show model answer
Huck was paddling ashore to turn Jim in when Jim called him his friend and the only white man who had ever kept a promise to him. The words hurt Huck sharply, because he was about to break that very promise. The simile shows how deeply guilty and ashamed he suddenly felt.
2. Describe the character of Huck Finn on the basis of the story.
👀 Show model answer
Huck is clever and resourceful: he fakes his death, lights a decoy fire and disguises himself as a girl. He loves freedom and dislikes rules. He is also kind and loyal, keeping his oath to Jim. Most of all he is conflicted, torn between what society taught him and what his heart tells him — and in the end his heart wins.
