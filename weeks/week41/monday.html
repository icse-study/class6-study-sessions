🔁 Recap

Ten chapters, twenty texts: stories of family (Ramchand, the March sisters, Swami and Granny), adventure (the railway children, Huck and Jim, the pilot), nature (Gathering Leaves, Adam’s Peak, the mountain and the squirrel), imagination (The Sailor, Across the Border, What Is This Game?), humour (the dentist and the crocodile) and compassion (Paying the Price, The Happy Prince, Silent Night).

🧩 Links and quotes to remember

Settings: Amritsar · a fairyland voyage · the Sahara · Yorkshire · Adam’s Peak, Sri Lanka · the Mississippi · a 19th-century American town · Malgudi · an Indian town of long ago · Oberndorf, Austria · a city with a golden statue.
Forms: novel excerpts, a true travel account, a graphic novel, a play, narrative and lyric poems, free verse.
Big themes: love over possessions · respect for children and the old · conscience · courage · imagination · kindness to the poor.

✍️ Writing Practice — answer on paper first (not scored)
1. Which character from the whole book do you admire most, and why?
👀 Show model answer
Model approach: name the character and text; give two qualities with evidence (for example, the Happy Prince — compassion: he gives his sapphire eyes; sacrifice: he gives away all his gold); compare with one other character; end with what you learnt from them.
2. “Love matters more than possessions.” Show how three texts from the book support this idea.
👀 Show model answer
In “The Most Beautiful Thing”, Ramchand’s parents give him understanding, not a toy, and he loves them more. In “Little Women”, the March sisters give away their breakfast and become the merriest people in the city. In “The Happy Prince”, the statue gives away all his gold, and the Angel calls his heart one of the two most beautiful things on earth.
