🌍 Geography · Ch 6 North America · Part 5
Rivers of North America & Lumberjacks of Canada
Where do North America's rivers flow — and how do Canada's lumberjacks use forests and rivers to harvest timber? This week: river systems and the lumbering case study.

🌊 To the Gulf of Mexico

Mississippi — the most important river; drains much of the Great Plains and flows south into the Gulf. Tributaries: Missouri and Arkansas (from the Western Cordilleras), Ohio (drains the Appalachians).
Rio Grande — rises in the Colorado Plateau, flows south-east into the Gulf.

❄️ To Hudson Bay & the Arctic

Many short rivers drain the Canadian Shield into Hudson Bay. The Mackenzie is the most important north-flowing river (to the Arctic Ocean).

🌅 To the Pacific

Columbia — drains the Columbia Plateau.
Colorado — drains the Colorado Plateau; flows through the Grand Canyon, a deep steep-sided valley over 1,500 m deep.

🌄 To the Atlantic

St Lawrence — most important; drains the Great Lakes and gives waterway access to the interior. Niagara Falls lies between Lake Erie and Lake Ontario. Many short rivers drain the Appalachians to the Atlantic.

🌲 Lumbering in Canada

Lumbering: harvesting timber and cutting it into required shapes and sizes. Canada is the leading exporter of wood — about 31% of the world's forest products. Coniferous forests cover about 60% of the land, supplying paper, pulp, plywood, veneer.

🏚️ A lumberjack's life

Lumberjacks ("shanty boys") are mostly farmers who stop farming in winter (in British Columbia some work all year). They set out in autumn to live in shanties — temporary wooden houses with steep roofs. Permanent frost makes building hard, so in summer they build rough track roads to lumber camps (kitchen, dining room, bedroom). After work they swap stories and socialise. Once they walked home; today they travel by plane, snowmobile or bus and most live in settled communities.

👷 Four kinds of lumberjack

High riggers — the riskiest job: climb tall conifers (spruce, fir, pine, hemlock) in spiked shoes with a safety belt, cut branches and the top.
Sawyers/buckers — cut logs to size: 4.5 or 5 m for saw mills, 1 or 1.5 m for pulp mills; now with power chainsaws.
Skidders — drag logs to a road, railway or river bank; tractors pull chained bundles along skid trails.
Haulers — move logs down the stream in spring when ice thaws; walk on floating logs in spiked boots, separate jams with spiked poles; logs can roll over them. Dams now regulate the flow; on big rivers logs are chained into rafts.

💰 Modern lumbering

Machines and science made work easier. Successful ones became lumber barons — some of Canada's richest people. Canada's lumbering thrives because of: hydroelectric power, huge coniferous softwood resources, modern mechanised mills, excellent rail and water transport, and a small population. But areas like Nova Scotia are now devoid of trees.

🎯 Must-remember facts
• Gulf: Mississippi (+ Missouri, Arkansas, Ohio), Rio Grande
• Arctic: Mackenzie · Hudson Bay: short Shield rivers
• Pacific: Columbia, Colorado (Grand Canyon > 1,500 m)
• Atlantic: St Lawrence (Great Lakes) · Niagara between Erie & Ontario
• Canada: 31% world forest products · conifers 60% · high riggers riskiest

1–2 · Articles & Determiners

a/an by sound (an hour, a university) · the + unique things, rivers, oceans, ranges, instruments, superlatives · no article with meals, games, languages, single mountains · how much sugar, several friends, not every one is a friend

Christopher Columbus was a great sailor… He made a long voyage across the Atlantic Ocean with a small fleet.

3–4 · Adjectives & Comparison

Kinds: quality, quantity, number, demonstrative, distributive, interrogative, possessive · no double comparatives (more good ✗ → better; most eldest ✗ → eldest) · not as … as · fewer + countable · the + comparative for two

5 · Subject–Verb Agreement

Singular subject → singular verb · each/every/everyone → singular · along with / as well as → first subject · either/or, neither/nor → nearer subject · amounts, titles, subjects like physics → singular · The Abrahams live by the sea; Mr Abraham runs a food shack.

6–8 · Sentences, Questions, Tags

Four kinds: assertive (.), interrogative (?), imperative (. or !), exclamatory (!) · helper before subject; else do/does/did + base verb · positive → negative tag; I am → aren't I; Let's → shall we

9–10 · Prepositions & Adverbs

at/on/in for time · between two, among many · no preposition after reach, enter, discuss · five kinds of adverbs: manner, place, time, frequency, degree · order M → P → T · frequency adverb before main verb, after is

🧠 Exam strategy

• Read the whole sentence before choosing — the clue is often at the end.
• For agreement questions, cross out 'of …' phrases.
• For tags, find the helper verb first.
• If two options look right, check spelling and apostrophes.
